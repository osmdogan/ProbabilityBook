40.1 Kronecker’s Lemma

Kronecker’s Lemma serves as the primary analytical bridge translating the convergence of infinite weighted series (obtained via Kolmogorov’s Convergence Criterion) into almost sure Cesàro-type limits for partial sums. The central message of Kronecker’s Lemma is:

  • If the weighted terms \(x_k/b_k\) are summable, then the corresponding cumulative sum \(\sum_{k=1}^n x_k\) grows more slowly than \(b_n\).

Lemma 40.1 (Kronecker’s Lemma) Let \(\{x_n\}_{n=1}^\infty\) be a sequence in \(\mathbb{R}\) (or any normed vector space), and let \(\{b_n\}_{n=1}^\infty\) be a sequence of positive real numbers such that \(b_n \uparrow \infty\). If the infinite series \(\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{x_k}{b_k}\) converges in \(\mathbb{R}\), then: \[ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{b_n} \sum_{k=1}^n x_k = 0 \tag{40.1}\]

Proof (Proof of Lemma 40.1). Define \(y_k \triangleq \frac{x_k}{b_k}\) and \(S_n \triangleq \sum_{k=1}^n y_k\) for \(n \ge 1\) (with \(S_0 \triangleq 0\)). By hypothesis, \(S_n \to S \in \mathbb{R}\) as \(n \to \infty\). Express \(x_k = b_k (S_k - S_{k-1})\) and perform summation by parts: \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{k=1}^n x_k &= \sum_{k=1}^n b_k (S_k - S_{k-1}) \\ &= \sum_{k=1}^n b_kS_k - \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} b_{k+1} S_k\\ &= b_n S_n + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} (b_k - b_{k+1}) S_k \end{aligned} \tag{40.2}\]

Divide both sides by \(b_n\) and add/subtract \(\frac{S}{b_n} \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} (b_{k+1} - b_k) = S \left(1 - \frac{b_1}{b_n}\right)\): \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{1}{b_n} \sum_{k=1}^n x_k &= S_n - \frac{1}{b_n} \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} (b_{k+1} - b_k) S_k \\ &= S_n - \left(1 - \frac{b_1}{b_n}\right) S + R_n, \end{aligned} \] where the remainder term \(R_n\) is defined as: \[ R_n \triangleq \frac{1}{b_n} \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} (b_{k+1} - b_k) (S - S_k) \tag{40.3}\]

Since \(S_n \to S\) and \(b_n \to \infty\), \(S_n - \left(1 - \frac{b_1}{b_n}\right) S \to S - S = 0\). For \(R_n\), fix \(N \in \mathbb{N}\). For \(n > N\), split the sum at \(N\): \[ \begin{aligned} |R_n| &\le \frac{1}{b_n} \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} (b_{k+1} - b_k) |S - S_k| \\ &+ \left( \sup_{k \ge N} |S - S_k| \right) \frac{1}{b_n} \sum_{k=N}^{n-1} (b_{k+1} - b_k) \end{aligned} \tag{40.4}\] As \(n \to \infty\), the first term vanishes because the numerator sum is constant while \(b_n \to \infty\). The second term is bounded by \(\sup_{k \ge N} |S - S_k| \cdot \left(1 - \frac{b_N}{b_n}\right) \le \sup_{k \ge N} |S - S_k|\). Taking \(N \to \infty\) forces \(R_n \to 0\). Thus \(\frac{1}{b_n} \sum_{k=1}^n x_k \to 0\).

Example 40.1 (Harmonic Sequence) Consider the sequences \(x_n=\frac{1}{n}\) and \(b_n=n\). Then, \(b_n\uparrow\infty\), and \(\frac{x_k}{b_k}=\frac{1/k}{k}=\frac{1}{k^2}\). Therefore, \[ \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{x_k}{b_k}=\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{k^2}<\infty. \] Hence, by Kronecker’s Lemma, \(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n x_k\longrightarrow 0\). Indeed, in this case, \[ \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n x_k=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{1}{k} =\frac{H_n}{n}, \] where \(H_n\) denotes the \(n\)-th harmonic number. Since \(H_n\sim\log n\), we have \(\frac{H_n}{n} \sim \frac{\log n}{n} \longrightarrow 0\). Thus, Kronecker’s Lemma gives \[ \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{1}{k}\longrightarrow0. \]

Example 40.2 (An Alternating Sequence) Consider \(x_n=\frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}\) and \(b_n=n\). Then \(\frac{x_n}{b_n} = \frac{(-1)^n}{n^{3/2}}\). The corresponding series converges absolutely because \[ \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left| \frac{x_n}{b_n} \right| = \sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{n^{3/2}} <\infty. \] Therefore, Kronecker’s Lemma implies \(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{(-1)^k}{\sqrt{k}}\longrightarrow0\). Equivalently, \(\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{(-1)^k}{\sqrt{k}}=o(n)\). This example illustrates an important feature of Kronecker’s Lemma. The lemma does not say that the partial sums \(\sum_{k=1}^n x_k\) must converge. Rather, it says that their growth is negligible relative to \(b_n\): \(\sum_{k=1}^n x_k=o(b_n)\). Here, \(b_n=n\), so the conclusion is that the partial sums grow more slowly than linearly.

40.2 Complete Proof of Kolmogorov’s Strong Law of Large Numbers

Theorem 40.1 (Kolmogorov’s Strong Law of Large Numbers (SLLN)) Let \(\{X_n\}_{n=1}^\infty\) be an i.i.d. sequence of \(L^1\) random variables with \(E[X_1] = \alpha\). Let \(S_n \triangleq \sum_{k=1}^n X_k\). Then: \[ \frac{S_n}{n} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \alpha \quad \text{as } n \to \infty \tag{40.5}\]

Proof. By Corollary 38.1, \(\{X_n\}\) is tail equivalent to the truncated sequence \(X_n' \triangleq X_n \mathbf{1}_{\{|X_n| \le n\}}\). Hence by Proposition 38.1 (applied with \(b_n = n\) and target limit \(\alpha\)), it suffices to prove \(\frac{S_n'}{n} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \alpha\), where \(S_n' \triangleq \sum_{k=1}^n X_k'\).

  1. Variance Sum Bound: Set \(Y_n \triangleq \frac{X_n'}{n}\). Calculate the sum of variances: \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \operatorname{Var}\left( \frac{X_n'}{n} \right) &= \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} \operatorname{Var}(X_n') \\ &\le \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} \mathbb{E}\left[ |X_n'|^2 \right] \\ &= \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} \mathbb{E}\left[ |X_1|^2 \mathbf{1}_{\{|X_1| \le n\}} \right]. \end{aligned} \tag{40.6}\]

    Interchange expectation and summation via Fubini-Tonelli: \[ \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} \mathbb{E}\left[ |X_1|^2 \mathbf{1}_{\{|X_1| \le n\}} \right] = \mathbb{E}\left[ |X_1|^2 \sum_{n \ge |X_1|}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} \right] \tag{40.7}\]

    For \(x > 1\), estimate the tail sum \(\sum_{n \ge x}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2}\) using the integral test (with the shift \(t \mapsto t-1\) so the integral is finite): \[ \sum_{n \ge x}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} \le \min\left( \frac{\pi^2}{6}, \int_{x-1}^\infty \frac{1}{t^2} \, dt \right) = \min\left( \frac{\pi^2}{6}, \frac{1}{x-1} \right). \tag{40.8}\] We claim this is \(\le \min\left(4, \frac{4}{x}\right)\) for every \(x > 1\): if \(1 < x \le 2\) then \(\frac{4}{x} \ge 2 > \frac{\pi^2}{6}\), so \(\min\left(\frac{\pi^2}{6}, \frac{1}{x-1}\right) \le \frac{\pi^2}{6} < \frac{4}{x}\); if \(x > 2\) then \(\frac{1}{x-1} \le \frac{4}{x}\) (equivalent to \(x \ge \frac{4}{3}\), which holds), so \(\min\left(\frac{\pi^2}{6}, \frac{1}{x-1}\right) \le \frac{1}{x-1} \le \frac{4}{x}\). (The naive constant \(2\) in place of \(4\) fails here: at \(x=1.5\), \(\pi^2/6 \approx 1.645 > 2/x \approx 1.333\).) For \(0 \le x \le 1\), the sum is bounded by \(\pi^2/6 \le 4 \le \min(4,4/x)\) trivially. Hence \(\sum_{n \ge x}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} \le \min\left(4, \frac{4}{x}\right)\) for all \(x \ge 0\).

    Substituting this back with \(x = |X_1|\) yields: \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \operatorname{Var}\left( \frac{X_n'}{n} \right) &\le \mathbb{E}\left[ |X_1|^2 \cdot \min\left( 4, \frac{4}{|X_1|} \right) \right] \\ &= 4\, \mathbb{E}\left[ \min(|X_1|^2, |X_1|) \right] \le 4\, \mathbb{E}[|X_1|] < \infty, \end{aligned} \tag{40.9}\] using \(\min(|X_1|^2,|X_1|) \le |X_1|\) pointwise (true since \(\min(a^2,a) \le a\) for every \(a \ge 0\)).

  2. Application of Kolmogorov’s Convergence Criterion: Since \(\sum_{n=1}^\infty \operatorname{Var}\left( \frac{X_n'}{n} \right) < \infty\), Kolmogorov’s Convergence Criterion (Theorem 39.2) implies that: \[ \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{X_n' - E[X_n']}{n} \quad \text{converges almost surely.} \tag{40.10}\]

  3. Application of Kronecker’s Lemma: By Step 2, for almost every fixed \(\omega \in \Omega\) the real-number series \(\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{X_k'(\omega) - E[X_k']}{k}\) converges. Since Kronecker’s Lemma (Lemma 40.1) is a purely deterministic statement about real sequences, it applies pathwise to each such \(\omega\) with \(x_k = X_k'(\omega) - E[X_k']\) and \(b_k = k\), giving: \[ \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^n (X_k' - E[X_k']) = \frac{S_n'}{n} - \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^n E[X_k'] \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} 0 \tag{40.11}\]

  4. Cesàro Means of Expectations: By the Dominated Convergence Theorem (dominating function \(|X_1| \in L^1\), since \(|X_1 \mathbf{1}_{\{|X_1|\le k\}}| \le |X_1|\)), \(\mathbb{E}[X_k'] = \mathbb{E}[X_1 \mathbf{1}_{\{|X_1| \le k\}}] \xrightarrow{k \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_1] = \alpha\). The Cesàro mean of any convergent real sequence \(a_k \to \alpha\) converges to the same limit: given \(\epsilon>0\), choose \(N\) with \(|a_k - \alpha| < \epsilon\) for all \(k \ge N\); then \[ \left| \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n a_k - \alpha \right| \le \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1} |a_k - \alpha| + \frac{n-N+1}{n}\epsilon \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} \epsilon, \] and since \(\epsilon>0\) was arbitrary, the limit is \(\alpha\). Applying this with \(a_k = \mathbb{E}[X_k']\): \[ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^n \mathbb{E}[X_k'] = \alpha \tag{40.12}\] Combining Steps 3 and 4 establishes \(\frac{S_n'}{n} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \alpha\), which completes the proof.

40.3 Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund Extensions and Rates of Convergence

Theorem 40.2 (Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund Strong Law) Let \(\{X_n\}_{n=1}^\infty\) be i.i.d. random variables in \(L^p\) for \(p \in (1, 2)\) with \(E[X_1] = \alpha\). Then: \[ \frac{S_n - n\alpha}{n^{1/p}} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} 0, \tag{40.13}\] or equivalently, dividing both sides by \(n^{1-1/p}\): \[ \left| \frac{S_n}{n} - \alpha \right| = o\left( \frac{1}{n^{1 - 1/p}} \right) \quad \text{a.s.} \tag{40.14}\]

Proof (Proof of Theorem 40.2). The proof follows the \(L^1\) SLLN outline using cut-offs \(X_n' \triangleq X_n \mathbf{1}_{\{|X_n| \le n^{1/p}\}}\) and normalization \(b_n = n^{1/p}\). The tail integral bound evaluates as \(\sum_{n \ge x} n^{-2/p} \le \frac{p}{2-p} (x-1)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\).

Theorem 40.3 (\(L^2\)-SLLN under Independent Heterogeneous Variables) Let \(\{X_n\}_{n=1}^\infty\) be independent random variables with common mean \(E[X_n] = \alpha\) and uniformly bounded variance \(\operatorname{Var}(X_n) \le s^2 < \infty\). Let \(b_n > 0\) with \(b_n \uparrow \infty\) satisfying \(\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{b_n^2} < \infty\). Then: \[ \frac{S_n - n\alpha}{b_n} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s. and } L^2} 0 \tag{40.15}\]

Proof (Proof of Theorem 40.3).

  • Almost Sure Convergence: Since \(\operatorname{Var}(X_n/b_n) = \operatorname{Var}(X_n)/b_n^2 \le s^2/b_n^2\), we have \(\sum_{n=1}^\infty \operatorname{Var}\left( \frac{X_n}{b_n} \right) \le s^2 \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{b_n^2} < \infty\). By Kolmogorov’s Convergence Criterion (Theorem 39.2), \(\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{X_n - \alpha}{b_n}\) converges almost surely. Applying Kronecker’s Lemma (Lemma 40.1) pathwise with \(x_k = X_k - \alpha\) and weights \(b_k\) gives \(\frac{1}{b_n}\sum_{k=1}^n (X_k - \alpha) = \frac{S_n - n\alpha}{b_n} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} 0\).
  • \(L^2\) Convergence: \(\left\|\frac{S_n - n\alpha}{b_n}\right\|_{L^2}^2 = \frac{1}{b_n^2} \operatorname{Var}(S_n) = \frac{1}{b_n^2} \sum_{k=1}^n \operatorname{Var}(X_k) \le s^2 \frac{n}{b_n^2}\). To see \(\frac{n}{b_n^2} \to 0\), apply Kronecker’s Lemma (Lemma 40.1) to the constant sequence \(x_k = 1\) with weights \(b_k^2\) (not \(b_k\)): since \(b_k^2 \uparrow \infty\) and \(\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{x_k}{b_k^2} = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{b_k^2} < \infty\) by hypothesis, the lemma gives \(\frac{1}{b_n^2}\sum_{k=1}^n 1 = \frac{n}{b_n^2} \to 0\), hence \(\left\|\frac{S_n-n\alpha}{b_n}\right\|_{L^2}^2 \to 0\).

40.4 Comparison of Normalization Rates

The following table compares different normalization rates and the corresponding convergence guarantees for sums of independent random variables.

Rate Choice / Theorem Normalization \(b_n\) Convergence Guarantee
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund (\(p \in (1, 2)\)) \(b_n = n^{1/p}\) \(\frac{S_n - n\alpha}{n^{1/p}} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} 0\)
Polynomial \(L^2\)-SLLN (\(p > 0\)) \(b_n = n^{1/2 + p}\) \(\frac{S_n - n\alpha}{n^{1/2+p}} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s. and } L^2} 0\)
Log-power \(L^2\)-SLLN (\(\epsilon > 0\)) \(b_n = \sqrt{n} (\log n)^{1/2 + \epsilon}\) \(\frac{S_n - n\alpha}{\sqrt{n}(\log n)^{1/2+\epsilon}} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s. and } L^2} 0\)
Law of the Iterated Logarithm (Khinchin) \(b_n = \sqrt{2 s^2 n \log \log n}\) \(\limsup_{n \to \infty} \frac{S_n - n\alpha}{b_n} = 1\) a.s.

40.5 Takeaways

  • Kronecker’s Lemma is the analytic engine, not a probability result. It is a purely deterministic statement about real sequences: if a weighted series \(\sum x_k/b_k\) converges, the Cesàro-type average \(\frac{1}{b_n}\sum_{k=1}^n x_k\) vanishes. Its power in probability comes from applying it pathwise, i.e., separately to almost every \(\omega\) after a.s. convergence of a random series has been established some other way (typically via Kolmogorov’s Convergence Criterion).
  • The two-step recipe: variance-summability \(\to\) Kronecker. Every SLLN-type result in this lecture (thm-kolmogorov-slln-full, thm-l2-slln-general) follows the same pattern: (1) truncate/normalize so that \(\sum \operatorname{Var}(X_n/b_n) < \infty\), invoke Kolmogorov’s Convergence Criterion to get a.s. convergence of the centered weighted series, then (2) apply Kronecker’s Lemma to convert that series convergence into convergence of the Cesàro-type average \(S_n/b_n\).
  • Truncation tail bounds are delicate. Converting a second-moment tail sum like \(\sum_{n\ge x} n^{-2}\) into a bound linear in \(x\) requires care with the constants — a naive integral shift can produce a bound that fails on part of the range (as seen with the constant \(2\) vs. \(4\) in thm-kolmogorov-slln-full’s proof). Always verify such min-type bounds hold on the entire range, not just asymptotically.
  • Normalization rate trades off against moment assumptions. The comparison table makes explicit the tradeoff: the classical SLLN needs only \(L^1\) with \(b_n=n\); weaker moments (\(L^p\), \(p\in(1,2)\)) force a slower-growing normalization \(b_n = n^{1/p}\) (Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund); while retaining \(L^2\) but allowing heterogeneity lets \(b_n\) grow anywhere from just above \(\sqrt n\) (with a log-power correction) to \(n^{1/2+p}\), so long as \(\sum 1/b_n^2 < \infty\).
  • The Law of the Iterated Logarithm is the sharp boundary. Where the SLLN only tells us \(S_n/b_n \to 0\), the LIL identifies the exact almost-sure growth envelope \(b_n = \sqrt{2s^2 n \log\log n}\) at which \(\limsup (S_n-n\alpha)/b_n = 1\) — showing that no normalization growing strictly slower than \(\sqrt{n\log\log n}\) can force a.s. convergence to \(0\) for general finite-variance i.i.d. sequences.
  • Net effect: together with the truncation and tail-equivalence tools from 18-1-SLLN1.qmd and the Maximal Inequality from 18-2-Kolmogorov-Convergence-Criterion.qmd, this lecture completes the full proof of Kolmogorov’s Strong Law of Large Numbers and situates it within a broader family of normalization-rate results.
