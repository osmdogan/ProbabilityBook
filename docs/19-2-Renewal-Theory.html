41  Lecture 19.2: Renewal Theory

41.1 Frequentist Interpretation of Probability

The Strong Law of Large Numbers (SLLN) provides mathematical justification for the frequentist interpretation of probability. Given an event \(A \in \mathcal{F}\), let \(\{X_n\}_{n=1}^\infty\) be an i.i.d. sequence of indicator random variables \(X_n \triangleq \mathbf{1}_A^{(n)}\) representing independent trials. Since \(E[X_1] = E[\mathbf{1}_A] = P(A)\), SLLN establishes: \[ \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^n X_k \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} P(A) \quad \text{as } n \to \infty \tag{41.1}\]

41.2 Elementary Renewal Theorem

Consider a sequence \(\{X_n\}_{n=1}^\infty\) of non-negative, independent, identically distributed \(L^1\) random variables representing inter-arrival times or lifetime durations. Assume \(P(X_n > 0) > 0\), so \(\mathbb{E}[X_1] \in (0, \infty)\).

Define the \(n\)-th arrival time \(S_n \triangleq \sum_{k=1}^n X_k\) (with \(S_0 \triangleq 0\)). The renewal process \(\{N_t\}_{t \ge 0}\) counts the total number of renewals occurring in the time interval \([0, t]\): \[ N_t \triangleq \sup \{ n \in \mathbb{N}_0 \mid S_n \le t \} \tag{41.2}\]

Proposition 41.1 (First-Order Renewal Rate) Under the assumptions above, the renewal process satisfies: \[ \lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{N_t}{t} = \frac{1}{\mathbb{E}[X_1]} \quad \text{almost surely.} \tag{41.3}\]

Proof (Proof of Proposition 41.1).

  1. Finiteness and Growth of \(N_t\): Since \(X_1 \ge 0\) with \(P(X_1>0)>0\), \(\mathbb{E}[X_1] > 0\). By Kolmogorov’s SLLN, \(\frac{S_n}{n} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \mathbb{E}[X_1] > 0\), which forces \(S_n \to \infty\) almost surely (since \(S_n \sim n\,\mathbb{E}[X_1] \to \infty\)). Consequently, for a.e. \(\omega\): given any fixed \(t \ge 0\), since \(S_n(\omega) \to \infty\) there is a smallest \(n\) with \(S_n(\omega) > t\), so \(N_t(\omega) < \infty\); and given any fixed \(m\), since \(S_m(\omega) < \infty\) we have \(N_t(\omega) \ge m\) for all \(t \ge S_m(\omega)\), so \(N_t(\omega) \to \infty\) as \(t \to \infty\). Thus \(N_t < \infty\) a.s. for all \(t \ge 0\), and \(N_t \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \infty\) as \(t \to \infty\).

  2. Fundamental Renewal Inequality: By definition of \(N_t\), \(S_{N_t} \le t < S_{N_t + 1}\). For \(N_t > 0\), dividing by \(N_t\) and using \(\mathbb{E}\) notation yields: \[ \frac{S_{N_t}}{N_t} \le \frac{t}{N_t} < \frac{S_{N_t + 1}}{N_t} = \frac{S_{N_t + 1}}{N_t + 1} \cdot \left( 1 + \frac{1}{N_t} \right) \tag{41.4}\]

  3. Taking Limits via SLLN: By Kolmogorov’s SLLN, \(\frac{S_n}{n} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \mathbb{E}[X_1]\), i.e., for a.e. \(\omega\) the real sequence \(a_n(\omega) \triangleq S_n(\omega)/n\) converges to \(\mathbb{E}[X_1]\). Since \(N_t(\omega) \to \infty\) as \(t \to \infty\) for the same a.e. \(\omega\) (Step 1), the elementary composition-of-limits fact for real sequences (if \(a_n \to L\) and \(k_t \to \infty\) through positive integers as \(t \to \infty\), then \(a_{k_t} \to L\)) applies pathwise and yields: \[ \begin{aligned} &\lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{S_{N_t}}{N_t} = \mathbb{E}[X_1] \quad \text{a.s.} \quad \text{and}\\ &\lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{S_{N_t + 1}}{N_t + 1} \cdot \left( 1 + \frac{1}{N_t} \right) = \mathbb{E}[X_1] \cdot 1 = \mathbb{E}[X_1] \quad \text{a.s.}, \end{aligned} \tag{41.5}\] where the second line applies the composition fact to \(a_n = S_n/n\) at the index \(N_t+1 \to \infty\), together with \(1/N_t \to 0\). Applying the Squeeze Theorem to Equation 41.4 gives \(\lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{t}{N_t} = \mathbb{E}[X_1]\) a.s.; since \(\mathbb{E}[X_1] \in (0,\infty)\), inverting (by continuity of \(x \mapsto 1/x\) away from \(0\)) gives \(\lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{N_t}{t} = \frac{1}{\mathbb{E}[X_1]}\) a.s.

41.3 Alternating Renewal Processes: Lightbulb with Replacement Delays

Consider a system operating in alternating operational and delay phases:

  • Operational durations \(\{X_n\}_{n=1}^\infty \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} X \in L^1\), with \(X_n \ge 0\) and \(\mathbb{P}(X_n > 0) > 0\).
  • Replacement delays \(\{Y_n\}_{n=1}^\infty \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} Y \in L^1\), with \(Y_n \ge 0\) and \(\mathbb{P}(Y_n > 0) > 0\).

The \((n+1)\)-th replacement finishes at \(S_n \triangleq \sum_{k=1}^n (X_k + Y_k)\). The total operational time during \([0, t]\) is given by: \[ T_t \triangleq \sum_{n=1}^{N_t} X_n + \left( X_{N_t + 1} \wedge (t - S_{N_t}) \right) \tag{41.6}\]

Theorem 41.1 (Operational Asymptotic Fraction) The asymptotic fraction of operational time satisfies: \[ \lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{T_t}{t} = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X_1]}{\mathbb{E}[X_1] + \mathbb{E}[Y_1]} \quad \text{almost surely.} \tag{41.7}\]

Proof (Proof of Theorem 41.1). Define the simplified total operational sum \(\widetilde{T}_t \triangleq \sum_{n=1}^{N_t} X_n\). Since the cycle lengths \(\{X_n+Y_n\}_{n=1}^\infty\) are i.i.d., nonnegative, in \(L^1\), with \(P(X_n+Y_n>0)>0\) (because \(P(X_n>0)>0\)), and \(N_t\) is exactly the renewal counting process for this cycle-length sequence with arrival times \(S_n = \sum_{k=1}^n (X_k+Y_k)\), Proposition 41.1 applies directly: \[ \frac{N_t}{t} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \frac{1}{\mathbb{E}[X_1] + \mathbb{E}[Y_1]}, \] hence also \[ \frac{N_t+1}{t} = \frac{N_t}{t} + \frac1t \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \frac{1}{\mathbb{E}[X_1] + \mathbb{E}[Y_1]}. \tag{41.8}\] Both facts are used freely in Steps 2 and 3 below.

  1. Bounding Operational Time: Since \(0 \le X_{N_t + 1} \wedge (t - S_{N_t}) \le X_{N_t + 1}\): \[ \frac{\widetilde{T}_t}{t} \le \frac{T_t}{t} \le \frac{\widetilde{T}_t}{t} + \frac{X_{N_t + 1}}{t} \tag{41.9}\]

  2. Vanishing Tail Ratio: Rewrite \(\frac{X_{N_t + 1}}{t} = \frac{X_{N_t + 1}}{N_t + 1} \cdot \frac{N_t + 1}{t}\). By Lemma 41.1, \(\frac{X_n}{n} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} 0\) as a deterministic real sequence for a.e. \(\omega\); since \(N_t + 1 \to \infty\) a.s. as \(t \to \infty\) (from Equation 41.8), the pathwise composition-of-limits argument gives \(\frac{X_{N_t + 1}}{N_t + 1} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} 0\). Combined with \(\frac{N_t + 1}{t} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \frac{1}{E[X_1] + E[Y_1]}\) from Equation 41.8, the product \(\frac{X_{N_t+1}}{t}\) vanishes a.s. as \(t \to \infty\).

  3. Main Ratio Limit: Factor the main ratio \(\frac{\widetilde{T}_t}{t}\): \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\widetilde{T}_t}{t} = \frac{N_t}{t} \cdot \left( \frac{1}{N_t} \sum_{n=1}^{N_t} X_n \right) &\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \frac{1}{\mathbb{E}[X_1] + \mathbb{E}[Y_1]} \cdot \mathbb{E}[X_1]\\ & = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X_1]}{\mathbb{E}[X_1] + \mathbb{E}[Y_1]}. \end{aligned} \tag{41.10}\] The first factor’s limit is Equation 41.8. The second factor’s limit uses that, by Kolmogorov’s SLLN, \(\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n X_k \to \mathbb{E}[X_1]\) a.s. as a deterministic real sequence, composed pathwise at the random index \(N_t \to \infty\) a.s.; this composition is valid regardless of any dependence between \(N_t\) (which depends on both \(\{X_n\}\) and \(\{Y_n\}\)) and \(\{X_n\}\) itself, since for a.e. fixed \(\omega\) it is simply evaluating one convergent deterministic sequence along an index sequence that tends to infinity. By the Squeeze Theorem applied to Equation 41.9, \(\frac{T_t}{t} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \frac{\mathbb{E}[X_1]}{\mathbb{E}[X_1] + \mathbb{E}[Y_1]}\).

41.4 Supporting Lemma on \(L^1\) Sequences

Lemma 41.1 If \(X \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) and \(\{X_n\}_{n=1}^\infty\) are i.i.d. with \(X_n \sim X\), then: \[ \frac{X_n}{n} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty \tag{41.11}\]

Proof (Proof of Lemma 41.1). Fix \(\epsilon > 0\). By Lemma 38.1: \[ \sum_{n=1}^\infty P\left( \frac{|X_n|}{n} \ge \epsilon \right) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty P(|X| \ge n\epsilon) \le \frac{1}{\epsilon} \mathbb{E}[|X|] < \infty \tag{41.12}\] By the Borel-Cantelli Lemma I, \(P\left( \frac{|X_n|}{n} \ge \epsilon \text{ i.o.} \right) = 0\). Taking the countable intersection across \(\epsilon_k = \frac{1}{k}\) for \(k \in \mathbb{N}\) completes the proof.

41.5 Takeaways

  • SLLN is the frequentist interpretation’s mathematical backbone. Applying SLLN to indicator variables \(\mathbf{1}_A^{(n)}\) shows that the long-run relative frequency of an event converges almost surely to its probability — turning “probability as long-run frequency” from an informal heuristic into a theorem.
  • Renewal counts are the functional inverse of arrival times. \(N_t\) and \(S_n\) are related by the sandwich \(S_{N_t} \le t < S_{N_t+1}\), and this single inequality is the mechanical device that converts the SLLN statement about \(S_n/n\) (indexed by \(n\)) into a statement about \(N_t/t\) (indexed by \(t\)) — the Elementary Renewal Theorem is essentially SLLN viewed through the “inverse” of the arrival-time process.
  • Composition with a random time index is pathwise, not probabilistic. The recurring technique — “if \(a_n \to L\) a.s. and \(N_t \to \infty\) a.s., then \(a_{N_t} \to L\) a.s.” — is just ordinary real-sequence composition applied to almost every fixed \(\omega\) separately. It requires no independence between the index process \(N_t\) and the sequence \(a_n\), which is what lets it apply even when \(N_t\) is built from both \(\{X_n\}\) and \(\{Y_n\}\) in the alternating renewal setting.
  • Alternating renewal processes reduce to a ratio of means. The long-run operational fraction \(\mathbb{E}[X_1]/(\mathbb{E}[X_1]+\mathbb{E}[Y_1])\) falls out of two applications of the same machinery: the Elementary Renewal Theorem applied to the combined cycle length \(X_n+Y_n\) (for the rate \(N_t/t\)), and SLLN applied to \(\{X_n\}\) alone (for the average uptime per cycle) — the partial/incomplete final cycle contributes nothing in the limit, by Lemma 41.1.
  • The \(L^1\) tail lemma keeps reappearing. Lemma 41.1 is proved by the same tail-sum-plus-Borel-Cantelli pattern used throughout the SLLN lectures (18-1-SLLN1.qmd, 19-1-SLLN2.qmd): bound \(\sum_n P(|X_n|/n \ge \epsilon)\) using Lemma 38.1, then upgrade “eventually small” to “convergent to \(0\)” via a countable intersection over \(\epsilon_k = 1/k\).
  • Net effect: renewal theory is a direct application area for the Strong Law machinery built in Lectures 18–19, translating discrete-index a.s. convergence results into continuous-time (in \(t\)) a.s. convergence statements about counting and occupation-time processes.
