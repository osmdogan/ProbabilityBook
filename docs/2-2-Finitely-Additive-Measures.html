5  Lecture 2.2: Finitely Additive Measures

5.1 Introduction

Genuine measures (defined on full \(\sigma\)-algebras) are often difficult to construct directly, owing to the complex collection of sets in a \(\sigma\)-algebra. To approach this problem, we start with weaker notions of measure defined on simpler algebraic structures (algebras or semi-algebras) and later extend them to the full \(\sigma\)-algebra.

5.2 Premeasures and Finitely-Additive Measures

Definition 5.1 (Premeasurable Space and Premeasure) Let \(\Omega\) be a non-empty set.

  1. A pair \((\Omega, \mathcal{A})\) is called a premeasurable space if \(\mathcal{A}\) is a field (algebra) over \(\Omega\).
  2. A function \(\mu: \mathcal{A} \to [0, \infty]\) is called a premeasure if it is countably additive; that is, if \(\{E_i\}_{i=1}^\infty \subset \mathcal{A}\) is a sequence of pairwise disjoint sets such that \(\bigcup_{i=1}^\infty E_i \in \mathcal{A}\), then \[ \mu\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^\infty E_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^\infty \mu(E_i). \]
  3. If \(\mu: \mathcal{A} \to [0, \infty]\) is only finitely additive (i.e., \(\mu(A \cup B) = \mu(A) + \mu(B)\) for all disjoint \(A, B \in \mathcal{A}\)), then \(\mu\) is called a finitely-additive measure.

  • Note that algebras are closed under finite unions and intersections, but not necessarily under countable unions. Therefore, finitely-additive measures are weaker than premeasures, which require countable additivity.

  • That is also the reason why we assume that the union of the disjoint sets \(\{E_i\}_{i=1}^\infty\) belongs to \(\mathcal{A}\) in the definition of premeasure.

Proposition 5.1 (Subadditivity Inequality for Finitely-Additive Measures) Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{A}, \mu)\) be a finitely-additive measure space. If \(\{A_i\}_{i=1}^\infty \subset \mathcal{A}\) is a collection of pairwise disjoint sets such that \(A := \bigcup_{i=1}^\infty A_i \in \mathcal{A}\), then \[ \mu(A) \ge \sum_{i=1}^\infty \mu(A_i). \tag{5.1}\]

The inequality in Equation 5.1 can be strict.

Proof (Proof of Equation 5.1). Finitely-additive measures are monotone: if \(E, F \in \mathcal{A}\) with \(E \subseteq F\), then since \(F = E \cup (F \setminus E)\) with \(E \cap (F \setminus E) = \emptyset\) and \(F \setminus E \in \mathcal{A}\), finite additivity yields \[ \mu(F) = \mu(E) + \mu(F \setminus E) \ge \mu(E). \] Fix \(n \in \mathbb{N}\). Since \(\{A_i\}_{i=1}^\infty\) are pairwise disjoint and belong to the algebra \(\mathcal{A}\), the finite union \(\bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i\) belongs to \(\mathcal{A}\). Moreover, \(\bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i \subseteq A\). By monotonicity and finite additivity, \[ \mu(A) \ge \mu\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^n \mu(A_i). \] Taking the limit as \(n \to \infty\) on the right-hand side yields \[ \mu(A) \ge \sum_{i=1}^\infty \mu(A_i). \]

5.3 Generating Fields on \(\mathbb{R}\)

Among the natural generating sets for the Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra \(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\) is the collection of left-open, right-closed intervals: \[ \mathcal{S}_{(]} := \{(a, b]: -\infty \le a \le b \le \infty\}, \tag{5.2}\] where by convention \((a, b] = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : a < x \le b\}\), \((a, a] = \emptyset\), and \((a, \infty] = (a, \infty)\).

To study the field generated by these intervals, denoted \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\), we introduce the notion of a semi-algebra.

5.4 Semi-Algebras of Sets

Definition 5.2 (Semi-Algebra (Elementary Class)) A collection of subsets \(\mathcal{S} \subseteq 2^\Omega\) is called a semi-algebra (or an elementary class) over \(\Omega\) if:

  1. \(\emptyset \in \mathcal{S}\);
  2. \(\mathcal{S}\) is closed under finite intersections: if \(A, B \in \mathcal{S}\), then \(A \cap B \in \mathcal{S}\);
  3. If \(A \in \mathcal{S}\), then its complement \(A^c = \Omega \setminus A\) can be expressed as a finite disjoint union of elements from \(\mathcal{S}\): \[ A^c = \bigcup_{j=1}^k C_j, \quad \text{where } C_j \in \mathcal{S} \text{ are pairwise disjoint.} \]
  • The following proposition characterizes the field generated by a semi-algebra. This result is useful because it allows us to construct finitely-additive measures on the field generated by a semi-algebra, which can then be extended to a premeasure on the \(\sigma\)-algebra generated by the semi-algebra.
  • The field generated by a semi-algebra is the smallest field containing the semi-algebra, and it can be described explicitly as the collection of all finite disjoint unions of elements from the semi-algebra.

Proposition 5.2 (Structure of the Generated Field) If \(\mathcal{S}\) is a semi-algebra over \(\Omega\), then the field \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\) generated by \(\mathcal{S}\) is equal to the set of all finite disjoint unions of elements from \(\mathcal{S}\): \[ \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}) = \text{DU}(\mathcal{S}) := \left\{ \bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i : n \in \mathbb{N}, \, A_i \in \mathcal{S} \text{ pairwise disjoint} \right\}. \tag{5.3}\]

Proof (Proof of Proposition 5.2). Clearly, \(\mathcal{S} \subseteq \text{DU}(\mathcal{S}) \subseteq \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\). To prove equality, it suffices to show that \(\text{DU}(\mathcal{S})\) is a field.

  1. Empty Set and Universe: Since \(\emptyset \in \mathcal{S}\), \(\emptyset \in \text{DU}(\mathcal{S})\). By Property (3) of semi-algebras, \(\Omega = \emptyset^c\) is a finite disjoint union of elements in \(\mathcal{S}\), so \(\Omega \in \text{DU}(\mathcal{S})\).
  2. Closure under Finite Intersections: Let \(D = \bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i \in \text{DU}(\mathcal{S})\) and \(E = \bigcup_{j=1}^m B_j \in \text{DU}(\mathcal{S})\), where \(\{A_i\}\) and \(\{B_j\}\) are finite disjoint collections from \(\mathcal{S}\). Then \[ D \cap E = \bigcup_{i=1}^n \bigcup_{j=1}^m (A_i \cap B_j). \] Since \(\mathcal{S}\) is closed under intersection, \(A_i \cap B_j \in \mathcal{S}\). Furthermore, for \((i, j) \neq (i', j')\), the sets \((A_i \cap B_j)\) and \((A_{i'} \cap B_{j'})\) are disjoint: if \(i \neq i'\) then \((A_i \cap B_j) \cap (A_{i'} \cap B_{j'}) \subseteq A_i \cap A_{i'} = \emptyset\); if \(j \neq j'\) then \((A_i \cap B_j) \cap (A_{i'} \cap B_{j'}) \subseteq B_j \cap B_{j'} = \emptyset\). Thus, \(D \cap E\) is a finite disjoint union of sets from \(\mathcal{S}\), so \(D \cap E \in \text{DU}(\mathcal{S})\).
  3. Closure under Complement: Let \(D = \bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i \in \text{DU}(\mathcal{S})\) with \(A_i \in \mathcal{S}\) pairwise disjoint. Then \[ D^c = \bigcap_{i=1}^n A_i^c. \] For each \(i\), \(A_i^c \in \text{DU}(\mathcal{S})\) by definition of a semi-algebra. Since \(\text{DU}(\mathcal{S})\) is closed under finite intersections (as established above), it follows that \(D^c \in \text{DU}(\mathcal{S})\).

Thus, \(\text{DU}(\mathcal{S})\) is a field containing \(\mathcal{S}\), which implies \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}) = \text{DU}(\mathcal{S})\).

5.5 Finitely Additive Measures on Semi-Algebras

Once we have a finitely-additive measure defined on a semi-algebra, we can extend it to the field generated by that semi-algebra. This is a crucial step in constructing measures on more complex structures.

Proposition 5.3 (Extension to Generated Field) Let \(\mathcal{S}\) be a semi-algebra over \(\Omega\). Let \(\mu: \mathcal{S} \to [0, \infty]\) be finitely additive on \(\mathcal{S}\); that is, whenever \(A \in \mathcal{S}\) is the disjoint union of finitely many sets \(A = \bigsqcup_{i=1}^n A_i\) with \(A_i \in \mathcal{S}\), we have \(\mu(A) = \sum_{i=1}^n \mu(A_i)\).

Then \(\mu\) extends uniquely to a finitely-additive measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\), defined for \(A = \bigcup_{i=1}^n E_i \in \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\) (with \(\{E_i\}_{i=1}^n \subset \mathcal{S}\) pairwise disjoint) by: \[ \mu(A) := \sum_{i=1}^n \mu(E_i). \tag{5.4}\]

Proof (Proof of Proposition 5.3). If \(\mu\) is to be a finitely additive measure on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\), formula Equation 5.4 must hold, which guarantees uniqueness.

To show that \(\mu\) is well-defined, suppose \(A \in \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\) has two finite disjoint representations: \[ A = \bigcup_{i=1}^n E_i = \bigcup_{j=1}^m F_j, \quad \text{where } E_i, F_j \in \mathcal{S}. \] For each fixed \(i\), \(E_i = E_i \cap A = \bigcup_{j=1}^m (E_i \cap F_j)\) is a finite disjoint union of elements in \(\mathcal{S}\). By finite additivity on \(\mathcal{S}\), \[ \mu(E_i) = \sum_{j=1}^m \mu(E_i \cap F_j). \] Summing over \(i=1, \dots, n\) gives: \[ \sum_{i=1}^n \mu(E_i) = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^m \mu(E_i \cap F_j) = \sum_{j=1}^m \sum_{i=1}^n \mu(E_i \cap F_j) = \sum_{j=1}^m \mu(F_j). \] Thus, the value assigned to \(\mu(A)\) is independent of the choice of disjoint decomposition, so \(\mu\) is well-defined.

It remains to verify finite additivity on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\). Let \(A, B \in \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\) be disjoint, with representations \[ A = \bigcup_{i=1}^n E_i, \qquad B = \bigcup_{j=1}^m F_j, \] where \(\{E_i\}_{i=1}^n, \{F_j\}_{j=1}^m \subset \mathcal{S}\) are each pairwise disjoint. Since \(A \cap B = \emptyset\), the full collection \(\{E_1, \dots, E_n, F_1, \dots, F_m\}\) is pairwise disjoint. Therefore, \[ A \cup B = \bigcup_{i=1}^n E_i \;\cup\; \bigcup_{j=1}^m F_j \] is a finite disjoint union of elements of \(\mathcal{S}\), so \(A \cup B \in \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\). By definition Equation 5.4 applied to this representation, \[ \mu(A \cup B) = \sum_{i=1}^n \mu(E_i) + \sum_{j=1}^m \mu(F_j) = \mu(A) + \mu(B). \] By induction, \(\mu\) is finitely additive on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\).

5.6 Stieltjes (Pre)Measures on \(\mathcal{S}_{(]}\)

The Stieltjes measure is a classical example of a finitely-additive measure defined on the semi-algebra of left-open, right-closed intervals. It is constructed from a non-decreasing function \(F: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\), and it can be extended to a premeasure on the field generated by \(\mathcal{S}_{(]}\).

The Stieltjes measure is particularly important in probability theory, as it provides a way to define measures corresponding to cumulative distribution functions (CDFs) of random variables.

Definition 5.3 (Stieltjes Set Function) Let \(F: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) be a non-decreasing function. On the semi-algebra \(\mathcal{S}_{(]} = \{(a, b]: -\infty \le a \le b \le \infty\}\), define: \[ \mu_F((a, b]) := F(b) - F(a) \ge 0. \tag{5.5}\]

Proposition 5.4 (Additivity on Interval Semi-Algebra) The set function \(\mu_F\) is additive on \(\mathcal{S}_{(]}\).

Proof (Proof of Proposition 5.4). Suppose \((a, b] = \bigsqcup_{k=1}^n (c_k, d_k]\) is a finite disjoint covering of \((a,b]\) by elements of \(\mathcal{S}_{(]}\). We verify the hypothesis of Proposition 5.3 by showing \(\sum_{k=1}^n \mu_F((c_k, d_k]) = \mu_F((a,b])\).

The intervals are pairwise disjoint subsets of \((a, b]\), so \(c_k \ge a\) and \(d_k \le b\) for all \(k\). After sorting by left endpoint (reindexing so that \(c_1 \le c_2 \le \cdots \le c_n\)), the covering condition forces:

  • \(c_1 = a\): any point \(a + \varepsilon \in (a, b]\) must lie in some interval \((c_k, d_k]\), so \(c_k < a + \varepsilon\); letting \(\varepsilon \to 0\) gives \(c_k \le a\), and combined with \(c_k \ge a\) gives \(c_k = a\).
  • \(d_k = c_{k+1}\) for \(k = 1, \ldots, n-1\): if \(d_k < c_{k+1}\), the point \((d_k + c_{k+1})/2\) would belong to \((a,b]\) but to no interval.
  • \(d_n = b\): otherwise \(b\) itself is uncovered.

Hence \(a = c_1 < d_1 = c_2 < \cdots < d_{n-1} = c_n < d_n = b\), and the sum telescopes: \[ \sum_{k=1}^n \mu_F((c_k, d_k]) = \sum_{k=1}^n \bigl(F(d_k) - F(c_k)\bigr) = F(b) - F(a) = \mu_F((a, b]). \] By Proposition 5.3, \(\mu_F\) extends uniquely to a finitely-additive measure on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\).

5.7 Counterexample: When \(\mu_F\) Fails Countable Additivity

A fundamental question is: Is \(\mu_F\) always a premeasure (countably additive) on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\)?

Consider a non-decreasing function \(F\) that is not right-continuous at \(a \in \mathbb{R}\). Take the interval \((a, a+1] \in \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\). We can decompose this interval into a countable disjoint union: \[ (a, a+1] = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty \left(a + \frac{1}{n+1}, a + \frac{1}{n}\right]. \]

Evaluating \(\mu_F\) on the whole set gives: \[ \mu_F((a, a+1]) = F(a+1) - F(a). \]

On the other hand, summing the series of measures of the individual disjoint pieces: \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu_F\left(\left(a + \frac{1}{n+1}, a + \frac{1}{n}\right]\right) &= \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left( F\left(a + \frac{1}{n}\right) - F\left(a + \frac{1}{n+1}\right) \right) \\ &= \lim_{m \to \infty} \sum_{n=1}^m \left( F\left(a + \frac{1}{n}\right) - F\left(a + \frac{1}{n+1}\right) \right) \\ &= \lim_{m \to \infty} \left( F(a+1) - F\left(a + \frac{1}{m+1}\right) \right) \\ &= F(a+1) - F(a+), \end{aligned} \] where \(F(a+) := \lim_{\epsilon \downarrow 0} F(a + \epsilon)\).

If \(F\) is discontinuous from the right at \(a\), then \(F(a+) \neq F(a)\), which implies \[ \begin{align*} \mu_F((a, a+1]) &= F(a+1) - F(a) \neq F(a+1) - F(a+)\\ &= \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu_F\left(\left(a + \frac{1}{n+1}, a + \frac{1}{n}\right]\right). \end{align*} \]

Thus, \(\mu_F\) is not countably additive (and hence not a premeasure) if \(F\) is not right-continuous.

5.8 Takeaways

  • Premeasures vs finitely-additive measures. A premeasure on an algebra is countably additive (whenever the countable disjoint union lies in the algebra); a finitely-additive measure is only finitely additive. The two notions coincide for finite unions but diverge in general.

  • Subadditivity for finitely-additive measures (Proposition 5.1). If \(\{A_i\}_{i=1}^\infty\) are pairwise disjoint in \(\mathcal{A}\) with \(\bigcup_i A_i \in \mathcal{A}\), then \(\mu\!\left(\bigcup_i A_i\right) \ge \sum_i \mu(A_i)\), and the inequality can be strict. A premeasure would give equality; the gap measures the failure of countable additivity.

  • Structure of the generated field (Proposition 5.2). The field generated by a semi-algebra \(\mathcal{S}\) is exactly \(\operatorname{DU}(\mathcal{S})\), the collection of all finite disjoint unions of elements of \(\mathcal{S}\). This explicit description is the key to extending measures from \(\mathcal{S}\).

  • Unique extension from semi-algebras (Proposition 5.3). Any finitely-additive measure \(\mu\) on a semi-algebra \(\mathcal{S}\) extends uniquely to a finitely-additive measure on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\) via \(\mu\!\left(\bigsqcup_i E_i\right) := \sum_i \mu(E_i)\). Uniqueness is forced by the formula; well-definedness requires showing the value is independent of the chosen \(\mathcal{S}\)-decomposition, which follows from the common-refinement identity \(\mu(E_i) = \sum_j \mu(E_i \cap F_j)\).

  • Stieltjes measure (Proposition 5.4). Every non-decreasing \(F: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) defines a finitely-additive Stieltjes measure \(\mu_F((a,b]) = F(b) - F(a)\) on \(\mathcal{S}_{(]}\), which extends uniquely to \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\). Additivity on \(\mathcal{S}_{(]}\) follows from the telescoping identity for ordered partitions of an interval.

  • Right-continuity is necessary for countable additivity. \(\mu_F\) is a premeasure only if \(F\) is right-continuous: a right-discontinuity at \(a\) causes \(\mu_F((a,a+1]) > \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu_F\!\left(\!\left(a+\tfrac{1}{n+1}, a+\tfrac{1}{n}\right]\right)\). Right-continuity is also sufficient, as shown in Lecture 3.1 via the Carathéodory extension theorem.
