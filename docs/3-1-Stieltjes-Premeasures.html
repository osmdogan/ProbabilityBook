6  Lecture 3.1: Stieltjes Premeasures

6.1 Characterization of Stieltjes Premeasures

Let \(\mathcal{S}_{(]} = \{(a, b]: -\infty \le a \le b \le \infty\}\) be the canonical semi-algebra of left-open, right-closed intervals in \(\mathbb{R}\), and let \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]}) = \mathcal{B}_{\text{el}}(\mathbb{R})\) denote the generated elementary field of finite disjoint unions of such intervals.

For a non-decreasing function \(F: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\), the set function \(\mu_F((a, b]) = F(b) - F(a)\) defines a well-defined finitely-additive measure \(\mu_F\) on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\).

As established previously, if \(F\) fails to be right-continuous at any point, \(\mu_F\) fails to be countably additive. The converse also holds, yielding a full characterization.

Theorem 6.1 (Stieltjes Premeasure Theorem) The finitely-additive measure \(\mu_F\) is a premeasure (i.e., countably additive) on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\) if and only if \(F\) is right-continuous on \(\mathbb{R}\): \[ \lim_{\delta \downarrow 0} F(x + \delta) = F(x) \quad \text{for all } x \in \mathbb{R}. \tag{6.1}\]

To prove this theorem, we first establish a general criterion for countable additivity of a finitely-additive measure on a field generated by a semi-algebra.

6.2 Countable Subadditivity Criterion on Semi-Algebras

Recall that a finitely-additive measure \(\mu\) on a field \(\mathcal{A}\) is countably superadditive; that is, for any countable collection of disjoint sets \(\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty \subset \mathcal{A}\) with \(A = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n \in \mathcal{A}\), we have: \[ \mu(A) \ge \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu(A_n). \] The following proposition shows that, for a finitely additive set function defined on the field generated by a semi-algebra, it is enough to verify countable subadditivity on the semi-algebra to obtain countable additivity on the entire field.

Proposition 6.1 (Countable Subadditivity Criterion) Let \(\mathcal{S} \subseteq 2^\Omega\) be a semi-algebra over \(\Omega\), and let \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\) be the generated field. A finitely-additive measure \(\mu: \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}) \to [0, \infty]\) is a premeasure (countably additive) if and only if \(\mu\) is countably subadditive on \(\mathcal{S}\); that is, whenever \(E = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty E_n\) with \(E, E_n \in \mathcal{S}\), we have: \[ \mu(E) \le \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu(E_n). \tag{6.2}\]

Proof (Proof of Proposition 6.1). \((\Rightarrow)\) If \(\mu\) is a premeasure on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\), it is countably additive on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\), which directly implies countable subadditivity on \(\mathcal{S}\).

\((\Leftarrow)\) Finitely-additive measures are always countably superadditive on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\). Thus, to establish countable additivity, it suffices to prove that \(\mu\) is countably subadditive on the entire field \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\).

Let \(A = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\) where \(A, A_n \in \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\). By Proposition 5.2, any set in \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\) is a finite disjoint union of elements from \(\mathcal{S}\). Thus, we can write: \[ A = \bigcup_{j=1}^N E_j \quad \text{and} \quad A_n = \bigcup_{i=1}^{N_n} E_i^n, \] where \(\{E_j\}_{j=1}^N \subset \mathcal{S}\) are pairwise disjoint, and for each \(n \in \mathbb{N}\), \(\{E_i^n\}_{i=1}^{N_n} \subset \mathcal{S}\) are pairwise disjoint.

For each fixed \(j \in \{1, \dots, N\}\), since \(E_j \subseteq A = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\), we have: \[ E_j = E_j \cap A = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty (A_n \cap E_j) = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty \bigcup_{i=1}^{N_n} (E_i^n \cap E_j). \]

Since \(\mathcal{S}\) is a semi-algebra, \(E_i^n \cap E_j \in \mathcal{S}\). Applying countable subadditivity on \(\mathcal{S}\) yields: \[ \mu(E_j) \le \sum_{n=1}^\infty \sum_{i=1}^{N_n} \mu(E_i^n \cap E_j). \]

Summing over \(j = 1, \dots, N\) and using finite additivity on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\) gives: \[ \begin{aligned} \mu(A) = \sum_{j=1}^N \mu(E_j) &\le \sum_{j=1}^N \sum_{n=1}^\infty \sum_{i=1}^{N_n} \mu(E_i^n \cap E_j) \\ &= \sum_{n=1}^\infty \sum_{i=1}^{N_n} \sum_{j=1}^N \mu(E_i^n \cap E_j) \\ &= \sum_{n=1}^\infty \sum_{i=1}^{N_n} \mu\left( E_i^n \cap \bigcup_{j=1}^N E_j \right) \\ &= \sum_{n=1}^\infty \sum_{i=1}^{N_n} \mu(E_i^n \cap A) \\ &\le \sum_{n=1}^\infty \sum_{i=1}^{N_n} \mu(E_i^n) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu(A_n). \end{aligned} \]

Thus, \(\mu(A) \le \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu(A_n)\), proving that \(\mu\) is countably subadditive and hence a premeasure on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})\).

6.3 Proof of the Stieltjes Premeasure Theorem

We now prove Theorem 6.1 by verifying countable subadditivity of \(\mu_F\) on the semi-algebra \(\mathcal{S}_{(]}\) using a Heine-Borel compactness argument.

Proof (Proof of Theorem 6.1). Assume \(F: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) is right-continuous. By Proposition 6.1, it suffices to prove that \(\mu_F\) is countably subadditive on \(\mathcal{S}_{(]}\).

Suppose \((a, b] \subseteq \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty (a_j, b_j]\) with \((a, b], (a_j, b_j] \in \mathcal{S}_{(]}\). If \(a = b\) or \(a > b\), \((a, b] = \emptyset\) and subadditivity holds trivially. Assume \(-\infty < a < b < \infty\).

Fix \(\epsilon > 0\) and \(\delta > 0\) such that \(a + \delta < b\).

  1. Open Cover Construction: The compact interval \([a + \delta, b]\) is contained in \((a, b]\): \[ [a + \delta, b] \subset (a, b] \subseteq \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty (a_j, b_j]. \] By right-continuity of \(F\), for each \(j \ge 1\), we can choose \(\delta_j > 0\) such that: \[ F(b_j + \delta_j) - F(b_j) < \frac{\epsilon}{2^j}. \tag{6.3}\] Then \([a + \delta, b] \subset \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty (a_j, b_j + \delta_j)\).

  2. Compactness Argument: Since \([a + \delta, b]\) is compact and \(\{(a_j, b_j + \delta_j)\}_{j=1}^\infty\) is an open cover of \([a + \delta, b]\), there exists a finite subcover \(N \in \mathbb{N}\) such that: \[ [a + \delta, b] \subset \bigcup_{j=1}^N (a_j, b_j + \delta_j). \] This implies the interval cover \((a + \delta, b] \subseteq \bigcup_{j=1}^N (a_j, b_j + \delta_j]\).

  3. Finite Subadditivity and Limit Bounds: By finite subadditivity of \(\mu_F\) on interval unions, we have: \[ \mu_F((a + \delta, b]) \le \sum_{j=1}^N \mu_F((a_j, b_j + \delta_j]). \] Using additivity \(\mu_F((a_j, b_j + \delta_j]) = \mu_F((a_j, b_j]) + \mu_F((b_j, b_j + \delta_j])\), we obtain: \[ \begin{aligned} \mu_F((a + \delta, b]) &\le \sum_{j=1}^N \left( \mu_F((a_j, b_j]) + \mu_F((b_j, b_j + \delta_j]) \right) \\ &\le \sum_{j=1}^\infty \mu_F((a_j, b_j]) + \sum_{j=1}^\infty \mu_F((b_j, b_j + \delta_j]). \end{aligned} \] Applying bound Equation 6.3: \[ \mu_F((a + \delta, b]) \le \sum_{j=1}^\infty \mu_F((a_j, b_j]) + \sum_{j=1}^\infty \frac{\epsilon}{2^j} = \sum_{j=1}^\infty \mu_F((a_j, b_j]) + \epsilon. \]

    Evaluating the left-hand side: \(\mu_F((a + \delta, b]) = F(b) - F(a + \delta)\). Taking \(\delta \downarrow 0\), right-continuity of \(F\) at \(a\) implies \(F(a + \delta) \to F(a)\), yielding: \[ \mu_F((a, b]) = F(b) - F(a) \le \sum_{j=1}^\infty \mu_F((a_j, b_j]) + \epsilon. \] Since \(\epsilon > 0\) was arbitrary, letting \(\epsilon \to 0\) gives: \[ \mu_F((a, b]) \le \sum_{j=1}^\infty \mu_F((a_j, b_j]). \]

Extending to unbounded intervals (\(a = -\infty\) or \(b = \infty\)) follows by taking limits. Thus \(\mu_F\) is a premeasure on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\). \(\blacksquare\)

6.4 Lebesgue Premeasure and Translation Invariance

Definition 6.1 (Lebesgue Premeasure) When \(F(x) = x\), the associated Stieltjes premeasure \(\mu: \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]}) \to [0, \infty]\) is called the Lebesgue premeasure, satisfying: \[ \mu((a, b]) = b - a. \tag{6.4}\]

Proposition 6.2 (Translation Invariance of Lebesgue Premeasure) The Lebesgue premeasure \(\mu\) is translation-invariant on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\). Specifically, for any \(E \in \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\) and \(\alpha \in \mathbb{R}\), if \(E + \alpha := \{x + \alpha : x \in E\}\), then \(E + \alpha \in \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\) and: \[ \mu(E + \alpha) = \mu(E). \tag{6.5}\]

Proof (Proof of Proposition 6.2). Any set \(E \in \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\) can be expressed as a finite disjoint union \(E = \bigcup_{j=1}^n (a_j, b_j]\). Then \(E + \alpha = \bigcup_{j=1}^n (a_j + \alpha, b_j + \alpha]\), which is also a finite disjoint union of elements in \(\mathcal{S}_{(]}\).

By definition and finite additivity: \[ \begin{align*} \mu(E + \alpha) &= \sum_{j=1}^n \mu((a_j + \alpha, b_j + \alpha])\\ & = \sum_{j=1}^n ((b_j + \alpha) - (a_j + \alpha)) \\ &= \sum_{j=1}^n (b_j - a_j) = \sum_{j=1}^n \mu((a_j, b_j]) \\ &= \mu(E). \end{align*} \]

6.5 Takeaways

  1. The Stieltjes premeasure \(\mu_F\) is countably additive on the field \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\) if and only if \(F\) is right-continuous.
  2. The Lebesgue premeasure \(\mu\) is translation-invariant on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\).
  3. The Lebesgue premeasure \(\mu\) is the unique translation-invariant premeasure on \(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{(]})\) satisfying \(\mu((0, 1]) = 1\).
  4. The Lebesgue premeasure \(\mu\) can be extended to a complete measure on the Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra \(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\), known as the Lebesgue measure.
  5. The Lebesgue measure is the unique translation-invariant measure on \(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\) satisfying \(\mu((0, 1]) = 1\).
