66  Lecture 30.2: Conditional Expectation-Part I

66.1 Conditioning on a Single Event

Given a probability space \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})\) and an event \(A \in \mathcal{F}\) with \(\mathbb{P}(A) > 0\), recall the conditional probability measure on \((\Omega,\mathcal F)\): \[ \mathbb{P}_A(B) = \mathbb{P}[B \mid A] = \frac{\mathbb{P}(A \cap B)}{\mathbb{P}(A)}, \qquad B\in\mathcal F. \]

We can evaluate the expected value of an integrable random variable \(X \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}_A)\) under this updated measure:

\[ \mathbb{E}_A[X] = \mathbb{E}[X \mid A] := \int_\Omega X \, d\mathbb{P}_A. \]

Lemma 66.1 (Expectation under Restricted Conditioning) For \(X \in L^1(\mathbb{P})\), we have \(X \in L^1(\mathbb{P}_A)\), and: \[ \mathbb{E}_A[X] = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X:A]}{\mathbb{P}(A)} = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X \mathbb{I}_A]}{\mathbb{P}(A)}, \] where \(\mathbb{E}[X:A] := \mathbb{E}[X \mathbb{I}_A]\) is the expectation of \(X\) restricted to the event \(A\).

Proof (Proof of Lemma 66.1).

  • Step 1 (Simple Functions): Let \(X = \sum_{j=1}^m \alpha_j \mathbb{I}_{B_j}\) be a simple random variable, where \(B_j \in \mathcal{F}\) and \(m<\infty\). Since \(\mathbb{I}_{B_j \cap A} = \mathbb{I}_{B_j} \cdot \mathbb{I}_A\), we get \(\sum_{j=1}^m \alpha_j \mathbb{I}_{B_j \cap A} = X \mathbb{I}_A\) pointwise. Integrating with respect to \(\mathbb{P}_A\) yields:

    \[ \begin{align*} \int_\Omega X \, d\mathbb{P}_A &= \sum_{j=1}^m \alpha_j \mathbb{P}_A(B_j) = \sum_{j=1}^m \alpha_j \frac{\mathbb{P}(A \cap B_j)}{\mathbb{P}(A)} \\ &= \frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(A)} \int_\Omega \underbrace{\left( \sum_{j=1}^m \alpha_j \mathbb{I}_{B_j \cap A} \right)}_{=\, X\mathbb{I}_A} d\mathbb{P} = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X \mathbb{I}_A]}{\mathbb{P}(A)}. \end{align*} \]

  • Step 2 (General Case): Decompose \(X = X^+ - X^-\) and choose non-negative simple approximations \(0 \le X_n^{\pm} \uparrow X^{\pm}\). Then \(X_n^\pm \mathbb{I}_A \uparrow X^\pm \mathbb{I}_A\) are non-negative and increasing, so the Monotone Convergence Theorem (MCT) applies to each part separately:

    \[ \int_\Omega X^{\pm} \, d\mathbb{P}_A = \lim_{n \to \infty} \int_\Omega X_n^{\pm} \, d\mathbb{P}_A = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\mathbb{E}[X_n^{\pm} \mathbb{I}_A]}{\mathbb{P}(A)} = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X^{\pm} \mathbb{I}_A]}{\mathbb{P}(A)}. \]

    Since \(\mathbb{E}[X^\pm \mathbb{I}_A] \le \mathbb{E}[X^\pm] < \infty\), both integrals are finite. Thus \(X\in L^1(\mathbb P_A)\), and the difference is well-defined:

    \[ \int_\Omega X \, d\mathbb{P}_A = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X^+ \mathbb{I}_A]}{\mathbb{P}(A)} - \frac{\mathbb{E}[X^- \mathbb{I}_A]}{\mathbb{P}(A)} = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X \mathbb{I}_A]}{\mathbb{P}(A)}. \]

66.2 Conditioning on Countable Partitions

Let \(\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty \subset \mathcal F\) be a countable partition of \(\Omega\); that is, the events are pairwise disjoint, \(A_n\cap A_m=\varnothing\) for \(n\neq m\) and \(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n=\Omega\), and suppose that \(\mathbb P(A_n)>0\) for every \(n\). Let \(X\in L^1(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)\). We combine the individual conditional expectations \(\mathbb E_{A_n}[X]=\mathbb E[X\mid A_n]\) into a single random variable: \[ \mathbb E_{\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty}[X](\omega) := \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mathbb E_{A_n}[X]\mathbb I_{A_n}(\omega) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\mathbb E[X\mathbb I_{A_n}]}{\mathbb P(A_n)} \mathbb I_{A_n}(\omega). \] Because the sets \(A_n\) are pairwise disjoint and form a partition, exactly one indicator \(\mathbb I_{A_n}(\omega)\) equals \(1\) for each \(\omega\in\Omega\). Thus, if \(\omega\in A_n\), \[ \mathbb E_{\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty}[X](\omega) = \frac{\mathbb E[X\mathbb I_{A_n}]}{\mathbb P(A_n)} = \mathbb E[X\mid A_n]. \] In other words, the conditional expectation is constant on each partition element: it assigns to every \(\omega\in A_n\) the average value of \(X\) under the probability measure conditional on \(A_n\).

66.3 \(\sigma\)-Algebras Generated by Partitions

Instead of indexing by the explicit set sequence \(\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty\), it is more natural to label the operator using the sub-\(\sigma\)-algebra generated by the partition:

\[ \mathcal{G} = \sigma(\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty). \]

We write the preceding random variable as \[ \mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]:=\mathbb E_{\mathcal G}[X] :=\mathbb{E}_{\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty}[X]. \] Using the fact that \(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]\) is constant on each \(A_n\), it can be shown that \(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]\) is integrable because the triangle inequality and the single-event formula give \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E\left[\left|\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]\right|\right] &=\sum_{n=1}^\infty \left|\mathbb E_{A_n}[X]\right|\mathbb P(A_n) \\ &\le \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mathbb E_{A_n}[|X|]\mathbb P(A_n)\\ &=\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\mathbb E[|X|\mathbb I_{A_n}]}{\mathbb P(A_n)}\mathbb P(A_n)\\ &=\sum_{n=1}^\infty \mathbb E[|X|\mathbb I_{A_n}]\\ &=\mathbb E[|X|]. \end{aligned} \]

Exercise 66.1 (Structure of Partition \(\sigma\)-Algebras) If \(\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty\) forms a partition of \(\Omega\) into nonempty sets, then:

\[ \sigma(\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty) = \left\{ \bigcup_{n \in \Lambda} A_n : \Lambda \subseteq \mathbb{N} \right\}. \]

Thus, each \(A_n\) is an atom of \(\mathcal G\), and every element of \(\mathcal G\) is a union of these atoms.

Corollary 66.1 (Measurability of Conditional Expectation) If \(\mathcal{G} = \sigma(\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty)\), then \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\) is \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable and, for every \(B\in\mathcal G\), \[ \mathbb E\left[\mathbb E_{\mathcal G}[X]\mathbb I_B\right] =\mathbb E[X\mathbb I_B]. \]

Proof (Proof of Corollary 66.1). For \(m\ge1\), the partial sum \[ S_m=\sum_{n=1}^m \mathbb{E}_{A_n}[X]\mathbb I_{A_n} \] is \(\mathcal G\)-measurable. Since every \(\omega\in\Omega\) belongs to exactly one \(A_n\), \(S_m(\omega)\) converges pointwise to \(\mathbb E_{\mathcal G}[X](\omega)\). Therefore, \(\mathbb E_{\mathcal G}[X]\) is \(\mathcal G\)-measurable.

For \(B\in\mathcal G\), the partition description above gives \(B=\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda}A_n\) for some \(\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb N\). The series below are absolutely convergent because \[ \sum_{n=1}^\infty\left|\mathbb E[X\mathbb I_{A_n}]\right| \le\sum_{n=1}^\infty\mathbb E[|X|\mathbb I_{A_n}] =\mathbb E[|X|]<\infty. \] Thus, \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E\left[\mathbb E_{\mathcal G}[X]\mathbb I_B\right] &=\sum_{n\in\Lambda}\mathbb E_{A_n}[X]\mathbb P(A_n) \\ &=\sum_{n\in\Lambda}\mathbb E[X\mathbb I_{A_n}] \\ &=\mathbb E\left[X\mathbb I_{\bigcup_{n\in\Lambda}A_n}\right]\\ &=\mathbb E[X\mathbb I_B]. \end{aligned} \] Together with the preceding integrability estimate, this verifies that the displayed random variable is a version of \(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]\).

66.4 Conditioning on Discrete Random Variables

Let \(S\) be a countable set equipped with the discrete \(\sigma\)-algebra \(2^S\), and let \(Y:(\Omega,\mathcal F)\to(S,2^S)\) be measurable with \(\mathbb{P}(Y=s)>0\) for all \(s\in S\). The pre-images \(\{Y^{-1}(\{s\})\}_{s \in S}\) form a countable partition of \(\Omega\) generating \(\mathcal{G} = \sigma(Y)\).

For \(X \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})\), the conditional expectation \(\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y]\) is defined as:

\[ \mathbb{E}[X \mid Y](\omega) = \sum_{s \in S} \mathbb{E}[X \mid Y = s] \mathbb{I}_{\{Y = s\}}(\omega) = \sum_{s \in S} \frac{\mathbb{E}[X \mathbb{I}_{\{Y = s\}}]}{\mathbb{P}(Y = s)} \mathbb{I}_{\{Y = s\}}(\omega). \]

Define \(g:S\to\mathbb R\) by \(g(s)=\mathbb E[X\mid Y=s]\). Since \(S\) has the discrete \(\sigma\)-algebra, \(g\) is measurable, and:

\[ \mathbb{E}[X \mid Y](\omega) = g(Y(\omega)), \quad \text{where } g(s) = \mathbb{E}[X \mid Y = s] = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X \mathbb{I}_{\{Y = s\}}]}{\mathbb{P}(Y = s)}. \]

66.5 Smoothing and Orthogonality Properties

The fundamental characterization of conditional expectation is given by its action under integration against \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable functions.

Theorem 66.1 (The Smoothing / Projection Property) Let \(\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty\) be a partition of \(\Omega\) with \(\mathbb{P}(A_n) > 0\), and let \(\mathcal{G} = \sigma(\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty)\). If \(X \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})\) and \(Y \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, \mathbb{P})\), then:

\[ \mathbb{E}[X Y] = \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] Y]. \] Moreover, \(\mathbb E_{\mathcal G}[X]\in L^2(\Omega,\mathcal G,\mathbb P)\) and \[ \left\|\mathbb E_{\mathcal G}[X]\right\|_2\le\|X\|_2. \]

Proof (Proof of Theorem 66.1).

  • Step 1 (Finitely supported atomwise \(Y\)): Consider \(Y = \sum_{k=1}^N \beta_k \mathbb{I}_{A_k}\) for some \(N < \infty\). Because the partition atoms are disjoint (\(\mathbb{I}_{A_n}\mathbb{I}_{A_k} = \delta_{nk}\mathbb{I}_{A_k}\)), the product reduces pointwise to a finite sum:

    \[ \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] \cdot Y = \left(\sum_{n=1}^\infty \mathbb{E}_{A_n}[X]\,\mathbb{I}_{A_n}\right)\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^N \beta_k\,\mathbb{I}_{A_k}\right) = \sum_{k=1}^N \beta_k\,\mathbb{E}_{A_k}[X]\,\mathbb{I}_{A_k}. \]

    The infinite sum in \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\) collapses to \(N\) surviving terms: for each fixed \(\omega \in A_j\), only the \(n = j\) term in \(\sum_n \mathbb{E}_{A_n}[X]\mathbb{I}_{A_n}\) is nonzero, and the factor \(\mathbb{I}_{A_j}(\omega)\) from \(Y\) is also 1 only if \(j \le N\). Taking expectations — now a finite sum so linearity needs no justification — and substituting \(\mathbb{E}_{A_k}[X]\,\mathbb{P}(A_k) = \mathbb{E}[X\mathbb{I}_{A_k}]\) (from Lemma 66.1) gives:

    \[ \begin{align*} \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\,Y] &= \sum_{k=1}^N \beta_k\,\mathbb{E}_{A_k}[X]\,\mathbb{P}(A_k)\\ &= \sum_{k=1}^N \beta_k\,\mathbb{E}[X\mathbb{I}_{A_k}]\\ &= \mathbb{E}\!\left[X\sum_{k=1}^N \beta_k\,\mathbb{I}_{A_k}\right]\\ &= \mathbb{E}[XY]. \end{align*} \]

  • Step 2 (General \(Y \in L^2(\mathcal{G})\)): Since \(Y\) is \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable, it takes a constant value \(\beta_k\) on each \(A_k\). Define the finite truncations \[Y_m = \sum_{k=1}^m \beta_k\,\mathbb{I}_{A_k}.\] Each \(Y_m\) is a finite simple \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable function, so Step 1 gives \(\mathbb{E}[XY_m] = \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\,Y_m]\). Since every \(\omega\) belongs to exactly one \(A_k\), we have \(Y_m(\omega) = Y(\omega)\) for all \(m \ge k\), hence \(Y_m \to Y\) pointwise and \(|Y_m| \le |Y|\). DCT applies to both limits:

    • For \(\lim_m \mathbb{E}[XY_m]\): the dominating function is \(|XY_m| \le |X||Y| \in L^1\), since \(\mathbb{E}[|X||Y|] \le \|X\|_2\|Y\|_2 < \infty\) by Cauchy–Schwarz.
    • For \(\lim_m \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\,Y_m]\): the dominating function is \(|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]||Y| \in L^1\). On each atom \(A_n\), \(|\mathbb{E}_{A_n}[X]| \le \mathbb{E}_{A_n}[|X|]\) by the triangle inequality \(|\mathbb{E}[Z]| \le \mathbb{E}[|Z|]\). Therefore, exchanging the countable sum and expectation via MCT on non-negative terms: \[\mathbb{E}[|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]||Y|] \le \sum_n \mathbb{E}_{A_n}[|X|]\,|\beta_n|\,\mathbb{P}(A_n) = \mathbb{E}[|X||Y|] \le \|X\|_2\|Y\|_2 < \infty.\]

    Passing to the limit on both sides:

    \[ \mathbb{E}[X Y] = \lim_{m \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X Y_m] = \lim_{m \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] Y_m] = \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] Y]. \]

Finally, the measurability corollary gives \(\mathbb E_{\mathcal G}[X]\in L^0(\mathcal G)\). On each \(A_n\), the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality under \(\mathbb P_{A_n}\) yields \[ \left|\mathbb E_{A_n}[X]\right|^2\le\mathbb E_{A_n}[X^2]. \] Therefore, using MCT for the non-negative series, \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E\left[\mathbb E_{\mathcal G}[X]^2\right] &=\sum_{n=1}^\infty \left|\mathbb E_{A_n}[X]\right|^2\mathbb P(A_n) \\ &\le\sum_{n=1}^\infty\mathbb E_{A_n}[X^2]\mathbb P(A_n) \\ &=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\mathbb E[X^2\mathbb I_{A_n}] =\mathbb E[X^2]. \end{aligned} \] Thus \(\mathbb E_{\mathcal G}[X]\in L^2(\mathcal G)\) and \(\|\mathbb E_{\mathcal G}[X]\|_2\le\|X\|_2\).

Corollary 66.2 (Law of Total Expectation) Under the hypotheses of Theorem 66.1, taking \(Y = \mathbb{I}_\Omega = 1\) yields:

\[ \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]] = \mathbb{E}[X]. \]

66.6 Takeaways

  1. Explicit formula. For \(X\in L^1(\mathcal F)\) and a partition \(\{A_n\}\) of \(\Omega\) with \(\mathbb{P}(A_n) > 0\), the conditional expectation is \(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] = \sum_n \frac{\mathbb{E}[X\mathbb{I}_{A_n}]}{\mathbb{P}(A_n)} \mathbb{I}_{A_n}\): on each atom, it equals the average of \(X\) over that atom. It is \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable and integrable, and for \(\mathcal{G} = \sigma(Y)\) with \(Y\) countable-valued, it has the form \(\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y] = g(Y)\) with \(g(s) = \mathbb{E}[X \mid Y = s]\).

  2. Smoothing / projection property. For \(X \in L^2(\mathcal{F})\) and \(Y \in L^2(\mathcal{G})\): \(\mathbb{E}[XY] = \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\,Y]\). Equivalently, \(X - \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\) is orthogonal in \(L^2\) to every \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable function: \[ \begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}[XY] = \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\,Y]\\ &\implies \mathbb{E}[(X - \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}])Y] = 0. \end{aligned} \] The proof of \(\mathbb{E}[XY] = \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\,Y]\) first treats finitely supported atomwise functions and then passes to general \(Y\) via DCT; both limits are justified by the dominating functions \(|X||Y| \in L^1\) and \(|\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[X]||Y| \in L^1\).

  3. Law of total expectation. For \(X\in L^2(\mathcal F)\), taking \(Y = 1\) in the smoothing property gives \(\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]] = \mathbb{E}[X]\): conditioning and then averaging recovers the unconditional mean.
