68  Lecture 31.2: Conditional Expectation-Part II

68.1 Background: Hilbert Space Projections

Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) be a probability space. The space \(H = L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) equipped with the inner product \[\langle X, Y \rangle = \mathbb{E}[XY]\] and induced norm \(\|X\|_{L^2} = \sqrt{\mathbb{E}[X^2]}\) is a Hilbert space.

For any closed subspace \(K \subseteq H\), the Orthogonal Projection Theorem guarantees the existence of a unique linear operator \(P_K: H \to K\) such that for every \(X \in H\):

  1. \(P_K(X) \in K\) is the unique element minimizing the \(L^2\)-distance to \(X\): \[\|X - P_K(X)\|_{L^2} = \min_{Z \in K} \|X - Z\|_{L^2}\]
  2. \(P_K(X)\) is characterized by orthogonality: \[X - P_K(X) \perp K \iff \langle X - P_K(X), Y \rangle = 0 \quad \forall Y \in K\] Equivalently: \[\mathbb{E}[P_K(X) Y] = \mathbb{E}[XY] \quad \forall Y \in K\]
  3. \(P_K\) is a non-expansive linear transformation: \[\|P_K(X)\|_{L^2} \le \|X\|_{L^2}\]

68.2 \(L^2\) Subspaces Defined by Sub-\(\sigma\)-Fields

Let \(\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}\) be a sub-\(\sigma\)-field of \(\mathcal{F}\). We define the subset of \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable \(L^2\)-random variables: \[L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P) \coloneqq \{ X \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P) \mid X \text{ is } \mathcal{G}\text{-measurable} \}\]

Theorem 68.1 (Closure of Sub-\(\sigma\)-Field \(L^2\) Spaces) The subset \(L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\) is a closed linear subspace of \(L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\).

Proof (Proof of Theorem 68.1).

  1. Subspace Property:
    If \(X, Y \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\) and \(\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{R}\), then \(\alpha X + \beta Y\) is \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable. Moreover, by Minkowski’s inequality: \[\|\alpha X + \beta Y\|_{L^2} \le |\alpha| \|X\|_{L^2} + |\beta| \|Y\|_{L^2} < \infty\] Thus, \(L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\) is a vector subspace of \(L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\).

  2. Closedness Property:
    Let \(\{X_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty} \subset L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\) be a sequence such that \(X_n \to X\) in \(L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\). Convergence in \(L^2\) implies convergence in probability, which guarantees the existence of a subsequence \(\{X_{n_k}\}\) converging almost surely to \(X\): \[X_{n_k} \xrightarrow{a.s.} X \quad \text{as } k \to \infty\] Since each \(X_{n_k}\) is \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable, the almost sure limit \(X\) is also \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable (modulo null sets under completion). Hence, \(X \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\), showing that \(L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\) is closed.

68.3 Definition of Conditional Expectation in \(L^2\)

Definition 68.1 (Conditional Expectation in \(L^2\)) For any \(X \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) and sub-\(\sigma\)-field \(\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}\), the conditional expectation of \(X\) given \(\mathcal{G}\), denoted \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\) or \(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\), is defined as the orthogonal projection of \(X\) onto \(L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\): \[ \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] \coloneqq P_{L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)}(X). \]

By Theorem 67.1 and Theorem 67.2, \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\) is the unique \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable random variable satisfying the orthogonality condition: \[ \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] Y] = \mathbb{E}[XY] \quad \forall Y \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P). \]

68.4 Geometric Intuition

  1. \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\) is the \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable random variable closest to \(X\) in \(L^2\): \[ \|X - \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\|_{L^2} = \min_{Y \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)} \|X - Y\|_{L^2}. \] In information-theoretic terms, \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\) is the “best guess” or best mean-squared estimation of \(X\) given only the information in \(\mathcal{G}\).

  2. By the orthogonal projection property, the error \(X - \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]=X - P_{L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)}(X)\) is orthogonal to all \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable random variables: \[ \begin{aligned} &\langle X - P_{L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)}(X), Y \rangle = 0 \\ &\iff \mathbb{E}[(X - \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]) Y] = 0 \\ &\iff \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] Y] = \mathbb{E}[XY] , \end{aligned} \] for all \(Y \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\).

68.5 Concrete Example: Partition Sub-\(\sigma\)-field

Let \(\Omega = \{1, 2, 3\}\), \(\mathcal{F} = 2^{\Omega}\), and \(P\) be the uniform distribution \(P(\{i\}) = \frac{1}{3}\) for \(i \in \{1, 2, 3\}\).
Consider the sub-\(\sigma\)-field generated by the partition \(A_1 = \{1, 2\}\) and \(A_2 = \{3\}\): \[ \mathcal{G} = \sigma(\{1, 2\}, \{3\}) = \{ \emptyset, \{1, 2\}, \{3\}, \Omega \}. \]

Any \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable function \(X\) must be constant on the atoms of \(\mathcal{G}\), meaning \(X(1) = X(2)\). Thus, \[ L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P) \cong \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} x \\ x \\ y \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid x, y \in \mathbb{R} \right\}. \]

An orthogonal basis for this subspace under the standard inner product \(\mathbb{E}[XY] = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{i=1}^3 X(i)Y(i)\) is given by the indicator functions: \[ u_1 = \mathbb{1}_{\{1, 2\}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad u_2 = \mathbb{1}_{\{3\}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}. \]

For any random variable \(X = \begin{bmatrix} X(1) \\ X(2) \\ X(3) \end{bmatrix} \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\), the projection onto \(L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\) is: \[ \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X u_1]}{\|u_1\|_{L^2}^2} u_1 + \frac{\mathbb{E}[X u_2]}{\|u_2\|_{L^2}^2} u_2 = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X \mathbb{1}_{\{1, 2\}}]}{P(\{1, 2\})} \mathbb{1}_{\{1, 2\}} + \frac{\mathbb{E}[X \mathbb{1}_{\{3\}}]}{P(\{3\})} \mathbb{1}_{\{3\}}. \]

Evaluating the components explicitly: \[ \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X](1) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X](2) = \frac{X(1) + X(2)}{2}, \quad \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X](3) = X(3). \]

68.6 Extension of Conditional Expectation to \(L^1\)

To extend the definition from \(L^2\) to \(L^1\), we first establish a contractivity lemma in \(L^1\).

Lemma 68.1 (\(L^1\)-Contraction Lemma) For any \(X \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\), \[ \mathbb{E}\left[|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]|\right] \le \mathbb{E}[|X|]. \] That is, \(\|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\|_{L^1} \le \|X\|_{L^1}\).

  • Note that the \(L^1\) norms are
    1. \(\|X\|_{L^1} = \mathbb{E}[|X|]=\int_\Omega |X| dP\), and
    2. \(\|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\|_{L^1} = \mathbb{E}[|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]|] = \int_\Omega |\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]| dP\) is the \(L^1\) norm of the conditional expectation.
  • The lemma states that the conditional expectation operator is a contraction in \(L^1\).

Proof (Proof of Lemma 68.1). Set \(Y = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\). Consider the sign function \(Z = \operatorname{sgn}(Y)\), defined as: \[ \begin{align*} Z(\omega) &= \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } Y(\omega) > 0 \\ 0 & \text{if } Y(\omega) = 0 \\ -1 & \text{if } Y(\omega) < 0 \end{cases}\\ &=\mathbb{I}_{\{Y\ne0\}} \frac{Y}{|Y|}. \end{align*} \] Since \(Y\) is \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable, \(Z\) is also \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable. Furthermore, \(|Z(\omega)| \le 1\), so \(Z \in L^\infty(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P) \subset L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\).

Using \(Z\) as a test function in the orthogonality characterization of \(L^2\) conditional expectation: \[ \mathbb{E}[|Y|] = \mathbb{E}[Y \cdot \operatorname{sgn}(Y)] = \mathbb{E}[Y Z] = \mathbb{E}[X Z]. \] Since \(|Z(\omega)| \le 1\), we have \[ \mathbb{E}[|Y|] = \mathbb{E}[X Z] \le \mathbb{E}[|X Z|] \le \mathbb{E}[|X| \cdot 1] = \mathbb{E}[|X|]. \] Hence, \(\|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\|_{L^1} \le \|X\|_{L^1}\).

68.7 Extension Procedure via Density

Note that \(L^2(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)\subset L^1(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)\). Indeed, by Cauchy–Schwarz, \[ \mathbb E[|X|] = \mathbb E[|X|\cdot 1] \leq \sqrt{\mathbb E[X^2]}\sqrt{\mathbb E[1]} = \sqrt{\mathbb E[X^2]}. \] So \(X\in L^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad X\in L^1\). But the converse is not true. For example, a random variable can have a finite first moment but an infinite second moment. So the hierarchy is: \(L^2 \subsetneq L^1\). This distinction is important: \(L^1\) is the natural space for defining conditional expectation in general, whereas \(L^2\) is the natural space when we want the geometric interpretation as an orthogonal projection.

In this section, we will discuss how to extend the definition of conditional expectation from \(L^2\) to \(L^1\) using the density of \(L^2\) in \(L^1\).

  • Density of \(L^2\) in \(L^1\): For any \(X \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\), define \(X_n = X \cdot \mathbb{1}_{\{|X| \le n\}}\). Each \(X_n\) is bounded, hence \(X_n \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\). By the Dominated Convergence Theorem (DCT): \[ \|X - X_n\|_{L^1} = \mathbb{E}[|X| \mathbb{1}_{\{|X| > n\}}] \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} 0. \] Therefore, \(L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) is dense in \(L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\). That is, for any \(X \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\), there exists a sequence \(\{X_n\} \subset L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) such that \[ \|X - X_n\|_{L^1} \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} 0. \]

  • Definition in \(L^1\): Given \(X \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\), select any sequence \(\{X_n\}_{n=1}^\infty \subset L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) converging to \(X\) in \(L^1\). By Lemma 68.1: \[ \|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X_n] - \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X_m]\|_{L^1} = \|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X_n - X_m]\|_{L^1} \le \|X_n - X_m\|_{L^1}. \] Thus, \(\{\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X_n]\}_{n=1}^\infty\) is a Cauchy sequence in the complete space \(L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\). We define: \[ \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] \coloneqq L^1\text{-}\lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X_n]= L^1\text{-}\lim_{n \to \infty} P_{L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)}(X_n). \] That is, we have \[ \|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X_n]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\|_{L^1} = \mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X_n]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\right|\right] \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} 0. \]

  • Well-Definedness: If \(\{Y_n\}\) is another sequence in \(L^2\) converging to \(X\) in \(L^1\), then: \[ \|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X_n] - \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[Y_n]\|_{L^1} \le \|X_n - Y_n\|_{L^1} \to 0. \] The limit is unique and independent of the choice of approximating sequence.

  • Conclusion: This gives the standard \(L^1\)-conditional expectation as an extension of the \(L^2\) orthogonal-projection operator. That is, conceptually,

    1. \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] = P_{L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)}(X) \quad \text{for } X \in L^2\), and
    2. \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] = L^1\text{-}\lim_{n \to \infty} P_{L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)}(X_n) \quad \text{for } X \in L^1\), where \(X_n \in L^2\) and \(X_n \to X\) in \(L^1\).

So structurally, we have the following hierarchy and extension process: \[ \boxed{ \begin{array}{c} X\in L^2 \\[2mm] \downarrow \\ \mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G] =P_{L^2(\Omega,\mathcal{G},P)}(X) \\[2mm] \downarrow\quad\text{\(L^1\)-contraction} \\ \text{continuous extension} \\[2mm] \downarrow \\ X\in L^1 \\[2mm] \mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G] = L^1\!-\!\lim_n\mathbb E[X_n\mid\mathcal G]= L^1\text{-}\lim_{n \to \infty} P_{L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)}(X_n). \end{array} } \] That gives a very clean geometric-to-measure-theoretic construction of conditional expectation.

68.8 Averaging Property / Characterization

By Definition 68.1, the orthogonality condition gives the following averaging property for conditional expectation: \[ \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] Y] = \mathbb{E}[XY] \quad \forall Y \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P). \] In the case of \(L^1\), the averaging characterization uses \(Y\in\mathcal B_b(\Omega,\mathcal G)\). This change is important. For \(X\in L^1\), the product \(XY\) is guaranteed to be integrable when \(Y\) is bounded: \[ |XY|\leq \|Y\|_\infty |X|\in L^1. \] So bounded \(\mathcal G\)-measurable functions are exactly the natural test functions for the \(L^1\) definition.

Theorem 68.2 (Averaging Property) For \(X \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) and sub-\(\sigma\)-field \(\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}\), the conditional expectation \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\) is the unique \(L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\) random variable satisfying: \[ \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] Y] = \mathbb{E}[XY] \quad \forall Y \in \mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{G}), \] where \(\mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{G})\) denotes the space of bounded, \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable random variables.

Proof (Proof of Theorem 68.2).

  1. Existence:
    For \(X \in L^2\), this holds directly by Definition 68.1. For general \(X \in L^1\), let \(X_n \in L^2 \to X\) in \(L^1\). For each \(n\), since \(X_n \in L^2\), the \(L^2\) definition gives: \[\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X_n] Y] = \mathbb{E}[X_n Y].\] We pass to the limit \(n \to \infty\) on both sides. Fix any \(Y \in \mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{G})\) with \(|Y| \le M\). On the left side: \[|\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X_n] Y] - \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] Y]| \le M \|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X_n] - \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\|_{L^1} \to 0.\] On the right side: \[|\mathbb{E}[X_n Y] - \mathbb{E}[XY]| \le M \|X_n - X\|_{L^1} \to 0.\] Since both sides converge to the same quantity in the limit, we conclude \(\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] Y] = \mathbb{E}[XY]\).

  2. Uniqueness:
    Suppose \(Z_1, Z_2 \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\) both satisfy the property. Then \(\mathbb{E}[(Z_1 - Z_2) Y] = 0\) for all \(Y \in \mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{G})\).
    Choose \(Y_n = \operatorname{sgn}(Z_1 - Z_2) \cdot \mathbb{1}_{\{|Z_1 - Z_2| \le n\}} \in \mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{G})\). Then: \[0 = \mathbb{E}[(Z_1 - Z_2) Y_n] = \mathbb{E}[|Z_1 - Z_2| \mathbb{1}_{\{|Z_1 - Z_2| \le n\}}]\] By Monotone Convergence Theorem (MCT) as \(n \to \infty\), \(\mathbb{E}[|Z_1 - Z_2|] = 0\), implying \(Z_1 = Z_2\) almost surely.

Remark 68.1 (Remark on Averaging Property with Indicator Functions). The averaging property can be particularly useful when \(Y\) is taken as an indicator function of a \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable set \(A \in \mathcal{G}\): \[ \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] \mathbb{1}_A] = \mathbb{E}[X \mathbb{1}_A]. \] This shows that the conditional expectation \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\) correctly averages \(X\) over the sets in \(\mathcal{G}\).

In terms of integral notation, the averaging property with indicator functions can be written as: \[ \int_A \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] \, dP = \int_A X \, dP. \] Thus, the conditional expectation preserves the integral of \(X\) over every event whose occurrence can be determined from \(\mathcal G\). That is why it is called the averaging property.

Finally, note that the indicator function characterization is equivalent to the averaging property for all bounded \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable functions, since any such function can be approximated by linear combinations of indicator functions.

68.9 Fundamental Properties of Conditional Expectation

Theorem 68.3 Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) be a probability space, \(\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}\) a sub-\(\sigma\)-field, and \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}: L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P) \to L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\) the linear operator.

  1. Monotonicity:
    If \(X \le Y\) a.s., then \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] \le \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[Y]\) a.s.

  2. Triangle Inequality:
    \(|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]| \le \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[|X|]\) a.s. If \(|X| \le M\) a.s., then \(|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]| \le M\) a.s.

  3. Averaging Property:
    \(\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] Y] = \mathbb{E}[XY]\) for all \(Y \in \mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{G})\).

  4. Product Rule (Taking out what is known):
    If \(Y \in \mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{G})\), then \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[XY] = Y \cdot \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\) a.s.

  5. Tower Property (Iterated Expectations):
    If \(\mathcal{H} \subseteq \mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}\) are sub-\(\sigma\)-fields, then: \[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[X]] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[X] \quad \text{a.s.}\]

68.10 Detailed Proofs of Fundamental Properties

Property (3) is exactly the averaging characterization established in Theorem 68.2, so the only remaining properties to verify here are (1), (2), (4), and (5).

68.10.1 Proof of (1) Monotonicity:

First, we establish the positivity of conditional expectation. Suppose \(Z\ge0\) a.s. Define \(A=\{\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal G]<0\}\). Since \(\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal G]\) is \(\mathcal G\)-measurable, \(A\in\mathcal G\). By the averaging property, \[ \mathbb E\left[\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal G]1_A\right] = \mathbb E[Z1_A]. \] The right-hand side is nonnegative because \(Z\ge0\) a.s. However, on \(A\), \(\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal G]1_A<0\). Therefore, if \(P(A)>0\), \[ \mathbb E\left[\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal G]1_A\right]<0, \] which contradicts \(\mathbb E[Z1_A]\ge0\). Hence \(P(A)=0\), so \[ Z\ge0\quad\text{a.s.} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal G]\ge0\quad\text{a.s.} \] Now, since \(X\le Y\) a.s., we have \(Y-X\ge0\quad\text{a.s.}\). By positivity, \(\mathbb E[Y-X\mid\mathcal G]\ge0\quad\text{a.s.}\). Using linearity, \[ \mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal G]-\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]\ge0 \quad\text{a.s.} \] Therefore, \(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]\le \mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal G]\quad\text{a.s.}\). So the essential argument is positivity first, then apply it to \(Y-X\). \(\blacksquare\)

68.10.2 Proof of (2) Triangle Inequality:

Note that \(X \le |X|\) and \(-X \le |X|\) a.s.
By Monotonicity (1): \[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] \le \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[|X|] \quad \text{and} \quad -\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[-X] \le \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[|X|]\] Combining both yields \(|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]| \le \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[|X|]\) a.s.
If \(|X| \le M\) a.s., then \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[|X|] \le \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[M] = M\) a.s. \(\blacksquare\)

68.10.3 Proof of (4) Product Rule:

Let \(Y \in \mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{G})\) with \(|Y| \le M\). Since \(X \in L^1\) and \(Y\) is bounded, \(XY \in L^1\), so \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[XY]\) is well-defined. We prove the claim by verifying that \(Y \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\) satisfies the two conditions that uniquely characterize \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[XY]\) via Theorem 68.2.

\(\mathcal{G}\)-measurability: Both \(Y\) and \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\) are \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable, so their product \(Y \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\) is \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable. Moreover, \(\|Y \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\|_{L^1} \le M \|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\|_{L^1} \le M \|X\|_{L^1} < \infty\), so \(Y \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\).

Averaging property: For any test function \(Z \in \mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{G})\), the product \(YZ\) is also bounded and \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable, i.e. \(YZ \in \mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{G})\). Since \(|XYZ| \le M\|Z\|_\infty |X|\), all expectations below are finite. Therefore: \[ \begin{align*} \mathbb{E}\left[(Y \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]) \cdot Z\right] &= \mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] \cdot (YZ)\right] && \text{(rearranging)} \\ &= \mathbb{E}\left[X \cdot (YZ)\right] && \text{(averaging property)} \\ &= \mathbb{E}\left[(XY) \cdot Z\right]. && \text{(rearranging)} \end{align*} \] Since \(Y \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]\) is \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable and satisfies \(\mathbb{E}[Y\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] \cdot Z] = \mathbb{E}[XY \cdot Z]\) for all \(Z \in \mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{G})\), uniqueness in Theorem 68.2 gives \(Y \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[XY]\) a.s. \(\blacksquare\)

68.10.4 Proof of (5) Tower Property:

Let \(\mathcal{H} \subseteq \mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}\).
Since \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P) \subseteq L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) and \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[X] \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{H}, P)\), both iterated conditional expectations are well-defined. For any \(Z \in \mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{H})\), since \(\mathcal{H} \subseteq \mathcal{G}\), we also have \(Z \in \mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{G})\).
Using the averaging property at level \(\mathcal{H}\), then level \(\mathcal{G}\): \[\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]] Z\right] = \mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X] Z\right] = \mathbb{E}[X Z] = \mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[X] Z\right]\] By uniqueness of the conditional expectation onto \(\mathcal{H}\), we conclude: \[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[X] \quad \text{a.s.}\] For the second equality, note that \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[X]\) is \(\mathcal{H}\)-measurable, hence also \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable because \(\mathcal{H} \subseteq \mathcal{G}\). More generally, if \(W \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\), then \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[W] = W\) a.s.: \(W\) is already \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable and, for every \(Z \in \mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{G})\), it satisfies the averaging identity \[ \mathbb{E}[WZ] = \mathbb{E}[WZ]. \] Therefore, by uniqueness in Theorem 68.2, \(W\) must equal its own conditional expectation given \(\mathcal{G}\). Applying this fixed-point property with \(W = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[X]\) yields \[ \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[X]] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[X] \quad \text{a.s.} \] Combining the two identities proves \[ \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[X]] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[X]] = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{H}}[X] \quad \text{a.s.} \] The first equality is the substantive tower property; the second follows from the fact that conditioning does nothing to a random variable that is already measurable with respect to the conditioning \(\sigma\)-field. \(\blacksquare\)

68.11 Takeaways

1. Conditional expectation as orthogonal projection in \(L^2\).
For \(X \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\), the conditional expectation \(\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[X]\) is defined as the orthogonal projection onto the closed subspace \(L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\). The closedness of this subspace — proved by extracting an a.s.-convergent subsequence from any \(L^2\)-Cauchy sequence of \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable functions — is precisely what makes the Hilbert-space projection theorem applicable.

2. The averaging property is the universal characterization.
\(\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[X]\) is the unique \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable \(L^1\) random variable satisfying \[\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[X] \cdot Y] = \mathbb{E}[XY] \quad \forall Y \in \mathcal{B}_b(\Omega, \mathcal{G}).\] This is orthogonality rewritten in \(L^1\) language. Uniqueness uses the sign-function test: setting \(Y_n = \operatorname{sgn}(Z_1 - Z_2)\mathbb{1}_{\{|Z_1-Z_2| \le n\}}\) and applying MCT forces \(\mathbb{E}[|Z_1 - Z_2|] = 0\), hence \(Z_1 = Z_2\) a.s.

3. Extension from \(L^2\) to \(L^1\) rests on three steps.

  1. \(L^1\)-contraction: \(\|\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[X]\|_{L^1} \le \|X\|_{L^1}\) for \(X \in L^2\), proved by using \(\operatorname{sgn}(\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[X])\) as a bounded \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable test function and applying Hölder.
  2. Density: the truncations \(X_n = X \cdot \mathbb{1}_{\{|X| \le n\}}\) show \(L^2\) is dense in \(L^1\) via DCT.
  3. Cauchy completion: the contraction makes \(\{\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[X_n]\}\) Cauchy in the complete space \(L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)\); well-definedness follows because any two approximating sequences yield the same \(L^1\) limit.

4. Monotonicity is proved by a zero-integral sandwich argument.
To show \(X \le Y\) a.s. implies \(\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[X] \le \mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[Y]\) a.s., let \(B = \{\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[Y-X] < 0\}\). Using \(\mathbb{1}_B\) as a test function gives \(\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[Y-X]\mathbb{1}_B] = \mathbb{E}[(Y-X)\mathbb{1}_B] \ge 0\), while the definition of \(B\) forces the same quantity to be \(\le 0\). Hence \(\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[Y-X]\mathbb{1}_B] = 0\), so \(P(B) = 0\).

5. The product rule and tower property both reduce to the uniqueness of conditional expectation.

  • Product rule: verify that \(Y\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[X]\) satisfies the averaging characterization of \(\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[XY]\) by testing against \(Z \in \mathcal{B}_b(\mathcal{G})\): the product \(YZ\) is bounded \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable, so the averaging property applies to shift \(Y\) inside.
  • Tower property (\(\mathcal{H} \subseteq \mathcal{G}\)): \(\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{H}[\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[X]] = \mathbb{E}_\mathcal{H}[X]\) because both satisfy the same \(\mathcal{H}\)-averaging property (since every \(Z \in \mathcal{B}_b(\mathcal{H}) \subseteq \mathcal{B}_b(\mathcal{G})\) can be used to apply \(\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}\) and then \(\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{H}\) in sequence). The reverse identity \(\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{H}[X]] = \mathbb{E}_\mathcal{H}[X]\) follows from idempotency: any \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable \(W \in L^1\) satisfies \(\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{G}[W] = W\) by uniqueness, since \(W\) trivially satisfies its own averaging characterization.
