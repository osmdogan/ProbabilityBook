70.1 Extending Conditional Expectation Beyond \(L^1\)

Definition 70.1 (Conditional Expectation for Non-Negative Random Variables) Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})\) be a probability space, and let \(\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}\) be a sub-\(\sigma\)-field. If \(X \ge 0\) almost surely (not necessarily in \(L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})\)), there exists a unique (up to \(\mathbb{P}\)-almost sure equivalence) \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable random variable, denoted \(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\), such that for every non-negative \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable random variable \(Y\) (or equivalently, every \(B \in \mathcal{G}\)), \[ \mathbb{E}[X \mathbb{I}_B] = \mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] \mathbb{I}_B\big]. \tag{70.1}\] If \(X \in L^1\), \(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\) coincides with the standard \(L^1\) conditional expectation.

Proposition 70.1 (Well-Definedness of Conditional Expectation for \(X \ge 0\)) If \(X \ge 0\) almost surely, and \(\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}\) is a sub-\(\sigma\)-field, then there exists a unique (up to \(\mathbb{P}\)-null sets) \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable random variable \(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\) satisfying: \[ \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] \mathbb{I}_B] = \mathbb{E}[X \mathbb{I}_B], \quad \forall B \in \mathcal{G}. \]

Proof. Existence:

To define \(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\), consider the truncated sequence \(X_n = X \wedge n\) for each \(n \in \mathbb{N}\). Since \(0 \le X_n \le n\), we have \(X_n \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})\), so the standard conditional expectation \(\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}]\) exists for all \(n \in \mathbb{N}\).

By the monotonicity of conditional expectation on \(L^1\), the sequence \((\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}])_{n=1}^\infty\) is non-decreasing almost surely. Therefore, we may define: \[ \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] := \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \quad \text{a.s.} \]

Now, fix \(B \in \mathcal{G}\). Applying the classical Monotone Convergence Theorem (MCT) twice—first for the integrated sequence of non-negative \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable functions, and second for \(X_n \mathbb{I}_B\): \[ \begin{align*} \mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] \mathbb{I}_B\big] &= \mathbb{E}\left[\lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \mathbb{I}_B\right]\\ & \stackrel{\text{MCT}}{=} \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \mathbb{I}_B\big] \\ &= \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mathbb{I}_B]\\ & \stackrel{\text{MCT}}{=} \mathbb{E}[X \mathbb{I}_B]. \end{align*} \] This establishes the defining property.

Uniqueness:

Let \(W=\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]\). Suppose \(Z\ge0\) is \(\mathcal G\)-measurable and satisfies \(\mathbb E[Z1_B]=\mathbb E[X1_B]\) for all \(B\in\mathcal G\). We want to show \(Z=W\) a.s.

For the finite-valued part, define \(B_m=\{Z>W\}\cap\{Z\le m\}\). On \(B_m\), we have \[ 0\le W<Z\le m, \] so both \(Z1_{B_m}\) and \(W1_{B_m}\) are integrable. Therefore \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[(Z-W)1_{B_m}] &=\mathbb E[Z1_{B_m}]-\mathbb E[W1_{B_m}]\\ &=\mathbb E[X1_{B_m}]-\mathbb E[X1_{B_m}]\\ &=0. \end{aligned} \] Since \((Z-W)1_{B_m}\ge0\), it follows that \((Z-W)1_{B_m}=0\) a.s. But \(Z>W\) on \(B_m\), so \(P(B_m)=0\). Hence \(P(Z>W,\;Z<\infty)=0\).

We still need to deal with \(Z=\infty\). Define \(C_m=\{Z=\infty,\;W\le m\}\). If \(P(C_m)>0\), then \(\mathbb E[Z1_{C_m}]=\infty\), whereas \(\mathbb E[W1_{C_m}]\le mP(C_m)<\infty\). But both quantities must equal \(\mathbb E[X1_{C_m}]\), a contradiction. Hence \(P(C_m)=0\) for all \(m\). Since \(\{Z=\infty,\;W<\infty\} = \bigcup_{m=1}^\infty C_m\), we obtain \(P(Z=\infty,\;W<\infty)=0\). The same argument with \(Z\) and \(W\) interchanged gives \(P(W>Z)=0\). Thus \(Z=W \quad\text{a.s.}\)

Remark 70.1 (Monotonicity of Extended Conditional Expectation). The extended conditional expectation is monotone: if \(0 \le X \le Y\) a.s., then \[ \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] = \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X \wedge n \mid \mathcal{G}] \le \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[Y \wedge n \mid \mathcal{G}] = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{G}] \quad \text{a.s.} \]

70.2 Integral Convergence Theorems for Conditional Expectation

Theorem 70.1 (Conditional Convergence Theorems) The conditional expectation operator \(\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot \mid \mathcal{G}]\) satisfies the standard integral convergence theorems for non-negative random variables:

  1. Conditional Monotone Convergence Theorem (MCT): If \(0 \le X_n \uparrow X\) a.s., then \[ \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] = \mathbb{E}\left[\lim_{n \to \infty} X_n \;\middle|\; \mathcal{G}\right] = \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] \quad \text{a.s.} \]
  2. Conditional Fatou’s Lemma: If \(X_n \ge 0\) a.s. for all \(n \in \mathbb{N}\), then \[ \mathbb{E}\left[\liminf_{n \to \infty} X_n \;\middle|\; \mathcal{G}\right] \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \quad \text{a.s.} \]
  3. Conditional Dominated Convergence Theorem (DCT): If \(X_n \to X\) a.s., \(|X_n| \le Y\) a.s. for all \(n \in \mathbb{N}\), and \(Y \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})\), then \[ \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \to \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] \quad \text{a.s. and in } L^1. \]

Proof. Proof of (a) Conditional MCT

By the monotonicity of \(\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot \mid \mathcal{G}]\), the sequence \((\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}])_{n=1}^\infty\) is non-decreasing a.s., so its limit exists a.s. Fix \(B \in \mathcal{G}\). By the standard Monotone Convergence Theorem: \[ \begin{align*} \mathbb{E}\left[ \left(\lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}]\right) \mathbb{I}_B \right] &= \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \mathbb{I}_B\big] \\ &= \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mathbb{I}_B] \\ &= \mathbb{E}\left[\left(\lim_{n \to \infty} X_n\right) \mathbb{I}_B\right] \\ &= \mathbb{E}[X \mathbb{I}_B]. \end{align*} \] By definition of conditional expectation, this implies \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] = \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\) a.s.

Proof of (b) Conditional Fatou’s Lemma

Let \(Y_k = \inf_{n \ge k} X_n\). Then \(Y_k \le X_n\) for all \(n \ge k\), and \(Y_k \uparrow \liminf_{n \to \infty} X_n\) as \(k \to \infty\). Monotonicity implies \(\mathbb{E}[Y_k \mid \mathcal{G}] \le \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}]\) for all \(n \ge k\), so taking the infimum over \(n \ge k\) gives: \[ \mathbb{E}[Y_k \mid \mathcal{G}] \le \inf_{n \ge k} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \quad \text{a.s.} \] Taking the limit as \(k \to \infty\) and applying the Conditional Monotone Convergence Theorem to \(Y_k \uparrow \liminf_{n \to \infty} X_n\): \[ \begin{align*} \mathbb{E}\left[\liminf_{n \to \infty} X_n \;\middle|\; \mathcal{G}\right]& = \lim_{k \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[Y_k \mid \mathcal{G}] \\ &\le \lim_{k \to \infty} \inf_{n \ge k} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}]\\ & = \liminf_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \quad \text{a.s.} \end{align*} \]

Proof of (c) Conditional DCT First, by the standard Dominated Convergence Theorem, \(X_n \to X\) in \(L^1\), i.e., \(\mathbb{E}[|X_n - X|] \to 0\). Since \(\pm(X_n - X) \le |X_n - X|\), monotonicity of conditional expectation gives \(|\mathbb{E}[X_n - X \mid \mathcal{G}]| \le \mathbb{E}[|X_n - X| \mid \mathcal{G}]\) a.s. Taking total expectations: \[ \begin{align*} \left\| \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] - \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] \right\|_1 &= \left\| \mathbb{E}[X_n - X \mid \mathcal{G}] \right\|_1 \\ &\le \mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}[|X_n - X| \mid \mathcal{G}]\big] = \|X_n - X\|_1 \to 0, \end{align*} \] which proves \(\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \to \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\) in \(L^1\).

For almost-sure convergence, note that \(0 \le Y \pm X_n \text{ a.s.}\) Apply Conditional Fatou’s Lemma to both sequences:

  1. For \(Y + X_n \ge 0\): \[ \begin{align*} \mathbb{E}[Y + X \mid \mathcal{G}] &= \mathbb{E}\left[\liminf_{n \to \infty} (Y + X_n) \;\middle|\; \mathcal{G}\right] \\ &\le \liminf_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[Y + X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \\ &= \mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{G}] + \liminf_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \quad \text{a.s.} \end{align*} \] Subtracting \(\mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{G}] < \infty\) a.s. yields \(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}]\) a.s.

  2. For \(Y - X_n \ge 0\): \[ \begin{align*} \mathbb{E}[Y - X \mid \mathcal{G}] &= \mathbb{E}\left[\liminf_{n \to \infty} (Y - X_n) \;\middle|\; \mathcal{G}\right] \\ &\le \liminf_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[Y - X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \\ &= \mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{G}] - \limsup_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \quad \text{a.s.} \end{align*} \] Subtracting \(\mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{G}] < \infty\) a.s. and multiplying by \(-1\) yields \(\limsup_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \le \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\) a.s.

Combining both inequalities yields: \[ \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \le \limsup_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \le \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] \quad \text{a.s.,} \] hence \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] = \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\) a.s.

70.3 Conditional Jensen’s Inequality and \(L^p\) Contractions

Theorem 70.2 (Conditional Jensen’s Inequality) Let \(X \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})\) and let \(\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}\) be a sub-\(\sigma\)-field. If \(\varphi: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) is convex and \(\varphi(X) \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})\), then \[ \varphi(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]) \le \mathbb{E}[\varphi(X) \mid \mathcal{G}] \quad \text{a.s.} \tag{70.2}\] In particular, \(\mathbb{E}[\varphi(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}])] \le \mathbb{E}[\varphi(X)]\).

Proof. To illustrate the underlying mechanism, we first consider the smooth case where \(\varphi \in C^2(\mathbb{R})\) with \(\varphi'' > 0\). By Taylor’s theorem, for any \(x, y \in \mathbb{R}\): \[ \varphi(y) = \varphi(x) + \varphi'(x)(y - x) + \int_x^y (y - t) \varphi''(t) \, dt \ge \varphi(x) + \varphi'(x)(y - x). \] Substituting \(y = X\) and \(x = \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\) (which is \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable): \[ \varphi(X) \ge \varphi(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]) + \varphi'(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]) (X - \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]) \quad \text{a.s.} \] Taking conditional expectations with respect to \(\mathcal{G}\) on both sides, and noting that \(\varphi(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}])\) and \(\varphi'(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}])\) are \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable: \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}[\varphi(X) \mid \mathcal{G}] &\ge \mathbb{E}\left[ \varphi(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]) + \varphi'(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]) (X - \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]) \;\middle|\; \mathcal{G} \right] \\ &= \varphi(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]) + \varphi'(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]) \underbrace{\mathbb{E}[X - \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] \mid \mathcal{G}]}_{= 0} \\ &= \varphi(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]) \quad \text{a.s.} \end{aligned} \]

For a general convex function \(\varphi\), recall that \(\varphi\) is the supremum of a countable collection of affine lines: \(\varphi(t) = \sup_{k \in \mathbb{N}} (a_k t + b_k)\). For each \(k \in \mathbb{N}\): \[ \varphi(X) \ge a_k X + b_k \implies \mathbb{E}[\varphi(X) \mid \mathcal{G}] \ge \mathbb{E}[a_k X + b_k \mid \mathcal{G}] = a_k \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] + b_k \quad \text{a.s.} \] Taking the supremum over \(k \in \mathbb{N}\) yields: \[ \mathbb{E}[\varphi(X) \mid \mathcal{G}] \ge \sup_{k \in \mathbb{N}} \left( a_k \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] + b_k \right) = \varphi(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]) \quad \text{a.s.} \] Taking total expectations on both sides yields \(\mathbb{E}[\varphi(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}])] \le \mathbb{E}[\varphi(X)]\).

Corollary 70.1 (\(L^p\) Contraction Property) For \(1 \le p < \infty\), the conditional expectation operator \(\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot \mid \mathcal{G}]: L^p(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}) \to L^p(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, \mathbb{P})\) is a non-expansive contraction; that is, for all \(X \in L^p\): \[ \|\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\|_p \le \|X\|_p. \tag{70.3}\]

Proof. Consider the function \(\varphi(x) = |x|^p\). Since \(p \ge 1\), \(\varphi\) is convex on \(\mathbb{R}\). If \(X \in L^p\), then \(\varphi(X) = |X|^p \in L^1\).

Applying Conditional Jensen’s Inequality (Equation 70.2): \[ \left| \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] \right|^p \le \mathbb{E}\left[ |X|^p \;\middle|\; \mathcal{G} \right] \quad \text{a.s.} \] Taking total expectations on both sides: \[ \mathbb{E}\left[ \left| \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] \right|^p \right] \le \mathbb{E}\left[ \mathbb{E}\left[ |X|^p \;\middle|\; \mathcal{G} \right] \right] = \mathbb{E}\left[ |X|^p \right]. \] Taking the \(p\)-th root gives \(\|\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\|_p \le \|X\|_p\).

70.4 Takeaways

  1. Extension to \(X \ge 0\). Conditional expectation extends beyond \(L^1\) by setting \(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] := \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[X \wedge n \mid \mathcal{G}]\) a.s. The limit is non-decreasing; MCT applied twice (once to \(\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}]\mathbb{I}_B\) and once to \(X_n \mathbb{I}_B\)) verifies the defining identity, and uniqueness follows by truncating to bounded sets in \(\mathcal{G}\).

  2. Conditional MCT and Fatou. If \(0 \le X_n \uparrow X\) a.s., then \(\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \uparrow \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\) a.s. (Conditional MCT), proved by fixing \(B \in \mathcal{G}\) and applying ordinary MCT to pass the limit through the integral on both sides. Conditional Fatou follows by setting \(Y_k = \inf_{n \ge k} X_n \uparrow \liminf_n X_n\): monotonicity gives \(\mathbb{E}[Y_k \mid \mathcal{G}] \le \inf_{n \ge k} \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}]\) a.s. (by a countable intersection of null sets), and Conditional MCT takes the limit on the left.

  3. Conditional DCT. If \(X_n \to X\) a.s. and \(|X_n| \le Y \in L^1\), then \(\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \to \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\) in \(L^1\) and a.s. The \(L^1\) part uses \(|\mathbb{E}[Z \mid \mathcal{G}]| \le \mathbb{E}[|Z| \mid \mathcal{G}]\) a.s. (from monotonicity applied to \(\pm Z \le |Z|\)), giving \(\|\mathbb{E}[Z \mid \mathcal{G}]\|_1 \le \|Z\|_1\). The a.s. part applies Conditional Fatou to the non-negative sequences \(Y \pm X_n\): the two inequalities \(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] \le \liminf_n \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}]\) and \(\limsup_n \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{G}] \le \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\) are obtained by subtracting \(\mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{G}] < \infty\) a.s. (since \(Y \in L^1\)).

  4. Conditional Jensen and \(L^p\) contraction. For convex \(\varphi\) with \(\varphi(X) \in L^1\): \(\varphi(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]) \le \mathbb{E}[\varphi(X) \mid \mathcal{G}]\) a.s. The proof represents \(\varphi = \sup_k (a_k t + b_k)\) as a countable supremum of affine functions; linearity of conditional expectation gives the inequality for each affine minorant, and taking the supremum recovers \(\varphi(\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}])\). As a special case with \(\varphi(t) = |t|^p\) (\(p \ge 1\)): \(\|\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]\|_p \le \|X\|_p\).
