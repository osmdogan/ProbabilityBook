9  Lecture 4.2: Extension Theorem

9.1 Introduction and Review

Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{A}, \mu)\) be a finite premeasure space, where \(\mu: \mathcal{A} \to [0, \mu(\Omega)]\) is countably additive on the field \(\mathcal{A}\).

Let \(\mu^*: 2^\Omega \to [0, \mu(\Omega)]\) denote the induced Carathéodory outer measure: \[ \mu^*(E) := \inf \left\{ \sum_{j=1}^\infty \mu(A_j) : \{A_j\}_{j=1}^\infty \subseteq \mathcal{A}, \, E \subseteq \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty A_j \right\}. \tag{9.1}\]

We equip \(2^\Omega\) with the outer pseudo-metric \(d_\mu(E, F) := \mu^*(E \Delta F)\) for all \(E, F \subseteq \Omega\). As proved in the previous lecture, the closure \(\overline{\mathcal{A}}\) of \(\mathcal{A}\) in \((2^\Omega, d_\mu)\) is a \(\sigma\)-algebra.

9.2 Lipschitz Continuity of \(\mu\) and Extension to \(\overline{\mathcal{A}}\)

We first show that \(\mu\) is 1-Lipschitz continuous on \((\mathcal{A}, d_\mu)\) and therefore extends uniquely to \(\overline{\mathcal{A}}\).

Proposition 9.1 (1-Lipschitz Continuity of \(\mu\)) For any \(A, B \in \mathcal{A}\), we have \(\mu^*\vert{}_{ \mathcal{A}} = \mu\). Furthermore, \(\mu\) satisfies: \[ \vert{}\mu(A) - \mu(B)\vert{} \le d_\mu(A, B). \tag{9.2}\]

Proof (Proof of Proposition 9.1). For \(A, B \in \mathcal{A}\), by finite additivity on \(\mathcal{A}\): \[ \mu(A) = \mu(A \setminus B) + \mu(A \cap B) \le \mu(A \Delta B) + \mu(B) = d_\mu(A, B) + \mu(B). \] Rearranging yields \(\mu(A) - \mu(B) \le d_\mu(A, B)\). Reversing the roles of \(A\) and \(B\) gives \(\mu(B) - \mu(A) \le d_\mu(A, B)\). Combining both inequalities yields: \[ \vert{}\mu(A) - \mu(B)\vert{} \le d_\mu(A, B). \]

Proposition 9.2 (Unique Extension \(\overline{\mu}\)) Since \(\mu\) is 1-Lipschitz on the non-empty set \(\mathcal{A}\) in the pseudo-metric space \((2^\Omega, d_\mu)\), it extends uniquely to a continuous 1-Lipschitz function \(\overline{\mu}: \overline{\mathcal{A}} \to [0, \mu(\Omega)]\) such that \(\overline{\mu}\vert{}_{\mathcal{A}} = \mu\).

9.3 \(\sigma\)-Invariance and Monotone Sequence Convergence

Definition 9.1 (Countable Unions Collection \(\mathcal{E}_\sigma\)) For any collection \(\mathcal{E} \subseteq 2^\Omega\), define \(\mathcal{E}_\sigma\) as the collection of all countable unions of elements in \(\mathcal{E}\): \[ \mathcal{E}_\sigma := \left\{ \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty E_n : \{E_n\}_{n=1}^\infty \subseteq \mathcal{E} \right\}. \tag{9.3}\]

Note: \(\mathcal{E}_\sigma\) is automatically closed under countable unions. If \(\mathcal{E}\) is closed under finite intersections, then \(\mathcal{E}_\sigma\) is also closed under finite intersections: \[ \left(\bigcup_{i=1}^\infty E_i\right) \cap \left(\bigcup_{j=1}^\infty F_j\right) = \bigcup_{i,j=1}^\infty (E_i \cap F_j) \in \mathcal{E}_\sigma. \]

Lemma 9.1 (Closure Invariance under Countable Unions) For a finite premeasure space \((\Omega, \mathcal{A}, \mu)\), we have \(\overline{\mathcal{A}}_\sigma = \overline{\mathcal{A}}\). Furthermore, \(\overline{\mu} = \mu^*\) on \(\mathcal{A}_\sigma\).

Proof (Proof of Lemma 9.1). Let \(A \in \mathcal{A}_\sigma\), so \(A = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty B_n\) with \(B_n \in \mathcal{A}\). Define \(A_n := \bigcup_{j=1}^n B_j \in \mathcal{A}\). Then \(A_n \uparrow A\).

By the Approximation Lemma (from the previous lecture), \(d_\mu(A_n, A) = \mu^*(A) - \mu(A_n) \to 0\) as \(n \to \infty\). Thus, \(A\) is the sequential limit of \((A_n)_{n=1}^\infty \subseteq \mathcal{A}\), which implies \(A \in \overline{\mathcal{A}}\). Hence \(\mathcal{A}_\sigma \subseteq \overline{\mathcal{A}}\).

By continuity of the extension \(\overline{\mu}\), we have \(\mu^*(A) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \mu(A_n) = \overline{\mu}(A)\) for all \(A \in \mathcal{A}_\sigma\).

Finally, since \(\mathcal{A} \subseteq \overline{\mathcal{A}}\) and \(\overline{\mathcal{A}}\) is a \(\sigma\)-algebra (hence closed under countable unions), we have: \[ \overline{\mathcal{A}} \subseteq \overline{\mathcal{A}}_\sigma \subseteq \overline{\overline{\mathcal{A}}} = \overline{\mathcal{A}}, \]

which yields \(\overline{\mathcal{A}}_\sigma = \overline{\mathcal{A}}\).

9.4 Characterization of Sets in \(\overline{\mathcal{A}}\)

Theorem 9.1 (Outer Approximation Characterization) Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{A}, \mu)\) be a finite premeasure space, and let \(B \in 2^\Omega\). The following statements are equivalent:

  1. \(B \in \overline{\mathcal{A}}\).
  2. For every \(\epsilon > 0\), there exists a set \(C \in \mathcal{A}_\sigma\) such that \(B \subseteq C\) and \[ \mu^*(C \setminus B) = d_\mu(B, C) < \epsilon. \tag{9.4}\]

Proof (Proof of Theorem 9.1). (2) \(\Rightarrow\) (1): Assume Condition (2) holds. For each \(n \in \mathbb{N}\), select \(C_n \in \mathcal{A}_\sigma\) such that \(B \subseteq C_n\) and \(d_\mu(B, C_n) < \frac{1}{n}\). Then \(d_\mu(C_n, B) \to 0\) as \(n \to \infty\), so \(B \in \overline{\mathcal{A}_\sigma}\). Since \(\overline{\mathcal{A}_\sigma} = \overline{\mathcal{A}}\), it follows that \(B \in \overline{\mathcal{A}}\).

(1) \(\Rightarrow\) (2): Fix \(\epsilon > 0\). By (1), \(B \in \overline{\mathcal{A}}\), so there exists \(G \in \mathcal{A}\) such that: \[ d_\mu(B, G) = \mu^*(B \Delta G) < \frac{\epsilon}{2}. \]

By definition of outer measure as an infimum, since \(\mu^*(B \Delta G) < \frac{\epsilon}{2}\), there exists a sequence \(\{A_n\}_{n=1}^\infty \subseteq \mathcal{A}\) covering \(B \Delta G\) (i.e., \(B \Delta G \subseteq \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\)) such that: \[ \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu(A_n) < \frac{\epsilon}{2}. \]

Define \(C := G \cup \left(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\right) \in \mathcal{A}_\sigma\).

  1. Containment (\(B \subseteq C\)): \[ B \subseteq (B \setminus G) \cup G \subseteq (B \Delta G) \cup G \subseteq \left(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\right) \cup G = C. \]

  2. Difference Bound (\(\mu^*(C \setminus B) < \epsilon\)): \[ \begin{align*} C \setminus B &= \left( G \cup \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n \right) \setminus B \\ &= (G \setminus B) \cup \left( \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n \setminus B \right) \\ &\subseteq (B \Delta G) \cup \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n \\ &\subseteq \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n. \end{align*} \]

    By monotonicity and subadditivity of \(\mu^*\): \[ \mu^*(C \setminus B) \le \mu^*\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\right) \le \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu(A_n) < \frac{\epsilon}{2} < \epsilon. \]

This establishes Condition (2).

9.5 Identity of \(\mu^*\) and \(\overline{\mu}\) on \(\overline{\mathcal{A}}\)

Corollary 9.1 (Coincidence of Outer Measure and Extended Measure) Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{A}, \mu)\) be a finite premeasure space. Then: \[ \mu^* = \overline{\mu} \quad \text{on } \overline{\mathcal{A}}. \tag{9.5}\]

Proof (Proof of Corollary 9.1). Let \(B \in \overline{\mathcal{A}}\).

  1. Upper Bound (\(\overline{\mu}(B) \le \mu^*(B)\)): Since \(\overline{\mu}\) is 1-Lipschitz and \(\overline{\mu}(\emptyset) = 0\): \[ \vert{}\overline{\mu}(B)\vert{} = \vert{}\overline{\mu}(B) - \overline{\mu}(\emptyset)\vert{} \le d_\mu(B, \emptyset) = \mu^*(B \Delta \emptyset) = \mu^*(B). \]

    Thus \(\overline{\mu}(B) \le \mu^*(B)\).

  2. Lower Bound (\(\mu^*(B) \le \overline{\mu}(B)\)): Fix \(\epsilon > 0\). By Theorem 9.1, select \(C \in \mathcal{A}_\sigma\) such that \(B \subseteq C\) and \(d_\mu(B, C) = \mu^*(C \setminus B) < \epsilon\). By 1-Lipschitz continuity of \(\overline{\mu}\): \[ \vert{}\overline{\mu}(B) - \overline{\mu}(C)\vert{} \le d_\mu(B, C) < \epsilon \implies \overline{\mu}(C) \le \overline{\mu}(B) + \epsilon. \]

    Since \(C \in \mathcal{A}_\sigma\), Lemma 9.1 implies \(\overline{\mu}(C) = \mu^*(C)\). By monotonicity of outer measure (\(B \subseteq C \implies \mu^*(B) \le \mu^*(C)\)): \[ \mu^*(B) \le \mu^*(C) = \overline{\mu}(C) \le \overline{\mu}(B) + \epsilon. \]

    Taking \(\epsilon \downarrow 0\) yields \(\mu^*(B) \le \overline{\mu}(B)\).

Combining both bounds yields \(\overline{\mu}(B) = \mu^*(B)\) for all \(B \in \overline{\mathcal{A}}\).

9.6 Proof that \(\overline{\mu}\) is a Measure

Theorem 9.2 (Measure Extension Main Theorem) If \((\Omega, \mathcal{A}, \mu)\) is a finite premeasure space, then the unique extension \(\overline{\mu}: \overline{\mathcal{A}} \to [0, \mu(\Omega)]\) is a measure on the \(\sigma\)-algebra \(\overline{\mathcal{A}}\).

Proof (Proof of Theorem 9.2). We show that \(\overline{\mu}\) is finitely additive on \(\overline{\mathcal{A}}\).

Let \(A, B \in \overline{\mathcal{A}}\). By definition of closure, choose sequences \((A_n)_{n=1}^\infty \subseteq \mathcal{A}\) and \((B_n)_{n=1}^\infty \subseteq \mathcal{A}\) such that \(d_\mu(A_n, A) \to 0\) and \(d_\mu(B_n, B) \to 0\).

By Property 2 of \(d_\mu\), we have: \[ d_\mu(A_n \cup B_n, A \cup B) \le d_\mu(A_n, A) + d_\mu(B_n, B) \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} 0, \]

and similarly \(d_\mu(A_n \cap B_n, A \cap B) \to 0\).

By inclusion-exclusion on the algebra \(\mathcal{A}\) for each \(n \in \mathbb{N}\): \[ \mu(A_n \cup B_n) + \mu(A_n \cap B_n) = \mu(A_n) + \mu(B_n). \tag{9.6}\]

Taking limits as \(n \to \infty\) and using continuity of \(\overline{\mu}\): \[ \begin{align*} \overline{\mu}(A \cup B) + \overline{\mu}(A \cap B) &= \lim_{n \to \infty} [\mu(A_n \cup B_n) + \mu(A_n \cap B_n)] \\ &= \lim_{n \to \infty} [\mu(A_n) + \mu(B_n)] \\ &= \overline{\mu}(A) + \overline{\mu}(B). \end{align*} \]

If \(A\) and \(B\) are disjoint (\(A \cap B = \emptyset\)), then \(\overline{\mu}(A \cap B) = \overline{\mu}(\emptyset) = 0\), yielding finite additivity: \[ \overline{\mu}(A \cup B) = \overline{\mu}(A) + \overline{\mu}(B). \]

Thus, \(\overline{\mu}\) is a finitely-additive measure on the \(\sigma\)-algebra \(\overline{\mathcal{A}}\), which implies \(\overline{\mu}\) is countably superadditive.

On the other hand, Corollary 9.1 establishes that \(\overline{\mu} = \mu^*\) on \(\overline{\mathcal{A}}\). Since the outer measure \(\mu^*\) is countably subadditive on \(2^\Omega\), \(\overline{\mu}\) is countably subadditive on \(\overline{\mathcal{A}}\).

Combining countable superadditivity and countable subadditivity proves that \(\overline{\mu}\) is countably additive on \(\overline{\mathcal{A}}\). Since \(\overline{\mu}(\emptyset) = 0\), \(\overline{\mu}\) is a measure.
