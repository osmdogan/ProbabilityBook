88  Lecture 41.1: Generator Matrix

88.1 Introduction and Background

Recall from previous lectures that if \((Q_t)_{t \ge 0}\) is a Markov transition semigroup on the Banach space \(B(S, \mathcal{B})\) of bounded measurable functions equipped with the supremum norm (so that \(Q_0 = I\) and \(Q_{t+s} = Q_t Q_s\) for all \(s, t \ge 0\)), and \((Q_t)_{t \ge 0}\) is continuous in operator norm at \(t = 0\), i.e. \(\|Q_t - I\|_{\mathrm{op}} \to 0\) as \(t \downarrow 0\), then there exists a bounded linear operator \(A \in \mathcal{L}(B(S, \mathcal{B}))\) called the generator such that \[ Q_t = e^{tA} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{t^n}{n!} A^n, \quad t \ge 0, \tag{88.1}\] where the series converges in operator norm, and \(A = \lim_{t \downarrow 0} \frac{Q_t - I}{t}\) in operator norm.

Markov chains on discrete state spaces are a classical setting in which operator-norm continuity can be characterized concretely in terms of the transition probabilities. As we shall see, when the state space is infinite this is a genuinely restrictive, uniform condition (Remark 88.1).

Let \(S\) be a countable set endowed with the discrete \(\sigma\)-algebra \(\mathcal{B} = 2^S\). Since every function on \(S\) is \(2^S\)-measurable, the Banach space \(B(S) \coloneqq B(S, 2^S)\) is exactly \(\ell_\infty(S)\), the space of all bounded real-valued functions \(f \colon S \to \mathbb{R}\), equipped with the supremum norm \[ \|f\|_{\infty} = \sup_{i \in S} |f(i)|. \]

For each \(t \ge 0\), the operator action \(Q_t f\) on \(f \in B(S)\) is defined pointwise by \[ Q_t f(i) = \sum_{j \in S} q_t(i, j) f(j), \quad i \in S, \tag{88.2}\] where \((q_t)_{t \ge 0}\) are the transition probability mass functions satisfying:

  1. Non-negativity: \(q_t(i, j) \ge 0\) for all \(i, j \in S\) and \(t \ge 0\).
  2. Unit row sums: \(\sum_{j \in S} q_t(i, j) = 1\) for all \(i \in S\) and \(t \ge 0\).
  3. Initial condition: \(q_0(i, j) = \delta_{ij}\) for all \(i, j \in S\).
  4. Chapman–Kolmogorov equations: \(q_{t+s}(i, k) = \sum_{j \in S} q_t(i, j)\, q_s(j, k)\) for all \(i, k \in S\) and \(s, t \ge 0\).

By 1 and 2, the series in Equation 88.2 converges absolutely, since \(\sum_{j} q_t(i, j) |f(j)| \le \|f\|_\infty\); in particular, the sum over the countable set \(S\) does not depend on the order of summation. Conditions 3 and 4 are exactly the statements \(Q_0 = I\) and \(Q_{t+s} = Q_t Q_s\) evaluated on indicator functions \(\mathbf{1}_{\{k\}}\).

88.2 Matrix Operators and Their Norms

Throughout, a matrix means a family \(\Theta = (\Theta(i, j))_{i, j \in S}\) of real numbers. Its row-sum norm is \[ \|\Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}} \coloneqq \sup_{i \in S} \sum_{j \in S} |\Theta(i, j)| \in [0, \infty]. \] (We use the subscript “row” to avoid confusion with the supremum norm \(\|f\|_\infty\) of a function.)

Definition 88.1 (Matrix Representation) A bounded linear operator \(T \colon B(S) \to B(S)\) is represented by the matrix \(\Theta\) if \(\|\Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}} < \infty\) and \[ T f(i) = \sum_{j \in S} \Theta(i, j) f(j) \quad \text{for all } f \in B(S),\ i \in S. \] Taking \(f = \mathbf{1}_{\{j\}}\) shows that necessarily \(\Theta(i, j) = T(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i)\); hence a representing matrix, if it exists, is unique.

Lemma 88.1 (Operator Norm of a Matrix Operator) Let \(\Theta\) be a matrix with \(\|\Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}} < \infty\). Then for every \(f \in B(S)\) and \(i \in S\) the series \(\sum_{j \in S} \Theta(i, j) f(j)\) converges absolutely, the formula \[ T_\Theta f(i) \coloneqq \sum_{j \in S} \Theta(i, j) f(j) \] defines a bounded linear operator \(T_\Theta\) on \(B(S)\), and \[ \|T_\Theta\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \|\Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}}. \] Moreover, \(T_{\Theta} - T_{\Theta'} = T_{\Theta - \Theta'}\) whenever \(\|\Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}}, \|\Theta'\|_{\mathrm{row}} < \infty\).

Proof (Proof of Lemma 88.1). For \(f \in B(S)\) and \(i \in S\), \[ \sum_{j \in S} |\Theta(i, j)|\, |f(j)| \le \|f\|_\infty \sum_{j \in S} |\Theta(i, j)| \le \|\Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}} \|f\|_\infty < \infty, \] so the series converges absolutely and \(\|T_\Theta f\|_\infty \le \|\Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}} \|f\|_\infty\). Linearity of \(T_\Theta\), and the identity \(T_\Theta - T_{\Theta'} = T_{\Theta - \Theta'}\), follow from linearity of absolutely convergent series. Hence \(T_\Theta\) is bounded with \(\|T_\Theta\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \|\Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}}\).

For the reverse inequality, fix \(i_0 \in S\) and define \(f(j) \coloneqq \operatorname{sgn} \Theta(i_0, j)\) (with \(\operatorname{sgn} 0 = 0\)). Then \(\|f\|_\infty \le 1\) and \[ \sum_{j \in S} |\Theta(i_0, j)| = T_\Theta f(i_0) \le \|T_\Theta f\|_\infty \le \|T_\Theta\|_{\mathrm{op}}. \] Taking the supremum over \(i_0 \in S\) gives \(\|\Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}} \le \|T_\Theta\|_{\mathrm{op}}\).

By Equation 88.2, each \(Q_t\) is represented by the matrix \(q_t\), and by Lemma 88.1, \[ \|Q_t\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \|q_t\|_{\mathrm{row}} = \sup_{i \in S} \sum_{j \in S} q_t(i, j) = 1. \]

88.3 Operator Norm Continuity Condition

The identity \(I\) is represented by the matrix \((\delta_{ij})\), so by Lemma 88.1 the operator \(Q_t - I\) is represented by \(q_t - \delta\) and its operator norm can be expressed directly in terms of the transition probabilities. Using \(\sum_{j \ne i} q_t(i, j) = 1 - q_t(i, i)\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \|Q_t - I\|_{\mathrm{op}} &= \|q_t - \delta\|_{\mathrm{row}} = \sup_{i \in S} \sum_{j \in S} |q_t(i, j) - \delta_{ij}| \\ &= \sup_{i \in S} \left( |q_t(i, i) - 1| + \sum_{j \neq i} q_t(i, j) \right) \\ &= \sup_{i \in S} \left( (1 - q_t(i, i)) + (1 - q_t(i, i)) \right) \\ &= 2 \sup_{i \in S} \bigl(1 - q_t(i, i)\bigr). \end{aligned} \tag{88.3}\]

Thus, \(\|Q_t - I\|_{\mathrm{op}} \to 0\) as \(t \downarrow 0\) if and only if \[ \lim_{t \downarrow 0} \sup_{i \in S} \bigl(1 - q_t(i, i)\bigr) = 0, \quad \text{i.e.,} \quad \lim_{t \downarrow 0} \inf_{i \in S} q_t(i, i) = 1. \tag{88.4}\]

Hence, if \((q_t)_{t \ge 0}\) satisfy conditions 1–4 on a countable state space \(S\) and \(\lim_{t \downarrow 0} \inf_{i \in S} q_t(i, i) = 1\), then the semigroup \((Q_t)_{t \ge 0}\) has a bounded generator \(A\), and conversely.

Remark 88.1 (Finite versus Infinite State Spaces). If \(S\) is finite, the infimum in Equation 88.4 is a minimum over finitely many states, so operator-norm continuity is equivalent to the pointwise condition \(q_t(i, i) \to 1\) for every \(i \in S\).

If \(S\) is infinite, Equation 88.4 is strictly stronger than pointwise continuity. For example, let \(S = \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}\) and consider the chain that waits at state \(i\) for an exponential time with rate \(i\) and then jumps to the absorbing state \(0\): \[ q_t(i, i) = e^{-it}, \qquad q_t(i, 0) = 1 - e^{-it} \ (i \ge 1), \qquad q_t(0, 0) = 1, \] and all other entries are \(0\). One checks directly that conditions 1–4 hold; e.g. \(q_{t+s}(i, 0) = e^{-it}(1 - e^{-is}) + (1 - e^{-it}) \cdot 1 = 1 - e^{-i(t+s)}\). Here \(q_t(i, i) \to 1\) as \(t \downarrow 0\) for each fixed \(i\), but \(\inf_{i} q_t(i, i) = 0\) for every \(t > 0\), so \(\|Q_t - I\|_{\mathrm{op}} = 2\) for all \(t > 0\). Thus this semigroup does not have a bounded generator. As we shall see in Corollary 88.2, operator-norm continuity forces the jump rates \(-a(i, i)\) to be uniformly bounded.

88.4 The Generator Matrix Problem

We now ask: What can we say about the operator \(A\)?

Since \(\frac{Q_t - I}{t} \to A\) in operator norm, we have \(A f(i) = \lim_{t \downarrow 0} \frac{Q_t f(i) - f(i)}{t}\) for every \(f \in B(S)\) and \(i \in S\). Using \(q_0(i, j) = \delta_{ij}\) and writing \(\left. \frac{d}{dt} \right|_{t=0^+}\) for the right derivative at \(t = 0\), this reads \[ \begin{aligned} A f(i) &= \left. \frac{d}{dt} \right|_{t=0^+} Q_t f(i) \\ &= \left. \frac{d}{dt} \right|_{t=0^+} \sum_{j \in S} q_t(i, j) f(j) \\ &\overset{?}{=} \sum_{j \in S} \left( \left. \frac{d}{dt} \right|_{t=0^+} q_t(i, j) \right) f(j). \end{aligned} \]

This formal calculation suggests that \(A\) is represented by a matrix \((a(i, j))_{i, j \in S}\) with entries: \[ a(i, j) = \left. \frac{d}{dt} \right|_{t=0^+} q_t(i, j). \tag{88.5}\]

When the state space \(S\) is infinite, interchanging the derivative with the infinite summation \(\sum_{j \in S}\) (the step marked “?”) requires rigorous proof; it is also not a priori clear that the derivatives on the right-hand side exist.

88.5 Matrix Representation of Bounded Linear Operators on \(B(S)\)

88.5.1 Do all bounded operators have matrix representations?

One might naively expect that every bounded linear operator \(T \colon B(S) \to B(S)\) is represented by a matrix. If \(S\) is finite, every function \(f \in B(S)\) can be written uniquely as a finite sum: \[ f = \sum_{j \in S} f(j) \mathbf{1}_{\{j\}}. \]

More generally, call \(f\) finitely supported if \(f(j) = 0\) for all but finitely many \(j \in S\), and denote the space of such functions by \(c_{00}(S)\). (This is not the notion of a simple function from Lecture 7.1, which requires only that \(f\) take finitely many values.) For \(f \in c_{00}(S)\), linearity of \(T\) yields: \[ T f(i) = \sum_{j \in S} f(j) T(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i). \]

Thus, on finitely supported functions, \(T\) is given by the matrix \[ \Theta_T(i, j) \coloneqq T(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i). \] This matrix always has finite row-sum norm: fix \(i \in S\) and a finite set \(F \subseteq S\), and let \(f = \sum_{j \in F} \operatorname{sgn}(\Theta_T(i, j)) \mathbf{1}_{\{j\}}\). Then \(\|f\|_\infty \le 1\) and \(\sum_{j \in F} |\Theta_T(i, j)| = T f(i) \le \|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\); taking the supremum over \(F\) and then over \(i\) gives \(\|\Theta_T\|_{\mathrm{row}} \le \|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\). Hence, by Lemma 88.1, \(T_{\Theta_T}\) is a bounded operator that agrees with \(T\) on \(c_{00}(S)\). The real question is therefore whether \(T = T_{\Theta_T}\) on all of \(B(S)\).

When \(S\) is infinite, this cannot be settled by a density argument under the supremum norm.

Remark 88.2 (Basic Obstacle: Finitely Supported Functions Are Not Dense). Let \(S\) be infinite. For any \(g \in c_{00}(S)\), choose \(j \in S\) outside the (finite) support of \(g\); then \[ \|\mathbf{1} - g\|_{\infty} \ge |1 - g(j)| = 1. \] Thus \(c_{00}(S)\) is not dense in \(B(S)\) under the supremum norm; its closure is the space \(c_0(S)\) of functions \(f\) such that \(\{j : |f(j)| > \varepsilon\}\) is finite for every \(\varepsilon > 0\). For instance, if \(F_1 \subseteq F_2 \subseteq \cdots\) are finite sets with \(\bigcup_k F_k = S\), then \(\mathbf{1}_{F_k} \to \mathbf{1}\) pointwise, but \(\|\mathbf{1} - \mathbf{1}_{F_k}\|_\infty = 1\) for all \(k\). (By contrast, simple functions in the sense of Lecture 7.1 are dense in \(B(S)\).)

Consequently, the fact that \(T\) and \(T_{\Theta_T}\) agree on \(c_{00}(S)\) does not imply that they agree on \(B(S)\).

Example 88.1 (A Bounded Operator without a Matrix Representation) Let \(S = \mathbb{N}\) and let \(L \colon \ell_\infty(\mathbb{N}) \to \mathbb{R}\) be a Banach limit, i.e. a positive linear functional with \(L(\mathbf{1}) = 1\) and \(L(f) = \lim_{j \to \infty} f(j)\) whenever this limit exists (its existence follows from the Hahn–Banach theorem). Define \[ T f(i) \coloneqq L(f), \quad i \in \mathbb{N}. \] Then \(T\) is linear, positive, \(T \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{1}\), and \(\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}} = 1\). However, \(T(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}}) = 0\) for every \(j\), since \(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}}(k) \to 0\) as \(k \to \infty\). Hence \(\Theta_T \equiv 0\) while \(T \ne 0\), so \(T\) is not represented by any matrix (by Definition 88.1, the only candidate is \(\Theta_T\)).

If \(S\) is finite, every linear operator on \(B(S)\) is represented by a matrix, so such examples exist only for infinite \(S\).

Remark 88.3 (Characterization of Matrix Operators). A bounded linear operator \(T\) on \(B(S)\) is represented by a matrix if and only if it is bounded-pointwise sequentially continuous: whenever \(f_n \to f\) pointwise with \(\sup_n \|f_n\|_\infty < \infty\), we have \(T f_n \to T f\) pointwise. (Compare the bounded pointwise continuity condition in the characterization of Markov transition operators in Lecture 36.2.)

Indeed, if \(T = T_\Theta\), this follows from dominated convergence for the counting measure on \(S\), with dominating function \(j \mapsto \sup_n \|f_n\|_\infty |\Theta(i, j)|\). Conversely, given \(f \in B(S)\), choose finite sets \(F_k \uparrow S\); then \(f \mathbf{1}_{F_k} \to f\) boundedly and pointwise, so \[ T f(i) = \lim_{k \to \infty} T(f \mathbf{1}_{F_k})(i) = \lim_{k \to \infty} \sum_{j \in F_k} \Theta_T(i, j) f(j) = \sum_{j \in S} \Theta_T(i, j) f(j), \] where the last series converges absolutely because \(\|\Theta_T\|_{\mathrm{row}} < \infty\). The operator in Example 88.1 fails this condition, since \(\mathbf{1}_{\{1, \dots, k\}} \to \mathbf{1}\) boundedly and pointwise but \(T(\mathbf{1}_{\{1, \dots, k\}}) = 0 \not\to \mathbf{1} = T\mathbf{1}\).

88.5.2 Approximation Theorem for Matrix Operators

The following proposition shows that the class of operators represented by matrices is closed under operator-norm limits.

Proposition 88.1 (Matrix Representation of Limit Operators) Let \((T_n)_{n \ge 1}\) be a sequence of bounded linear operators on \(B(S)\), each represented by a matrix \(\Theta_n = (\Theta_n(i, j))_{i, j \in S}\) with \(\|\Theta_n\|_{\mathrm{row}} < \infty\), so that \[ T_n f(i) = \sum_{j \in S} \Theta_n(i, j) f(j), \quad f \in B(S),\ i \in S, \] and (by Lemma 88.1) \(\|T_n\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \|\Theta_n\|_{\mathrm{row}}\). If \(T \in \mathcal{L}(B(S))\) satisfies \(\|T_n - T\|_{\mathrm{op}} \to 0\) as \(n \to \infty\), then:

  1. for all \(i, j \in S\) the limit \(\Theta(i, j) \coloneqq \lim_{n \to \infty} \Theta_n(i, j)\) exists and equals \(T(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i)\);
  2. \(\|\Theta_n - \Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}} \le \|T_n - T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) for every \(n\); in particular \(\|\Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}} < \infty\);
  3. \(T\) is represented by the matrix \(\Theta\).

Proof (Proof of Proposition 88.1). Step 1: Pointwise convergence of matrix elements. For any fixed \(j \in S\), evaluate \(T_n\) on the indicator function \(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}} \in B(S)\): \[ T_n(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i) = \sum_{k \in S} \Theta_n(i, k) \mathbf{1}_{\{j\}}(k) = \Theta_n(i, j). \]

Hence, for each \(i, j \in S\): \[ \begin{aligned} |\Theta_n(i, j) - T(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i)| &= |T_n(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i) - T(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i)| \\ & \le \|T_n(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}}) - T(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})\|_{\infty} \\ & \le \|T_n - T\|_{\mathrm{op}} \|\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}}\|_{\infty}. \end{aligned} \]

Since \(\|\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}}\|_{\infty} = 1\) and \(\|T_n - T\|_{\mathrm{op}} \to 0\), the limit exists: \[ \lim_{n \to \infty} \Theta_n(i, j) = T(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i) \eqcolon \Theta(i, j). \tag{88.6}\]

Step 2: Norms of differences. For any \(n, m \ge 1\), by Lemma 88.1 the operator \(T_n - T_m = T_{\Theta_n} - T_{\Theta_m}\) is represented by the matrix \(\Theta_n - \Theta_m\), and \[ \|\Theta_n - \Theta_m\|_{\mathrm{row}} = \sup_{i \in S} \sum_{j \in S} |\Theta_n(i, j) - \Theta_m(i, j)| = \|T_n - T_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}. \]

Step 3: Fatou’s Lemma argument for the row-sum bound. Fix \(i \in S\) and \(n \ge 1\). By Equation 88.6 and Fatou’s Lemma applied to the counting measure on \(S\): \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{j \in S} |\Theta_n(i, j) - \Theta(i, j)| &= \sum_{j \in S} \lim_{m \to \infty} |\Theta_n(i, j) - \Theta_m(i, j)| \\ &\le \liminf_{m \to \infty} \sum_{j \in S} |\Theta_n(i, j) - \Theta_m(i, j)| \\ &\le \liminf_{m \to \infty} \|T_n - T_m\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \|T_n - T\|_{\mathrm{op}}, \end{aligned} \] where the last equality holds because \(\bigl| \|T_n - T_m\|_{\mathrm{op}} - \|T_n - T\|_{\mathrm{op}} \bigr| \le \|T_m - T\|_{\mathrm{op}} \to 0\). The right-hand side does not depend on \(i\), so taking the supremum over \(i \in S\) yields: \[ \|\Theta_n - \Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}} \le \|T_n - T\|_{\mathrm{op}}. \tag{88.7}\]

By the triangle inequality for \(\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{row}}\), \(\|\Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}} \le \|\Theta - \Theta_n\|_{\mathrm{row}} + \|\Theta_n\|_{\mathrm{row}} < \infty\). Therefore, by Lemma 88.1, \(\Theta\) defines a bounded linear operator \(\hat{T} \coloneqq T_\Theta \in \mathcal{L}(B(S))\), \[ \hat{T}f(i) = \sum_{j \in S} \Theta(i, j) f(j). \]

Step 4: Identification of the limit operator. By Lemma 88.1, \(\hat{T} - T_n = T_{\Theta - \Theta_n}\), so by Equation 88.7 \[ \|\hat{T} - T_n\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \|\Theta - \Theta_n\|_{\mathrm{row}} \le \|T - T_n\|_{\mathrm{op}} \to 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty. \]

Thus \(T_n \to \hat{T}\) and \(T_n \to T\) in operator norm. Since limits in a normed space are unique, \(\hat{T} = T\). Hence \(T\) is represented by the matrix \(\Theta\), i.e. \(T f(i) = \sum_{j \in S} \Theta(i, j) f(j)\) for all \(f \in B(S)\) and \(i \in S\).

88.6 Construction and Properties of the Generator Matrix \(a = (a(i, j))\)

Corollary 88.1 (Matrix Representation of the Generator) Let \((q_t)_{t \ge 0}\) satisfy conditions 1–4 on a countable set \(S\), and assume the operator-norm continuity condition \[ \|Q_t - I\|_{\mathrm{op}} \to 0 \quad \text{as } t \downarrow 0 \quad \left(\text{equivalently, } \lim_{t \downarrow 0} \inf_{i \in S} q_t(i, i) = 1 \right). \] Let \(A\) be the generator of \((Q_t)_{t \ge 0}\). Then:

  1. for all \(i, j \in S\), the right derivative \[ a(i, j) \coloneqq \left. \frac{d}{dt} \right|_{t=0^+} q_t(i, j) = \lim_{t \downarrow 0} \frac{q_t(i, j) - \delta_{ij}}{t} \] exists and equals \(A(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i)\);
  2. \(A\) is represented by the matrix \(a = (a(i, j))_{i, j \in S}\); that is, \(\|a\|_{\mathrm{row}} < \infty\) and \(A f(i) = \sum_{j \in S} a(i, j) f(j)\) for all \(f \in B(S)\), \(i \in S\).

Proof (Proof of Corollary 88.1). By Equation 88.1, \[ \begin{aligned} \left\| \frac{Q_t - I}{t} - A \right\|_{\mathrm{op}} &= \left\| \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{t^{n-1}}{n!} A^n \right\|_{\mathrm{op}} \\ & \le \frac{e^{t\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}} - 1 - t\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{t} \to 0 \quad \text{as } t \downarrow 0. |\end{aligned} \tag{88.8}\]

Part 1. Since \(\frac{Q_t - I}{t}\) is represented by the matrix \(\frac{q_t - \delta}{t}\), for all \(i, j \in S\) and \(t > 0\), \[ \begin{aligned} \left| \frac{q_t(i, j) - \delta_{ij}}{t} - A(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i) \right| &= \left| \left( \frac{Q_t - I}{t} - A \right)(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i) \right| \\ & \le \left\| \frac{Q_t - I}{t} - A \right\|_{\mathrm{op}} \to 0. \end{aligned} \] Hence the right derivative \(a(i, j)\) exists and equals \(A(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i)\).

Part 2. Choose any sequence \(t_n \downarrow 0\) and define \(T_n \coloneqq \frac{Q_{t_n} - I}{t_n}\). Each \(T_n\) is represented by the matrix \(\Theta_n\) with entries \[ \Theta_n(i, j) = \frac{q_{t_n}(i, j) - \delta_{ij}}{t_n}, \qquad \|\Theta_n\|_{\mathrm{row}} \le \frac{2}{t_n} < \infty. \] By Equation 88.8, \(\|T_n - A\|_{\mathrm{op}} \to 0\), so Proposition 88.1 implies that \(A\) is represented by the matrix with entries \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \Theta_n(i, j) = a(i, j)\), the last equality by Part 1.

Corollary 88.2 (Properties of the Generator Matrix) Under the assumptions of Corollary 88.1, the matrix entries \(a(i, j)\) satisfy:

  1. Off-diagonal non-negativity: \(a(i, j) \ge 0\) for all \(i \neq j\).
  2. Non-positive diagonal: \(a(i, i) \le 0\) for all \(i \in S\).
  3. Zero row sums: \(\sum_{j \in S} a(i, j) = 0\) for all \(i \in S\), where the series converges absolutely; equivalently, \(\sum_{j \ne i} a(i, j) = -a(i, i)\).
  4. Bounded diagonal: \(\sup_{i \in S} |a(i, i)| < \infty\); more precisely, \(\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \|a\|_{\mathrm{row}} = 2 \sup_{i \in S} |a(i, i)|\).

Proof (Proof of Corollary 88.2).

  1. For \(i \neq j\), \(q_t(i, j) \ge 0\) and \(\delta_{ij} = 0\). Thus: \[ a(i, j) = \lim_{t \downarrow 0} \frac{q_t(i, j)}{t} \ge 0. \]

  2. Since \(q_t(i, i) \le \sum_j q_t(i, j) = 1\), \[ a(i, i) = \lim_{t \downarrow 0} \frac{q_t(i, i) - 1}{t} \le 0. \]

  3. By Corollary 88.1, \(A\) is represented by \(a\), so applying this representation to the constant function \(\mathbf{1} \in B(S)\) (which is not finitely supported when \(S\) is infinite) gives \(A \mathbf{1}(i) = \sum_{j \in S} a(i, j)\), an absolutely convergent series since \(\|a\|_{\mathrm{row}} < \infty\). On the other hand, \[ Q_t \mathbf{1}(i) = \sum_{j \in S} q_t(i, j) = 1 \implies \frac{Q_t \mathbf{1} - \mathbf{1}}{t} = 0 \implies A \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{0}, \] where the last implication uses Equation 88.8. Therefore, \(\sum_{j \in S} a(i, j) = 0\) for every \(i \in S\).

  4. By 1–3, for each \(i \in S\), \[ \sum_{j \in S} |a(i, j)| = |a(i, i)| + \sum_{j \ne i} a(i, j) = |a(i, i)| - a(i, i) = 2 |a(i, i)|. \] Taking the supremum over \(i\) and using Lemma 88.1 together with Corollary 88.1 gives \(\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \|a\|_{\mathrm{row}} = 2 \sup_i |a(i, i)| < \infty\).

The four properties in Corollary 88.2 in fact characterize generators of operator-norm continuous Markov semigroups on a countable state space.

Theorem 88.1 (Every Bounded Q-Matrix Generates a Markov Semigroup) Let \(a = (a(i, j))_{i, j \in S}\) be a matrix on a countable set \(S\) such that \(a(i, j) \ge 0\) for \(i \ne j\), \(\sum_{j \in S} a(i, j) = 0\) for every \(i\), and \(\lambda_0 \coloneqq \sup_{i \in S} |a(i, i)| < \infty\). Let \(A \coloneqq T_a\) and \(Q_t \coloneqq e^{tA}\), and set \(q_t(i, j) \coloneqq Q_t(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i)\). Then \((q_t)_{t \ge 0}\) satisfies conditions 1–4, each \(Q_t\) is represented by the matrix \(q_t\), \(\|Q_t - I\|_{\mathrm{op}} \to 0\) as \(t \downarrow 0\), and the generator matrix of \((q_t)_{t \ge 0}\) is \(a\).

Proof (Proof of Theorem 88.1). By the computation in the proof of Corollary 88.2 (4), \(\|a\|_{\mathrm{row}} = 2\lambda_0 < \infty\), so \(A = T_a\) is bounded by Lemma 88.1 and \(e^{tA}\) is well defined.

Representation. If \(T_\Theta\) and \(T_{\Theta'}\) are matrix operators with finite row-sum norms, then for \(f \in B(S)\) \[ T_\Theta T_{\Theta'} f(i) = \sum_{j} \Theta(i, j) \sum_{k} \Theta'(j, k) f(k) = \sum_{k} \Bigl( \sum_j \Theta(i, j) \Theta'(j, k) \Bigr) f(k), \] where interchanging the sums is justified by Fubini’s theorem for the counting measure, since \(\sum_{j, k} |\Theta(i, j)| |\Theta'(j, k)| |f(k)| \le \|\Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}} \|\Theta'\|_{\mathrm{row}} \|f\|_\infty\). Hence products, and by Lemma 88.1 finite linear combinations, of matrix operators are matrix operators. In particular each partial sum \(\sum_{n=0}^{N} \frac{t^n}{n!} A^n\) is represented by a matrix, and these partial sums converge to \(Q_t\) in operator norm. By Proposition 88.1, \(Q_t\) is represented by the matrix \(q_t(i, j) = Q_t(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i)\).

Conditions 1–4. Choose \(\lambda > 0\) with \(\lambda \ge \lambda_0\) and let \(p(i, j) \coloneqq \delta_{ij} + a(i, j)/\lambda\). Then \(p(i, j) \ge 0\) for all \(i, j\) (for \(i = j\) because \(a(i, i) \ge -\lambda\)) and \(\sum_j p(i, j) = 1\), so \(P \coloneqq T_p\) maps non-negative functions to non-negative functions. Since \(A = \lambda(P - I)\) and \(I\) commutes with \(P\), \[ Q_t = e^{-\lambda t} e^{\lambda t P} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} e^{-\lambda t} \frac{(\lambda t)^n}{n!} P^n, \] with convergence in operator norm and hence pointwise. Each term maps \(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}} \ge 0\) to a non-negative function, so \(q_t(i, j) \ge 0\) (condition 1). Since \(A \mathbf{1} = \sum_j a(\cdot, j) = 0\), we get \(Q_t \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{1}\), i.e. \(\sum_j q_t(i, j) = 1\) (condition 2). Condition 3 follows from \(Q_0 = I\). For condition 4, \(Q_{t+s} = Q_t Q_s\) (as \(tA\) and \(sA\) commute), so \[ q_{t+s}(i, k) = Q_t\bigl(Q_s \mathbf{1}_{\{k\}}\bigr)(i) = Q_t\bigl(q_s(\cdot, k)\bigr)(i) = \sum_{j} q_t(i, j)\, q_s(j, k). \]

Continuity and generator. \(\|Q_t - I\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le e^{t\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}} - 1 \to 0\), and the generator of \((Q_t)\) is \(A = T_a\), so by Corollary 88.1 its generator matrix is \(A(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}})(i) = a(i, j)\).

88.7 Takeaways

  • Setting. For a Markov chain on a countable state space \(S\), the transition operators \(Q_t f(i) = \sum_j q_t(i, j) f(j)\) act on \(B(S) = \ell_\infty(S)\), and the semigroup structure is encoded by \(q_0 = \delta\) and the Chapman–Kolmogorov equations.
  • Matrix norms. An operator given by a matrix \(\Theta\) with finite row-sum norm has operator norm exactly \(\|\Theta\|_{\mathrm{row}} = \sup_i \sum_j |\Theta(i, j)|\) (Lemma 88.1).
  • Norm continuity is a uniform condition. \(\|Q_t - I\|_{\mathrm{op}} = 2 \sup_i (1 - q_t(i, i))\), so the semigroup has a bounded generator if and only if \(\inf_i q_t(i, i) \to 1\) as \(t \downarrow 0\). For finite \(S\) this is automatic once each \(q_t(i, i) \to 1\); for infinite \(S\) it fails, e.g., whenever jump rates are unbounded (Remark 88.1).
  • Not every bounded operator is a matrix. When \(S\) is infinite, finitely supported functions are not dense in \(B(S)\), and there exist bounded (even positive, constant-preserving) operators that are not represented by any matrix (Example 88.1). Matrix operators are exactly the bounded-pointwise sequentially continuous ones (Remark 88.3).
  • Matrix operators are closed under norm limits. If \(T_n \to T\) in operator norm and each \(T_n\) is a matrix operator, then \(T\) is a matrix operator whose entries are the entrywise limits, and the matrices converge in row-sum norm (Proposition 88.1). The key step is Fatou’s lemma for the counting measure.
  • The generator is a matrix. Under norm continuity, \(a(i, j) = \frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0^+} q_t(i, j)\) exists for all \(i, j\), and \(A f(i) = \sum_j a(i, j) f(j)\) for all \(f \in B(S)\); this justifies the formal interchange of derivative and sum (Corollary 88.1).
  • Q-matrix properties. The generator matrix has non-negative off-diagonal entries, non-positive diagonal, zero row sums, and uniformly bounded diagonal, with \(\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} = 2 \sup_i |a(i, i)|\) (Corollary 88.2). Conversely, every such matrix generates a norm-continuous Markov semigroup \(Q_t = e^{tA}\) (Theorem 88.1).
