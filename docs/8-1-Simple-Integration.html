17  Lecture 8.1: Simple Integration

17.1 Introduction and Definitions

Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) be a measure space.

Definition 17.1 (Simple Function) A function \(f: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}\) is called a simple \(\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\)-measurable function if it takes on only finitely many distinct real values. That is, \(f(\Omega) = \{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n\} \subset \mathbb{R}\) for some \(n \in \mathbb{N}\).

Equivalently, \(f\) can be written in its canonical representation as: \[ f = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_j \mathbf{1}_{A_j} \tag{17.1}\] where \(A_j = \{w \in \Omega : f(w) = \alpha_j\} = f^{-1}(\{\alpha_j\}) \in \mathcal{F}\) are pairwise disjoint measurable sets partitioning \(\Omega\), and \(\mathbf{1}_{A_j}\) denotes the indicator function of the set \(A_j\).

We denote the space of all simple \(\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\)-measurable functions by: \[ \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}} := \{ f: \Omega \to \mathbb{R} \mid f \text{ is a simple } \mathcal{F}/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\text{-measurable function} \} \]

Definition 17.2 (Integral of a Simple Function) For any simple function \(f \in \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\) with representation \(f = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_j \mathbf{1}_{A_j}\) (where \(A_j \in \mathcal{F}\) and \(\alpha_j \in \mathbb{R}\)), the integral of \(f\) with respect to \(\mu\) is defined as: \[ \int f \, d\mu := \sum_{t \in \mathbb{R}} t \cdot \mu(\{f = t\}) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_j \mu(A_j) \tag{17.2}\] where \(\{f = t\} := \{\omega \in \Omega : f(\omega) = t\}\).

If \(\mu = \mathbb{P}\) is a probability measure on \((\Omega, \mathcal{F})\), the integral is often denoted as the expectation: \[ \mathbb{E}(f) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}(f) := \int f \, d\mathbb{P}= \sum_{t \in \mathbb{R}} t \cdot \mathbb{P}(\{f = t\}) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_j \mathbb{P}(A_j). \]

Example 17.1 Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})\) be a probability space and let \(A \in \mathcal{F}\). The expectation of the indicator function \(\mathbf{1}_A\) is given by: \[ \mathbb{E}(\mathbf{1}_A) = 0 \cdot \mathbb{P}(A^c) + 1 \cdot \mathbb{P}(A) = \mathbb{P}(A) \]

17.2 Algebraic Properties of the Integral

Proposition 17.1 (Properties of Simple Functions and Integration (Driver [5.27])) Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) be a measure space and let \(\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\) be the set of simple \(\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\)-measurable functions.

  1. \(\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\) is a real vector space.
  2. The mapping \(\int \cdot \, d\mu : \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}} \to \mathbb{R}\) is a positive linear functional. That is:
    • Linearity: For all \(f, g \in \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\) and \(\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{R}\), \(\alpha f + \beta g \in \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\) and: \[ \int (\alpha f + \beta g) \, d\mu = \alpha \int f \, d\mu + \beta \int g \, d\mu \tag{17.3}\]
    • Monotonicity (Positivity): If \(f, g \in \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\) and \(f \le g\) pointwise, then: \[ \int f \, d\mu \le \int g \, d\mu \tag{17.4}\] In particular, if \(f \ge 0\), then \(\int f \, d\mu \ge 0\).

Proof (Proof of Proposition 17.1). We prove each property systematically.

17.2.1 Step 1: Scalar Multiplication

Let \(f \in \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\) and \(\beta \in \mathbb{R}\). If \(f = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_j \mathbf{1}_{A_j}\), then \((\beta f)(\omega) = \beta f(\omega) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} (\beta \alpha_j) \mathbf{1}_{A_j}(\omega)\). Since \(\beta f\) assumes at most \(n\) distinct finite values, \(\beta f \in \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\).

By definition of the integral: \[ \int \beta f \, d\mu = \sum_{j=1}^{n} (\beta \alpha_j) \mu(A_j) = \beta \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_j \mu(A_j) = \beta \int f \, d\mu \]

17.2.2 Step 2: Closure Under Addition

Let \(f, g \in \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\). The range of \(f + g\) is contained in \(\{x + y : x \in f(\Omega), y \in g(\Omega)\}\). Since \(f(\Omega)\) and \(g(\Omega)\) are finite sets, the sum of their ranges is also finite.

Furthermore, for any \(t \in \mathbb{R}\): \[ \{f + g = t\} = \bigcup_{\substack{x, y \in \mathbb{R} \\ x + y = t}} \left( \{f = x\} \cap \{g = y\} \right) \] Because \(\{f = x\}\) and \(\{g = y\}\) are empty for all but finitely many pairs \((x, y)\), this is a finite union of measurable sets, hence \(\{f + g = t\} \in \mathcal{F}\). Thus, \(f + g \in \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\).

17.2.3 Step 3: Additivity of Integral

Using the partition property, we compute: \[ \begin{aligned} &\int (f + g) \, d\mu = \sum_{t \in \mathbb{R}} t \cdot \mu(\{f + g = t\}) \\ &= \sum_{t \in \mathbb{R}} \sum_{\substack{x, y \in \mathbb{R} \\ x + y = t}} t \cdot \mu(\{f = x\} \cap \{g = y\}) \\ &= \sum_{x, y \in \mathbb{R}} (x + y) \mu(\{f = x\} \cap \{g = y\}) \\ &= \sum_{x, y \in \mathbb{R}} x \, \mu(\{f = x\} \cap \{g = y\}) + \sum_{x, y \in \mathbb{R}} y \, \mu(\{f = x\} \cap \{g = y\}) \\ &= \sum_{x \in \mathbb{R}} x \sum_{y \in \mathbb{R}} \mu(\{f = x\} \cap \{g = y\}) + \sum_{y \in \mathbb{R}} y \sum_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \mu(\{f = x\} \cap \{g = y\}) \end{aligned} \]

By countable additivity over the disjoint partitions \(\bigcup_{y \in \mathbb{R}} \{g = y\} = \Omega\) and \(\bigcup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \{f = x\} = \Omega\): \[ \begin{align*} \sum_{y \in \mathbb{R}} \mu(\{f = x\} \cap \{g = y\}) &= \mu(\{f = x\}), \quad \text{and} \quad \\ \sum_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \mu(\{f = x\} \cap \{g = y\}) &= \mu(\{g = y\}) \end{align*} \]

Substituting these back gives: \[ \int (f + g) \, d\mu = \sum_{x \in \mathbb{R}} x \, \mu(\{f = x\}) + \sum_{y \in \mathbb{R}} y \, \mu(\{g = y\}) = \int f \, d\mu + \int g \, d\mu \]

Combining Steps 1, 2, and 3 establishes that \(\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\) is a real vector space and \(\int \cdot \, d\mu\) is a linear functional on \(\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\).

17.2.4 Step 4: Positivity and Monotonicity

If \(f \ge 0\), all non-zero values \(t\) in the image of \(f\) satisfy \(t > 0\). Thus: \[ \int f \, d\mu = \sum_{t \ge 0} t \cdot \mu(\{f = t\}) \ge 0 \]

If \(f \le g\), then \(g - f \ge 0\). By linearity and positivity: \[ \int g \, d\mu - \int f \, d\mu = \int (g - f) \, d\mu \ge \int 0 \, d\mu = 0 \implies \int f \, d\mu \le \int g \, d\mu \]

Corollary 17.1 (Triangle Inequality for Simple Integrals) For any \(f \in \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\): \[ \left| \int f \, d\mu \right| \le \int |f| \, d\mu \tag{17.5}\]

Proof (Proof of Corollary 17.1). Using the discrete definition of the integral and the classical triangle inequality on \(\mathbb{R}\): \[ \left| \int f \, d\mu \right| = \left| \sum_{t \in \mathbb{R}} t \cdot \mu(\{f = t\}) \right| \le \sum_{t \in \mathbb{R}} |t| \cdot \mu(\{f = t\}) = \int |f| \, d\mu \]

17.3 Applications

17.4 The Principle of Inclusion-Exclusion

Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) be a measure space and \(A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n \in \mathcal{F}\) with \(\mu(A_i) < \infty\) for all \(i\). Note that for any events \(A, B \in \mathcal{F}\), \(\mathbf{1}_{A \cap B} = \mathbf{1}_A \cdot \mathbf{1}_B\).

Theorem 17.1 (Inclusion-Exclusion Principle) Let \(A = \bigcup_{j=1}^{n} A_j\). Then: \[ \mu\left( \bigcup_{j=1}^{n} A_j \right) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} (-1)^{j+1} \sum_{1 \le k_1 < \dots < k_j \le n} \mu(A_{k_1} \cap \dots \cap A_{k_j}) \tag{17.6}\]

Proof (Proof of Theorem 17.1). Consider the complement \(A^c = \bigcap_{j=1}^{n} A_j^c\). In terms of indicator functions: \[ \mathbf{1}_{A^c} = \prod_{j=1}^{n} \mathbf{1}_{A_j^c} = \prod_{j=1}^{n} (1 - \mathbf{1}_{A_j}) \]

Since \(\mathbf{1}_{A^c} = 1 - \mathbf{1}_A\), we expand the algebraic product: \[ \begin{align*} 1 - \mathbf{1}_A &= \sum_{j=0}^{n} \sum_{1 \le k_1 < \dots < k_j \le n} (-1)^j \mathbf{1}_{A_{k_1}} \cdots \mathbf{1}_{A_{k_j}}\\ &= 1 + \sum_{j=1}^{n} (-1)^j \sum_{1 \le k_1 < \dots < k_j \le n} \mathbf{1}_{A_{k_1} \cap \dots \cap A_{k_j}} \end{align*} \]

Subtracting \(1\) and multiplying by \(-1\) gives the indicator identity: \[ \mathbf{1}_A = \sum_{j=1}^{n} (-1)^{j+1} \sum_{1 \le k_1 < \dots < k_j \le n} \mathbf{1}_{A_{k_1} \cap \dots \cap A_{k_j}} \]

Integrating both sides with respect to \(\mu\) and applying linearity yields Equation 17.6.

Example 17.2  

  • For \(n = 2\): \[ \mu(A_1 \cup A_2) = \mu(A_1) + \mu(A_2) - \mu(A_1 \cap A_2) \]
  • For \(n = 3\): \[ \begin{align*} \mu(A_1 \cup A_2 \cup A_3) &= \mu(A_1) + \mu(A_2) + \mu(A_3) - \mu(A_1 \cap A_2)\\ & - \mu(A_1 \cap A_3) - \mu(A_2 \cap A_3) + \mu(A_1 \cap A_2 \cap A_3) \end{align*} \]

17.5 The Derangement Problem (Matching Cards)

Example 17.3 Shuffle a deck of \(n\) cards.

  1. What is the probability that at least one card remains in its original position?
  2. What is the expected number of unmoved cards?

Model Formalization: Let \(\Omega = S_n\) be the symmetric group of all \(n!\) permutations of \(\{1, 2, \dots, n\}\), equipped with the power set \(\mathcal{F} = 2^\Omega\) and the uniform probability measure \(\mathbb{P}(A) = \frac{\# A}{n!}\).

Let \(A_i = \{\omega \in S_n : \omega(i) = i\}\) be the event that the \(i\)-th card is fixed. The event that at least one card remains unmoved is \(B = \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} A_i\).

17.5.1 Probability of At Least One Fixed Point

For any subset \(\{k_1, \dots, k_j\} \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}\) of distinct indices, the intersection \(A_{k_1} \cap \dots \cap A_{k_j} = \{\omega \in S_n : \omega(k_1) = k_1, \dots, \omega(k_j) = k_j\}\) consists of permutations fixing \(j\) specified elements. The remaining \(n-j\) elements can be permuted arbitrarily, so: \[ \#(A_{k_1} \cap \dots \cap A_{k_j}) = (n - j)! \implies \mathbb{P}(A_{k_1} \cap \dots \cap A_{k_j}) = \frac{(n - j)!}{n!} \]

By Inclusion-Exclusion (Theorem 17.1): \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb{P}(B) &= \sum_{j=1}^{n} (-1)^{j+1} \sum_{1 \le k_1 < \dots < k_j \le n} \mathbb{P}(A_{k_1} \cap \dots \cap A_{k_j}) \\ &= \sum_{j=1}^{n} (-1)^{j+1} \binom{n}{j} \frac{(n - j)!}{n!} \\ &= \sum_{j=1}^{n} (-1)^{j+1} \frac{n!}{j!(n-j)!} \frac{(n - j)!}{n!} = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j!} = 1 - \sum_{j=0}^{n} \frac{(-1)^j}{j!} \end{aligned} \]

As \(n \to \infty\): \[ \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{P}(B) = 1 - e^{-1} \approx 63.2\% \]

17.5.2 Expected Number of Unmoved Cards

Let \(X\) be the random variable counting the total number of fixed cards: \[ X = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{1}_{A_i} \]

Using linearity of expectation: \[ \mathbb{E}(X) = \mathbb{E}\left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{1}_{A_i} \right) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{E}(\mathbf{1}_{A_i}) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{P}(A_i) \]

Since \(\mathbb{P}(A_i) = \frac{(n - 1)!}{n!} = \frac{1}{n}\) for each \(i \in \{1, \dots, n\}\): \[ \mathbb{E}(X) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{n} = 1 \]
