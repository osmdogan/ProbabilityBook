19  Lecture 9.1: Fatou and Borel-Cantelli

19.1 Null Sets and Almost Everywhere Properties

Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) be a measure space.

Definition 19.1 (Null Sets and Almost Everywhere Convergence) A set \(N \in \mathcal{F}\) is called a null set (or measure zero set) if \(\mu(N) = 0\).

A statement \(P(\omega)\) depending on \(\omega \in \Omega\) is said to hold almost everywhere (a.e.) with respect to \(\mu\) (or almost surely (a.s.) if \(\mu = \mathbb{P}\) is a probability measure) if there exists a null set \(N \in \mathcal{F}\) such that: \[ \{\omega \in \Omega \mid P(\omega) \text{ is false}\} \subseteq N \]

Lemma 19.1 (Integration over Null Sets) Let \(\varphi \in \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}^+\) be a non-negative simple function and let \(E \in \mathcal{F}\) with \(\mu(E) = 0\). Then: \[ \int_E \varphi \, d\mu := \int_{\Omega} \varphi \mathbf{1}_E \, d\mu = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \int_{\Omega} \varphi \, d\mu = \int_{E^c} \varphi \, d\mu \tag{19.1}\]

Proof (Proof of Lemma 19.1). Let \(\varphi = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_j \mathbf{1}_{A_j}\) with \(\alpha_j \ge 0\) and \(A_j \in \mathcal{F}\). Then \(\varphi \mathbf{1}_E = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_j \mathbf{1}_{A_j \cap E}\).

By definition of simple integrals: \[ \int_E \varphi \, d\mu = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_j \mu(A_j \cap E) \]

Since \(A_j \cap E \subseteq E\) and \(\mu(E) = 0\), monotonicity of measure gives \(\mu(A_j \cap E) = 0\) for all \(j \in \{1, \dots, n\}\). Hence \(\int_E \varphi \, d\mu = 0\).

Using the disjoint identity \(\mathbf{1}_{\Omega} = \mathbf{1}_E + \mathbf{1}_{E^c}\) and additivity: \[ \int_{\Omega} \varphi \, d\mu = \int_{\Omega} \varphi \mathbf{1}_E \, d\mu + \int_{\Omega} \varphi \mathbf{1}_{E^c} \, d\mu = 0 + \int_{E^c} \varphi \, d\mu = \int_{E^c} \varphi \, d\mu \]

The following proposition summarizes some important properties of \(L^+\) integrals that hold almost everywhere.

Proposition 19.1 (Almost Everywhere Properties of \(L^+\) Integrals) Let \(f, g \in L^+(\Omega, \mathcal{F})\).

  1. Monotonicity: If \(f \le g\) a.e., then \(\int f \, d\mu \le \int g \, d\mu\).
  2. Equivalence: If \(f = g\) a.e., then \(\int f \, d\mu = \int g \, d\mu\).
  3. Vanishing Integral: \(\int f \, d\mu = 0 \iff f = 0\) a.e.

Proof (Proof of Proposition 19.1). We prove each assertion step-by-step.

19.1.1 Proof of Part 1

Let \(E = \{\omega \in \Omega \mid f(\omega) > g(\omega)\} \in \mathcal{F}\). By assumption, \(\mu(E) = 0\). For any simple function \(\varphi \in \mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\) with \(0 \le \varphi \le f\), we have \(\mathbf{1}_{E^c} \varphi \le \mathbf{1}_{E^c} f \le \mathbf{1}_{E^c} g \le g\).

By Lemma 19.1 and monotonicity on simple functions: \[ \int \varphi \, d\mu = \int_{E^c} \varphi \, d\mu \le \int g \, d\mu \] Taking the supremum over all simple \(0 \le \varphi \le f\) gives \(\int f \, d\mu \le \int g \, d\mu\).

19.1.2 Proof of Part 2

If \(f = g\) a.e., then \(f \le g\) a.e. and \(g \le f\) a.e.. Applying Part 1 twice yields \(\int f \, d\mu \le \int g \, d\mu\) and \(\int g \, d\mu \le \int f \, d\mu\), so \(\int f \, d\mu = \int g \, d\mu\).

19.1.3 Proof of Part 3

\((\Leftarrow)\) If \(f = 0\) a.e., then by Part 2, \(\int f \, d\mu = \int 0 \, d\mu = 0\).

\((\Rightarrow)\) Assume \(\int f \, d\mu = 0\). For each \(n \in \mathbb{N}\), the pointwise bound \(\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}_{\{f \ge 1/n\}} \le f\) together with monotonicity of integration gives: \[ \frac{1}{n}\,\mu\!\left(\left\{f \ge \frac{1}{n}\right\}\right) = \int \frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}_{\{f \ge 1/n\}} \, d\mu \le \int f \, d\mu = 0 \implies \mu\!\left(\left\{f \ge \frac{1}{n}\right\}\right) = 0 \]

Notice that \(\{f > 0\} = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} \left\{f \ge \frac{1}{n}\right\}\). By countable subadditivity of measure: \[ \mu(\{f > 0\}) \le \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mu\left(\left\{f \ge \frac{1}{n}\right\}\right) = 0 \implies f = 0 \text{ a.e.} \]

The following corollary is a version of the Monotone Convergence Theorem that holds almost everywhere.

Corollary 19.1 (Monotone Convergence Theorem (a.e. Version)) If \((f_n)_{n=1}^\infty \subset L^+\) satisfies \(f_n \uparrow f\) a.e. as \(n \to \infty\), then: \[ \int f_n \, d\mu \uparrow \int f \, d\mu \tag{19.2}\]

Proof (Proof of Corollary 19.1). Let \(E = \{\omega \in \Omega \mid f_n(\omega) \uparrow f(\omega) \text{ fails}\} \in \mathcal{F}\), so \(\mu(E) = 0\). Then \(f_n \mathbf{1}_{E^c} \uparrow f \mathbf{1}_{E^c}\) pointwise everywhere on \(\Omega\).

Applying the pointwise Monotone Convergence Theorem and Lemma 19.1: \[ \int f_n \, d\mu = \int f_n \mathbf{1}_{E^c} \, d\mu \uparrow \int f \mathbf{1}_{E^c} \, d\mu = \int f \, d\mu \]

19.2 Markov’s Inequality & Fatou’s Lemma

  • Markov’s inequality shows that the measure of the set where a non-negative function exceeds a threshold is bounded by the integral of the function.

  • Fatou’s lemma provides a lower bound for the integral of the limit inferior of a sequence of non-negative functions.

Theorem 19.1 (Markov’s Inequality) Let \(f \in L^+\), \(\epsilon > 0\), and \(p > 0\). Then: \[ \mu(\{f \ge \epsilon\}) \le \frac{1}{\epsilon^p} \int f^p \, d\mu. \tag{19.3}\]

Proof (Proof of Theorem 19.1). Observe the indicator pointwise inequality: \[ \mathbf{1}_{\{f \ge \epsilon\}} \le \left( \frac{f}{\epsilon} \right)^p \mathbf{1}_{\{f \ge \epsilon\}} \le \left( \frac{f}{\epsilon} \right)^p. \]

Integrating both sides with respect to \(\mu\) and using monotonicity: \[ \mu(\{f \ge \epsilon\}) = \int \mathbf{1}_{\{f \ge \epsilon\}} \, d\mu \le \int \left( \frac{f}{\epsilon} \right)^p \, d\mu = \frac{1}{\epsilon^p} \int f^p \, d\mu. \]

Theorem 19.2 (Fatou’s Lemma) Let \((f_n)_{n=1}^\infty \subset L^+\) be any sequence of non-negative measurable functions. Then: \[ \int \liminf_{n \to \infty} f_n \, d\mu \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int f_n \, d\mu. \tag{19.4}\]

Proof (Proof of Theorem 19.2). For each \(k \in \mathbb{N}\), define the pointwise infimum sequence \(g_k := \inf_{n \ge k} f_n \in L^+\).

  1. Monotonicity: As \(k\) increases, the infimum is taken over smaller sets, so \(g_k \le g_{k+1}\) for all \(k\).
  2. Pointwise Limit: By definition of limit inferior, \(g_k \uparrow \liminf_{n \to \infty} f_n\) pointwise as \(k \to \infty\).

Applying the Monotone Convergence Theorem to the non-decreasing sequence \((g_k)_{k=1}^\infty\): \[ \int \liminf_{n \to \infty} f_n \, d\mu = \int \lim_{k \to \infty} g_k \, d\mu = \lim_{k \to \infty} \int g_k \, d\mu \tag{19.5}\]

For any fixed \(n \ge k\), \(g_k \le f_n\) pointwise. Monotonicity gives \(\int g_k \, d\mu \le \int f_n \, d\mu\) for all \(n \ge k\). Taking the infimum over \(n \ge k\) on the right side yields: \[ \int g_k \, d\mu \le \inf_{n \ge k} \int f_n \, d\mu. \tag{19.6}\]

Taking the limit as \(k \to \infty\) on both sides of Equation 19.6 and combining with Equation 19.5: \[ \int \liminf_{n \to \infty} f_n \, d\mu = \lim_{k \to \infty} \int g_k \, d\mu \le \lim_{k \to \infty} \left( \inf_{n \ge k} \int f_n \, d\mu \right) = \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int f_n \, d\mu. \]

19.3 Applications: Almost Disjoint Additivity & First Borel-Cantelli Lemma

19.4 Almost Disjoint Additivity

  • The following proposition shows that the additivity property of measures extends to sequences of events that are disjoint almost everywhere.

Proposition 19.2 Let \((A_n)_{n=1}^\infty \subset \mathcal{F}\) be a sequence of events that are disjoint a.e., meaning \(\mu(A_n \cap A_m) = 0\) for all \(n \ne m\). Then: \[ \mu\left( \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n \right) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mu(A_n) \tag{19.7}\]

Proof (Proof of Proposition 19.2). Step 1: Construct the null set. Define \(N = \bigcup_{n \ne m} (A_n \cap A_m) \in \mathcal{F}\). By countable subadditivity: \[ \mu(N) \le \sum_{n \ne m} \mu(A_n \cap A_m) = 0, \] so \(\mu(N) = 0\).

Step 2: Establish the pointwise identity on \(N^c\). The sets \((A_n \cap N^c)_{n \ge 1}\) are pairwise disjoint: if \(\omega \in (A_n \cap N^c) \cap (A_m \cap N^c)\) for some \(n \ne m\), then \(\omega \in A_n \cap A_m \subseteq N\), contradicting \(\omega \in N^c\). Therefore each \(\omega \in N^c\) belongs to at most one \(A_n\), so: \[ \mathbf{1}_{\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n}(\omega) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mathbf{1}_{A_n}(\omega) \quad \text{for all } \omega \in N^c. \tag{19.8}\]

Step 3: Reduce the indicator integral to \(N^c\). Since \(\mathbf{1}_{\bigcup A_n}\) is a simple function and \(\mu(N) = 0\), Lemma 19.1 gives: \[ \int_\Omega \mathbf{1}_{\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n} \, d\mu = \int_{N^c} \mathbf{1}_{\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n} \, d\mu. \tag{19.9}\]

Step 4: Reduce the series integral to \(N^c\). For each \(n\), monotonicity of measure gives \(\mu(A_n \cap N) \le \mu(N) = 0\). Applying Corollary 18.2: \[ \int_N \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mathbf{1}_{A_n} \, d\mu = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu(A_n \cap N) = 0, \] so \(\int_\Omega \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mathbf{1}_{A_n} \, d\mu = \int_{N^c} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mathbf{1}_{A_n} \, d\mu\).

Step 5: Conclude. Combining Equation 19.9, the pointwise identity Equation 19.8 on \(N^c\), Corollary 18.2, and \(\mu(A_n \cap N) = 0\): \[ \begin{align*} \mu\!\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n\right) &= \int_{N^c} \mathbf{1}_{\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n} \, d\mu = \int_{N^c} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mathbf{1}_{A_n} \, d\mu \\ &= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \int_{N^c} \mathbf{1}_{A_n} \, d\mu = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mu(A_n \cap N^c)\\ &= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mu(A_n). \end{align*} \]

19.5 The First Borel-Cantelli Lemma

Definition 19.2 (Infinitely Often (\(i.o.\)) / Limit Superior of Events) For a sequence of events \((A_n)_{n=1}^\infty \subset \mathcal{F}\), we define \(\{A_n \text{ i.o.}\}\) (read “\(A_n\) infinitely often”) as: \[ \begin{align*} \{A_n \text{ i.o.}\} &:= \{\omega \in \Omega \mid \omega \in A_n \text{ for infinitely many } n\} \\ &= \bigcap_{N=1}^{\infty} \bigcup_{n=N}^{\infty} A_n = \limsup_{n \to \infty} A_n \end{align*} \tag{19.10}\]

Theorem 19.3 (First Borel-Cantelli Lemma) Let \((A_n)_{n=1}^\infty \subset \mathcal{F}\) be a sequence of events. If \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mu(A_n) < \infty\), then: \[ \mu(\{A_n \text{ i.o.}\}) = 0 \tag{19.11}\]

Proof (Proof of Theorem 19.3). Step 1: Reformulate using \(Y\). Define \(Y := \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mathbf{1}_{A_n} \in L^+\). Since each \(\mathbf{1}_{A_n}(\omega) \in \{0,1\}\), the value \(Y(\omega)\) is exactly the number of sets \(A_n\) that contain \(\omega\). Therefore: \[ \{A_n \text{ i.o.}\} = \{\omega \in \Omega \mid \omega \in A_n \text{ for infinitely many } n\} = \{Y = \infty\}. \]

Step 2: Compute \(\int Y \, d\mu\). By Corollary 18.2: \[ \int_{\Omega} Y \, d\mu = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \int_{\Omega} \mathbf{1}_{A_n} \, d\mu = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mu(A_n) < \infty. \tag{19.12}\]

Step 3: Bound \(\mu(\{A_n \text{ i.o.}\})\) via Markov’s inequality. For each \(k \in \mathbb{N}\), the inclusion \(\{Y = \infty\} \subseteq \{Y \ge k\}\) and monotonicity of measure give: \[ \mu(\{A_n \text{ i.o.}\}) = \mu(\{Y = \infty\}) \le \mu(\{Y \ge k\}). \] Applying Theorem 19.1 with \(\epsilon = k\) and \(p = 1\): \[ \mu(\{Y \ge k\}) \le \frac{1}{k} \int_{\Omega} Y \, d\mu = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mu(A_n). \]

Step 4: Conclude. Combining Steps 3 and Equation 19.12: \[ 0 \le \mu(\{A_n \text{ i.o.}\}) \le \frac{1}{k} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mu(A_n) \quad \text{for all } k \in \mathbb{N}. \] Since \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mu(A_n) < \infty\) is a fixed finite constant, taking \(k \to \infty\) gives \(\mu(\{A_n \text{ i.o.}\}) = 0\).

Example 19.1 Consider an infinite sequence of independent coin tosses where \(X_n = 1\) if the \(n\)-th toss is heads and \(X_n = 0\) otherwise, with \(\mathbb{P}(X_n = 1) = p_n\).

  • Case \(p_n = \frac{1}{n^2}\): Since \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} p_n = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6} < \infty\), the First Borel-Cantelli Lemma implies \(\mathbb{P}(X_n = 1 \text{ i.o.}) = 0\). Thus, with probability 1, only finitely many heads occur (eventually, heads never appear again).
  • Case \(p_n = \frac{1}{n}\): Here \(p_n \to 0\), but \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} p_n = \infty\). The First Borel-Cantelli Lemma does not apply; determining \(\mathbb{P}(X_n = 1 \text{ i.o.})\) requires the Second Borel-Cantelli Lemma (for independent events).
