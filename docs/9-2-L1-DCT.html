20  Lecture 9.2: \(L^1\) and the Dominated Convergence Theorem

20.1 Definition of the Lebesgue Integral for Real-Valued Functions

Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) be a measure space. For a measurable function \(f: \Omega \to \overline{\mathbb{R}} = [-\infty, \infty]\), we define its positive and negative parts by: \[ f_{+}(\omega) := \max\{f(\omega), 0\} = f(\omega) \mathbf{1}_{\{f \ge 0\}}(\omega), \tag{20.1}\] \[ f_{-}(\omega) := \max\{-f(\omega), 0\} = -f(\omega) \mathbf{1}_{\{f \le 0\}}(\omega). \tag{20.2}\]

Notice that

  • \(f = f_{+} - f_{-}\),
  • \(|f| = f_{+} + f_{-}\), and
  • \(f_{+}, f_{-} \in L^+(\Omega, \mathcal{F})\).

Definition 20.1 (Integrability and Space \(\mathcal{L}^1\)) A measurable function \(f: \Omega \to \overline{\mathbb{R}}\) is called \(\mu\)-integrable if: \[ \int_{\Omega} f_{+} \, d\mu < \infty \quad \text{and} \quad \int_{\Omega} f_{-} \, d\mu < \infty. \tag{20.3}\]

The space of all integrable functions is denoted by \(\mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\). For any \(f \in \mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\), its Lebesgue integral is defined as: \[ \int_{\Omega} f \, d\mu := \int_{\Omega} f_{+} \, d\mu - \int_{\Omega} f_{-} \, d\mu. \tag{20.4}\]

  • Since \(|f| = f_{+} + f_{-}\), we can equivalently define \(\mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) as \[ \mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu) := \{f: \Omega \to \overline{\mathbb{R}} \text{ measurable s.t. } \int_{\Omega} |f| \, d\mu < \infty\}. \]

Remark 20.1. Note that \[ f \in \mathcal{L}^1 \implies \int_{\Omega} |f| \, d\mu = \int_{\Omega} f_{+} \, d\mu + \int_{\Omega} f_{-} \, d\mu < \infty. \] Thus, it follows that \(|f| < \infty\) almost everywhere. Thus, by modifying \(f\) on a null set, we can assume without loss of generality that \(f\) is strictly real-valued (\(f: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}\)).

20.2 Properties of \(\mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\)

In the following proposition, we summarize some important properties of the space \(\mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) and the Lebesgue integral.

Proposition 20.1 (Linearity and Monotonicity of \(\mathcal{L}^1\))  

  1. \(\mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) is a real vector space.
  2. The integral operator \(\int \cdot \, d\mu : \mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu) \to \mathbb{R}\) is a linear functional: \[ \int (a f + b g) \, d\mu = a \int f \, d\mu + b \int g \, d\mu \quad \forall a, b \in \mathbb{R}, \, f, g \in \mathcal{L}^1. \tag{20.5}\]
  3. Monotonicity: If \(f, g \in \mathcal{L}^1\) and \(f \le g\) a.e., then: \[ \int f \, d\mu \le \int g \, d\mu \tag{20.6}\]
  4. Triangle Inequality: For any \(f \in \mathcal{L}^1\): \[ \left| \int f \, d\mu \right| \le \int |f| \, d\mu. \tag{20.7}\]

Proof (Proof of Proposition 20.1). We prove each property systematically.

20.2.1 Proof of Step 1 & Vector Space Structure

Let \(f, g \in \mathcal{L}^1\) and \(a, b \in \mathbb{R}\). By the point-wise triangle inequality: \[ |a f + b g| \le |a| |f| + |b| |g|. \] Integrating both sides and using \(L^+\) linearity (Corollary 18.1 and Proposition 18.1): \[ \int |a f + b g| \, d\mu \le |a| \int |f| \, d\mu + |b| \int |g| \, d\mu < \infty. \] Hence, \(a f + b g \in \mathcal{L}^1\), proving \(\mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) is a vector space.

20.2.2 Proof of Step 2 (Linearity)

Scalar Multiplication: If \(a \ge 0\), \((af)_{+} = a f_{+}\) and \((af)_{-} = a f_{-}\). By \(L^+\) homogeneity: \[ \int a f \, d\mu = \int a f_{+} \, d\mu - \int a f_{-} \, d\mu = a \int f_{+} \, d\mu - a \int f_{-} \, d\mu = a \int f \, d\mu. \] If \(a < 0\), \((af)_{+} = -a f_{-}\) and \((af)_{-} = -a f_{+}\). Thus: \[ \begin{align*} \int a f \, d\mu &= \int (-a) f_{-} \, d\mu - \int (-a) f_{+} \, d\mu \\ &= -a \int f_{-} \, d\mu - (-a) \int f_{+} \, d\mu\\ & = a \left( \int f_{+} \, d\mu - \int f_{-} \, d\mu \right) \\ &= a \int f \, d\mu. \end{align*} \]

Addition: For \(f, g \in \mathcal{L}^1\), \(f + g = (f_{+} + g_{+}) - (f_{-} + g_{-})\), which implies: \[ \begin{align*} &(f + g)_{+} - (f + g)_{-} = (f_{+} + g_{+}) - (f_{-} + g_{-})\\ & \implies (f + g)_{+} + f_{-} + g_{-} = (f + g)_{-} + f_{+} + g_{+} \end{align*} \]

Since all terms belong to \(L^+\) with finite integrals, we integrate both sides using \(L^+\) additivity: \[ \int (f + g)_{+} \, d\mu + \int f_{-} \, d\mu + \int g_{-} \, d\mu = \int (f + g)_{-} \, d\mu + \int f_{+} \, d\mu + \int g_{+} \, d\mu \]

Rearranging the finite real terms gives: \[ \begin{align*} \int (f + g)_{+} \, d\mu - \int (f + g)_{-} \, d\mu &= \left( \int f_{+} \, d\mu - \int f_{-} \, d\mu \right) \\ &+ \left( \int g_{+} \, d\mu - \int g_{-} \, d\mu \right) \end{align*} \] Thus, \(\int (f + g) \, d\mu = \int f \, d\mu + \int g \, d\mu\).

20.2.3 Proof of Step 3 (Monotonicity)

Assume \(f \le g\) a.e. Then \(f_{+} - f_{-} \le g_{+} - g_{-}\) a.e., which rearranges to \(f_{+} + g_{-} \le g_{+} + f_{-}\) a.e.

By monotonicity of \(L^+\) integrals (Proposition 19.1 Part 1) applied to \(f_+ + g_- \le g_+ + f_-\), and additivity of \(L^+\) integrals (Corollary 18.1): \[ \begin{align*} &\int f_{+} \, d\mu + \int g_{-} \, d\mu \le \int g_{+} \, d\mu + \int f_{-} \, d\mu \\ &\implies \int f_{+} \, d\mu - \int f_{-} \, d\mu \le \int g_{+} \, d\mu - \int g_{-} \, d\mu. \end{align*} \] Therefore, \(\int f \, d\mu \le \int g \, d\mu\).

20.2.4 Proof of Step 4 (Triangle Inequality)

Using definition Equation 20.4 and standard triangle inequality on \(\mathbb{R}\): \[ \begin{align*} \left| \int f \, d\mu \right| &= \left| \int f_{+} \, d\mu - \int f_{-} \, d\mu \right| \\ &\le \int f_{+} \, d\mu + \int f_{-} \, d\mu \\ &= \int (f_{+} + f_{-}) \, d\mu \\ &= \int |f| \, d\mu \end{align*} \]

20.3 Semi-Norms, Equivalence Classes, and \(L^p\) Spaces

Definition 20.2 (\(\mathcal{L}^1\) Semi-Norm) The functional \(\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}^1} : \mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu) \to [0, \infty)\) defined by: \[ \|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^1} := \int_{\Omega} |f| \, d\mu. \tag{20.8}\] is a semi-norm on \(\mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\). It satisfies:

  1. Absolute Homogeneity: \(\|\alpha f\|_{\mathcal{L}^1} = |\alpha| \|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^1}\) for all \(\alpha \in \mathbb{R}\).
  2. Subadditivity (Triangle Inequality): \(\|f + g\|_{\mathcal{L}^1} \le \|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^1} + \|g\|_{\mathcal{L}^1}\).

Remark 20.2. Failure of Definiteness: By Proposition 19.1 Part 3, \(\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^1} = 0 \iff f = 0\) a.e. Thus, \(\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^1} = 0\) does not imply \(f(\omega) = 0\) everywhere on \(\Omega\).

Definition 20.3 (\(L^1\) Quotient Space) We define an equivalence relation \(\sim_\mu\) on \(\mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) by: \[ f \sim_\mu g \iff f = g \text{ a.e. } [\mu]. \tag{20.9}\]

The Banach space \(L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) is defined as the set of equivalence classes: \[ L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu) := \mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu) / \sim_\mu. \tag{20.10}\]

Some remarks on \(L^1\) spaces:

  • For any equivalence class \([f] \in L^1\), any function \(f_1 \in [f]\) is called a version of \([f]\). The integral \(\int [f] \, d\mu := \int f_1 \, d\mu\) is well-defined independent of the choice of version.
  • On \(L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\), \(\|[f]\|_{L^1} := \int |f| \, d\mu\) is a genuine norm, and \(d_\mu([f], [g]) := \|[f] - [g]\|_{L^1}\) is a genuine metric.
  • Going forward, we will often abuse notation and write \(f \in L^1\) to mean \([f] \in L^1\).

The next definition generalizes the \(L^1\) space to \(L^p\) spaces for \(1 \le p < \infty\).

Definition 20.4 (General \(L^p\) Spaces (\(1 \le p < \infty\))) For \(1 \le p < \infty\), \(L^p(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) is defined as the set of equivalence classes of measurable functions \(f: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}\) such that \(\int |f|^p \, d\mu < \infty\). The norm is defined by: \[ \|f\|_{L^p} := \left( \int_{\Omega} |f|^p \, d\mu \right)^{1/p}. \tag{20.11}\]

The next result shows that \(L^p\) spaces are complete normed vector spaces and that the \(L^p\) norm satisfies Minkowski’s inequality.

Theorem 20.1 (Completeness of \(L^p\) (Driver [17.27])) For \(1 \le p < \infty\), the space \((L^p(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu), \|\cdot\|_{L^p})\) is a complete normed vector space (a Banach space). The norm satisfies Minkowski’s inequality: \[ \|f + g\|_{L^p} \le \|f\|_{L^p} + \|g\|_{L^p} \quad \forall f, g \in L^p. \tag{20.12}\]

20.4 The Dominated Convergence Theorem (DCT)

For \(L^{+}\), we have two integral convergence theorems: Fatou’s Lemma (Theorem 19.2) and the Monotone Convergence Theorem (Theorem 18.1). For \(L^1\), we have a single integral convergence theorem, the Dominated Convergence Theorem (DCT).

Theorem 20.2 (Dominated Convergence Theorem) Let \((f_n)_{n=1}^\infty\) be a sequence of measurable functions, and let \(g_n, g \in \mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) be integrable dominating functions such that:

  1. \(f_n \to f\) a.e. as \(n \to \infty\).
  2. \(g_n \to g\) a.e. as \(n \to \infty\) and \(g_n \ge 0\) a.e.
  3. \(|f_n| \le g_n\) a.e. for all \(n \in \mathbb{N}\).
  4. \(\int_{\Omega} g_n \, d\mu \to \int_{\Omega} g \, d\mu < \infty\) as \(n \to \infty\).

Then \(f \in \mathcal{L}^1(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) and: \[ \lim_{n \to \infty} \int_{\Omega} f_n \, d\mu = \int_{\Omega} f \, d\mu. \tag{20.13}\] In fact, \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \int_{\Omega} |f_n - f| \, d\mu = 0\).

Proof (Proof of Theorem 20.2). First, since \(|f_n| \le g_n\) a.e. and \(f_n \to f\), \(g_n \to g\) a.e., taking \(n \to \infty\) yields \(|f| \le g\) a.e. Since \(g \in \mathcal{L}^1\), \(\int |f| \, d\mu \le \int g \, d\mu < \infty\), so \(f \in \mathcal{L}^1\).

Since \(|f_n| \le g_n\) a.e. and \(g_n \ge 0\) a.e., we have \(g_n + f_n \ge g_n - |f_n| \ge 0\) a.e. and \(g_n - f_n \ge g_n - |f_n| \ge 0\) a.e. Thus \((g_n + f_n)_{n=1}^\infty \subset L^+\) and \((g_n - f_n)_{n=1}^\infty \subset L^+\).

20.4.1 Step 1: Lower Bound via Fatou’s Lemma

Since \(g_n \to g\) and \(f_n \to f\) a.e. (hypotheses 1 and 2), \(g_n + f_n \to g + f\) a.e., so \(\liminf_{n \to \infty}(g_n + f_n) = g + f\) a.e. Applying Fatou’s Lemma (Theorem 19.2) to \(g_n + f_n \ge 0\): \[ \int (g + f) \, d\mu = \int \liminf_{n \to \infty} (g_n + f_n) \, d\mu \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int (g_n + f_n) \, d\mu. \]

Using linearity of the integral and \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \int g_n \, d\mu = \int g \, d\mu\): \[ \int g \, d\mu + \int f \, d\mu \le \int g \, d\mu + \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int f_n \, d\mu. \]

Subtracting the finite quantity \(\int g \, d\mu\) yields: \[ \int f \, d\mu \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int f_n \, d\mu. \tag{20.14}\]

20.4.2 Step 2: Upper Bound via Fatou’s Lemma

Since \(g_n \to g\) and \(f_n \to f\) a.e., \(g_n - f_n \to g - f\) a.e., so \(\liminf_{n \to \infty}(g_n - f_n) = g - f\) a.e. Applying Fatou’s Lemma to \(g_n - f_n \ge 0\): \[ \int (g - f) \, d\mu = \int \liminf_{n \to \infty} (g_n - f_n) \, d\mu \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int (g_n - f_n) \, d\mu. \]

Since \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \int g_n \, d\mu = \int g \, d\mu\) exists (hypothesis 4), \(\liminf_n(a_n + b_n) = \lim_n a_n + \liminf_n b_n\) gives: \[ \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int (g_n - f_n) \, d\mu = \int g \, d\mu + \liminf_{n \to \infty} \left( -\int f_n \, d\mu \right). \]

Recall that \(\liminf_{n \to \infty} (-a_n) = -\limsup_{n \to \infty} a_n\). Thus: \[ \int g \, d\mu - \int f \, d\mu \le \int g \, d\mu - \limsup_{n \to \infty} \int f_n \, d\mu. \]

Subtracting \(\int g \, d\mu\) and multiplying by \(-1\) reverses the inequality: \[ \limsup_{n \to \infty} \int f_n \, d\mu \le \int f \, d\mu. \tag{20.15}\]

Combining Equation A.17 and Equation A.18: \[ \limsup_{n \to \infty} \int f_n \, d\mu \le \int f \, d\mu \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int f_n \, d\mu. \]

Since \(\liminf \int f_n \le \limsup \int f_n\) always holds, all terms are equal, proving \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \int f_n \, d\mu = \int f \, d\mu\).

20.4.3 Step 3: Convergence in \(L^1\) norm

It remains to prove \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \int |f_n - f| \, d\mu = 0\). Since \(|f_n - f| \le |f_n| + |f| \le g_n + g\) a.e., the sequence \(h_n := g_n + g - |f_n - f| \ge 0\) a.e.

Since \(g_n \to g\) a.e. and \(|f_n - f| \to 0\) a.e. (as \(f_n \to f\) a.e.), we have \(h_n \to 2g\) a.e., so \(\liminf_{n \to \infty} h_n = 2g\) a.e. Applying Fatou’s Lemma to \((h_n)\): \[ \int 2g \, d\mu = \int \liminf_{n \to \infty} h_n \, d\mu \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int h_n \, d\mu. \]

Since \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \int g_n \, d\mu = \int g \, d\mu\) (hypothesis 4), the same \(\liminf\) splitting gives: \[ \begin{align*} \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int h_n \, d\mu &= \lim_{n \to \infty} \int g_n \, d\mu + \int g \, d\mu + \liminf_{n \to \infty}\left(-\int |f_n - f| \, d\mu\right)\\ & = 2\int g \, d\mu - \limsup_{n \to \infty} \int |f_n - f| \, d\mu. \end{align*} \]

Therefore \(2\int g \, d\mu \le 2\int g \, d\mu - \limsup_{n \to \infty} \int |f_n - f| \, d\mu\), which gives \(\limsup_{n \to \infty} \int |f_n - f| \, d\mu \le 0\). Since \(\int |f_n - f| \, d\mu \ge 0\), we conclude \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \int |f_n - f| \, d\mu = 0\).

Corollary 20.1 (Bounded Convergence Theorem) Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)\) be a finite measure space (\(\mu(\Omega) < \infty\)). Suppose \((f_n)_{n=1}^\infty\) is a sequence of measurable functions such that \(f_n \to f\) a.e., and there exists a constant \(M < \infty\) with \(|f_n(\omega)| \le M\) a.e. for all \(n\).

Then \(f \in \mathcal{L}^1\) and \(\int f_n \, d\mu \to \int f \, d\mu\).

Proof (Proof of Corollary 20.1). Set \(g_n = g = M \mathbf{1}_{\Omega}\). Since \(\int g \, d\mu = M \mu(\Omega) < \infty\), \(g\) is an integrable dominating function. The result follows directly from the Dominated Convergence Theorem (Theorem 20.2).
